\noindent{\fontsize{32}{37}\selectfont\bfseries\color{ink}The Work a\par\noindent Verifier Needs\par}
\vspace{0.25in}
\noindent{\Large Transported summaries, certified low-precision heads\par\noindent and the serving stack of a hybrid model}\par
\vspace{0.22in}
\noindent{\large Samuel Mausberg}\par
\noindent{\color{muted}30 September 2026}\par
\vspace{0.18in}
\begin{abstract}
\noindent A speculative decoder asks its target model for a full vocabulary projection at every verified position, although a greedy verifier needs only a winner and a sampled verifier often needs only the truth of one inequality. We ask what work a verifier actually needs and test two sources of evidence that could let it do less: transporting a drafter's tile summaries through the shared output head to the target state, as earlier revisions of this manuscript proposed, and evaluating the head at low precision with a rigorous per-row envelope, re-scoring only the rows that can still matter. The setting is Qwen3.5-4B in SGLang on one GH200, where the tied $248{,}320\times2{,}560$ head is 15\% of the weight bytes of a plain decode step and 84\% of those of a multi-token-prediction draft step (derived from the checkpoint).

We state the exactness contract a certified head can meet in a real engine: the token the stock head kernel returns at the same batch shape, certified only when a gap condition covers that kernel's accumulation error and BF16 rounding, with the stock kernel as the fallback. We prove a floating-point envelope for the low-precision pass that holds for Hopper tensor-core accumulation under a published model, or needs no accumulation model for an integer pass; exact greedy selection and exact Gumbel-max sampling under a counter-keyed noise field from one refinement procedure; bounds on the rows that must be re-scored; and conditions under which exact sampling can finish before the scores are known. From the weights alone, transport's envelope is narrower than an int8 envelope only if draft and target head inputs agree to within about 0.85\% in relative $\ell_2$ distance (median row, Euclidean envelopes, 64-row tiles); this compares envelope widths, not certification rates or runtime. The greedy certified head is prior art, and our claim is narrower (Section~\ref{sec:prior-novelty}).

On 6,005 plain-decode head inputs captured from the engine, an int8 head certifies the real-arithmetic winner with 1.3 to 1.6 candidate rows per decision on average, and the stock contract leaves 0.35\% of rows (2.8\% of decode batches) to the stock kernel under the published accumulation model; no certified token differed from the engine's. The other measurements that decide the hypotheses (the drift of real draft states, head-path runtime, the attribution of time across the stack, and latency--throughput frontiers for an exact stack and for a lossy stack under a stated quality budget) are in progress; each appears as a marked pending item, never as a number.
\end{abstract}
