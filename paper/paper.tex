{\large Certified low-precision output heads under a stock-kernel contract,\\ and the serving stack of a hybrid model\par}
\vspace{2ex}
{\normalsize Samuel Mausberg\footnote{Code, exact references, committed evidence and the source of this manuscript: \repourl. Cite as Samuel Mausberg, \emph{The Work a Verifier Needs}, 2026, at that address.}\par}
\vspace{.4ex}
{\small\color{muted}Working draft, 30 September 2026\par}
\end{center}
\vspace{1ex}
\begin{abstract}
\noindent A speculative decoder asks its target model for a full vocabulary projection at every verified position, although a greedy verifier needs only the winning token. We ask how much of that projection a verifier needs for Qwen3.5-4B served by SGLang on one GH200, where the tied 248,320-row output head is 15\% of the weight bytes of a plain decode step and 84\% of those of a multi-token-prediction draft step. The mechanism the earlier revisions of this work were built on, transporting a drafter's tile summaries to the target's hidden state, fails on real states: for the public DFlash drafter and for the model's own multi-token-prediction layer, draft and target head inputs differ by a median 92\% and 95\% in relative $\ell_2$ distance, against the 0.85\% at which transport would beat an int8 envelope, and for every bound family and tiling tested its certificates skip at most 0.65\% of the vocabulary. A low-precision copy of the head works instead. Screening such a copy with an error bound is prior art for greedy heads; we state the contract it can meet against the engine's own closed-source head kernel. A row is certified only when enclosures of that kernel's BF16 outputs, under a model of its FP32 accumulation error, fix the token its first-index argmax returns at the same batch shape; every other row runs the stock kernel. We prove an envelope that covers Hopper tensor-core accumulation under the published Khattak--Mikaitis model and extend certification to seeded Gumbel sampling and fixed-noise speculative verification. On 6,005 captured plain-decode head inputs an int8 head certifies the exact winner with 1.3--1.6 candidate rows on average; under the Khattak--Mikaitis model 0.35\% of rows (2.8\% of the capture's batches of up to 16) need the stock kernel, 1.40\% (10.9\%) under a conservative model, and no certified token differed from the engine's. At serving batch sizes the case rests on a row-wise fallback. Head-path runtime and served frontiers are pending.
\end{abstract}
