\section{Mechanisms carried over but not tested in this revision}\label{app:untested}
The earlier revision developed a broader programme around the head. Nobody is testing these parts in this revision, so they are condensed here with their proved statements; they are hypotheses, not results.

\paragraph{The DFlash~2 selector.} At the inspected implementation adjacent candidate scores have the form
\begin{equation}
S_t(a,b)=U_t(b)+\sum_{r=1}^{d_s}A(a)_r\,H(h_t)_r\,B(b)_r,\label{eq:selector}
\end{equation}
with a gate that depends on the draft hidden state~\cite{dflash,sgcode}. A greedy walk needs only the realized row $S_t(x_{t-1},\cdot)$, which reduces score work from $O(BLK^2d_s)$ to $O(BLKd_s)$ at the price of a dependent sequence of small contractions. Alternatively the lattice can emit, for every predecessor, its winning successor $f_t(a)=\argmax_bS_t(a,b)$, after which the path is $x_t=(f_t\circ\cdots\circ f_1)(x_0)$.

\begin{proposition}[Finite-map execution]\label{prop:maps}
Given the same row winners, serial map walking and prefix composition produce the same path. A work-efficient scan of length $L$ over $K$-entry maps uses $O(LK)$ map work and $O(\log L)$ composition stages, excluding the score producer.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The $t$-th prefix composition applies exactly the first $t$ transitions to the anchor, and function composition is associative. A work-efficient tree scan uses $O(L)$ compositions of $O(K)$ indexed evaluations each, with logarithmic depth.
\end{proof}
Function scanning is standard; the question would be its profitable placement. The inherited CPU reference implements the simpler $O(LK\log L)$ Hillis--Steele scan and checks path equivalence only.

\paragraph{Prefix value.} On a fixed lattice with a substochastic surrogate $r_t(a,b)$ for the probability of matching the next target token, $J_r(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{L}\prod_{t\le j}r_t(x_{t-1},x_t)$ is maximized by $V_t(a)=\max_br_t(a,b)(1+V_{t+1}(b))$ with $V_{L+1}=0$. If the estimate $\hat r$ also lies in $[0,1]$ and edge errors are uniformly bounded by $\epsilon_t$, telescoping gives $|J_r-J_{\hat r}|\le\delta=\sum_t(L-t+1)\epsilon_t$ and optimizer regret at most $2\delta$. LiLiCorr's finding that an exact best-path search over a different score decodes worse than the greedy rule~\cite{lilicorr} is the relevant warning.

\paragraph{A compiler interface for evidence and effects.} The earlier revision proposed typed interfaces (head summaries, decision demands, state leases, evidence with an enclosure contract) so that illegal compositions become visible: a top-$k$ summary cannot satisfy a residual-sampling demand, a lease cannot be reused after cancellation, and a real-arithmetic enclosure cannot discharge a bitwise requirement without a numerical bridge. Search over implementations~\cite{mirage} would operate inside that boundary. No part of it is implemented.

\paragraph{Scheduling and structural tuning.} If predicted progress $F_i(d)$ is known and cost depends only on the total optional depth $m$, the snapshot problem is solved by
\begin{equation}
G_i(m)=\max_{0\le d\le L_i}\{G_{i-1}(m-d)+F_i(d)\},\qquad\max_m\frac{G_B(m)}{C(m;z)},\label{eq:schedDP}
\end{equation}
which is exact only under that cost hypothesis. Graph tiers make cost discontinuous: with rewards $(1,1.9,2.7)$ and costs $(1,2,2)$ at depths $(0,1,2)$, depth one looks worse than zero while depth two is best, so stopping at the first local decline fails. Autotuning a kernel that mutates state must restore that state between trials~\cite{tritonautotune}.

\paragraph{GPU execution families.} A certificate path can run as a two-stage mask-then-compact pipeline, as a bounded number of guarded refinement rounds inside a CUDA graph, or as a persistent work queue; ordinary inter-block spin barriers are not a legal substitute for global synchronization. Graph-safe designs need fixed buffers, device-resident masks and stable addresses, and a guarded stage that does nothing still costs a launch~\cite{vllmgraphs}. Nsight Systems establishes the critical path and Nsight Compute the traffic of selected kernels, whose replay and cache control change what is measured~\cite{nsight}. Hopper's asynchronous warp-group MMA requires \code{sm\_90a}, and Blackwell's data-centre and consumer targets expose different instruction sets and limits~\cite{ptxisa,hopper,blackwell}; implementation backends range from Triton to TileLang and CuTe~\cite{cute,tilelang,tritonautotune}.

\section{Mathematical extensions and small falsifiers}\label{app:math}
\paragraph{Transformed logits.} If a transform adds a known per-token correction $b_i$ with tile bounds $b_c^-\le b_i\le b_c^+$, then $z_i^d+a_c-\epsilon_c+b_c^-\le z_i^t+b_i\le z_i^d+a_c+\epsilon_c+b_c^+$, and the mass bounds multiply by $e^{b_c^\pm}$. A mask that deletes unknown members cannot preserve the unmasked lower mass; a safe lower bound may collapse to zero.

\paragraph{One anchor mass cannot follow a temperature change.} The unnormalized masses $(1,3)$ and $(2,2)$ both sum to four, while their squares sum to ten and eight, so a mass at a new temperature cannot be reconstructed from the old sum alone.

\paragraph{A conditional rate certificate.} If positive intervals enclose expected progress and time for a candidate and a baseline, $R_1^-/T_1^+>R_0^+/T_0^-$ (with $T_0^->0$) certifies $\lambda_1>\lambda_0$ in~\eqref{eq:rate}. Benchmark confidence intervals are not deterministic enclosures, so the inequality exposes what must be estimated rather than certifying a speedup.

\paragraph{Agreement at an anchor proves nothing away from it.} Let $h^d=(1,0)$ and $h^t=(1,1)$. Rows with the same first coordinate have identical anchor logits while their second coordinates make the target logits arbitrarily different, so an anchor maximum or mass alone cannot certify the target; a geometric bound, more projection or exact completion is necessary.

\paragraph{RNG tests.} Exhaustive tiny-law tests should cover point-mass proposals, zero support, $p=q$, acceptance at the comparison boundary, all-accept cycles, first-position rejection and candidate-dependent stopping, with proposal draws, acceptance uniforms and residual noise aligned across the compared procedures.

\section{CPU evidence carried over from the earlier revision}\label{app:cpu}
The earlier revision's exact references use bounded integer head weights and hidden vectors, with unnormalized mass $2^z$ represented exactly in rational arithmetic; this is an exponential model with logits scaled by $\log2$ and avoids trusting floating exponentials. Eighteen test methods in the decision reference and three in the race reference pass, with four local tests of the benchmark client; Table~\ref{tab:tests} lists the generated finite instances inside them. The counts are not independent confirmations of anything about a trained model, and the race tests use fixed positive rational priorities, so they establish pathwise equivalence of the bounded and dense algorithms, not continuous Gumbel randomness. Seeds, commands and results are in \nolinkurl{evidence/decision_tests.json}, \nolinkurl{evidence/race_tests.json} and their logs.

\section{Reproduction}\label{app:repro}
The CPU evidence on \code{main} is reproduced from the repository root with Python 3.13 and NumPy:
\begin{lstlisting}
python -m venv .venv && . .venv/bin/activate
python -m pip install -r requirements-cpu.txt
python scripts/verify_artifact.py     # CPU tests, synthetic drift, Lean attempt
\end{lstlisting}
The paper builds with \code{latexmk -pdf paper.tex} in \code{paper/} (pdfLaTeX and BibTeX; no shell escape). Figures that read committed data files regenerate on each build. GPU experiments go through the repository's GPU lock and are documented with their exact commands in \code{RUNBOOK.md} and in the \code{README.md} of each \code{evidence/} directory.
