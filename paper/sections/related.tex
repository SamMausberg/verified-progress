\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}\label{sec:prior}
Descriptions below come from the literature review that accompanies this paper \ev{litreview}; numbers quoted from other work are the authors' own.

\paragraph{Certified low-precision heads}\label{sec:prior-certified} The greedy form of the certified head is public several times over. sparkpipe screens with an E4M3 shadow of a BF16 head whose per-group certificate covers CUDA-core FMA accumulation, re-scores survivors exactly, and routes the decode, last-prefill and MTP argmax rows of a model with the same 248,320-row vocabulary through it; sampled requests read the full head~\cite{sparkpipehead}. dgpp bounds logits from the high bit planes of an int8 head, re-reads survivors in the production accumulation order, and takes the quantized head as its reference with a fixed floating-point slack~\cite{dgpphead}. A Metal implementation re-scores candidates with a replica of the stock kernel~\cite{lagunaprune}, and knlp certifies with an int8 shadow in a PCA basis but reports an argmax match of 0.998 at 14B parameters~\cite{knlphead}. Du et al.\ show why the reference must be named: BF16 and FP16 greedy decoding diverge on 49--100\% of prompts in their experiments, decided at the head by the top-two margin~\cite{precisioninvariant}.

\paragraph{Screening and exact search} Cheap scores with bounds and exact refinement are old in similarity search~\cite{vafile,fexipro,lemp,ramgray2012}, where blocked dense multiplication often wins end to end~\cite{maximus}, a caution that applies here. SVD-Softmax and HiRE recompute a fixed top set chosen by a cheap copy of the head, with no certificate that the winner is in it~\cite{svdsoftmax,hire}. CSV-Decode bounds clusters of head rows by $\langle\mu_c,h\rangle+R_c\norm h$ and opens clusters until the top-$k$ is certified~\cite{csvdecode}: the static screen of Table~\ref{tab:transport}. Transport differs from these screens only in starting from a score measured at the draft state.

\paragraph{Sampling and speculation} The Gumbel-max identity makes sampling a race~\cite{gumbel1954,papandreou2011,kool2019}; A$^\ast$ Sampling and Mussmann et al.\ sample exactly from bounded scores by instantiating only the perturbations that could win~\cite{astar,mussmann2017}. FlashSampling fuses Gumbel-max sampling into the head projection over every tile, exact in distribution, with tiling-dependent noise~\cite{flashsampling}; it is a runtime comparator for any certified head. Batch-invariant kernels make greedy outputs reproducible across batches~\cite{he2025nondeterminism,sglangdeterministic}, which changes the kernel a certificate must match. Drafter-invariant decoding couples draft and target through shared Gumbel noise~\cite{daliri2025,rowan2025}, which Section~\ref{sec:decisions} uses to make sampled verification a race; DSpark and D-cut schedule verification depth and state the non-anticipating condition for lossless sampled speculation~\cite{dspark,dcut}. Drafters such as EAGLE, native MTP and DFlash project through a head at every draft step~\cite{eagle,eagle3,gloeckle2024,dflashpaper}, and draft-vocabulary reduction trades their acceptance for draft cost~\cite{frspec,vocabtrim,specvocab,fastmtp}; a certified head keeps the full-vocabulary token and also applies on the target side.

\paragraph{What we do not claim} We do not claim norm or quantization bounds, bound-then-refine search, certified greedy screening of an LM head, sampling from bounded scores, drafter-invariant verification or the non-anticipating condition. The certified head's claim is the narrow one of Section~\ref{sec:kernel-design}. Transport of draft-side tile summaries through a shared head is the one mechanism here without a precedent we found, and it is a negative result.
