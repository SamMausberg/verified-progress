\section{Exact repair of long windows}\label{sec:repair}
A drafter's window is verified once and then discarded after the first mismatch. Two later proposals ask whether the target's work on a long window can be reused instead. Both keep exactness by the same device: only tokens that an exact audit pass of the target has verified are committed, approximate states never enter the committed cache, and the noise of a position is never resampled. Both are hypotheses under test in this revision, and their kill tests are designed so that a negative answer is cheap to obtain.

\paragraph{Long-window repair (P2).} Greedy continuation is a triangular system $y_i=F_i(y_{<i})$. A parallel repair step $y^{(r+1)}_i=F_i(y^{(r)}_{<i})$ from a window initialized by the drafter commits the verified prefix plus the target's token at the first mismatch; suffix outputs are new guesses, not certified outputs or reusable states. After $r$ sweeps at least $r$ positions are correct, so repair converges within the window, but nothing guarantees one or two sweeps suffice. This is Jacobi decoding applied to a drafted window; lookahead and consistency-trained decoders pursue related fixed-point iterations~\cite{lookahead,cllm}. For sampling, fixed per-position random inputs held across repairs give the target law. The gated-delta recurrence composes associatively, so a verifier could reconstruct state only at the accepted boundary instead of materializing every position; whether SGLang already does so is part of the test. An end-to-end speedup $S$ with a non-decode fraction $f$ needs a decode speedup $s$ with $1/(f+(1-f)/s)=S$: for $S=5$, $s=5.44$ at $f=2\%$ and $s=9$ at $f=10\%$ (derived).

\paragraph{Target-anchored residual decoding (P3).} For each expensive linear operator $z=Wx$, retain an anchor $(\bar x,\bar z=W\bar x)$ from a full target pass over a candidate block and a fixed orthonormal basis $U_W$ with $WU_W$ precomputed; after a few token corrections, repair with $\tilde z=\bar z+(WU_W)U_W^\top(x-\bar x)$, leaving out $W(I-U_WU_W^\top)(x-\bar x)$. Fixed bases matter: a basis computed from the current correction would stream $W$ again. Nonlinearities are evaluated exactly on the repaired activations, and an exact audit pass commits only verified tokens, so exactness holds whatever the quality of the repair. The open question is whether one changed token leaves most downstream activations close enough to the anchor for the audit to accept long prefixes while paying for both full passes, the repairs and the activation cache.

\paragraph{What the repair experiment must show.} Local low rank does not imply useful repair of a whole model. Three things must hold separately: the fixed cheap operators must stay accurate after candidate tokens change, not only near the anchor; their errors must stay below the decision margins after attention, nonlinearities, the recurrent state and later layers; and each sweep must resolve enough positions to pay for itself and for the audit. A sufficient condition links these quantities. Let $m_i$ be the exact top-two logit margin at position $i$, $\varepsilon_i$ a bound on the logit error the approximate evaluator makes at $i$ when its inputs are exact, $b_j$ a bound on the error in the current guess's effect at an earlier position $j$ ($b_j=0$ once $j$ is correct), and $C_{ij}$ the influence of position $j$ on the logits at $i$. If $2(\varepsilon_i+\sum_{j<i}C_{ij}b_j)<m_i$, position $i$ is correct after the next sweep, by the triangle inequality. Two consequences follow. A persistent approximation error $\eta$ in a recurrence with contraction factor $a<1$ leaves an error floor of about $\eta/(1-a)$, and a faithful evaluator can advance only as fast as exact Jacobi iteration, which on a copy chain $F_i(y)=y_{i-1}$ gains one position per sweep even with exact rank-one operators. Expected accepted length is best read through hazards: with $h_i$ the conditional failure rate at position $i$ given an accepted prefix, $\E A=\sum_a\prod_{i\le a}(1-h_i)$, so the experiment reports prefix advance per sweep and the ratio $R_i=2\norm{\tilde z_i-z_i}_\infty/m_i$ ($R_i<1$ guarantees the same decision) on two cascading replays against the original anchor, not hidden-state error. The economic gate is $\mu_R/C_R>\mu_0/C_0$, where $\mu$ is the mean number of committed tokens per attempt and $C$ its mean cost for the repair scheme and the baseline; since no attempt over a block of width $B$ commits more than $B+1$ tokens, a lower bound on repair cost with $C_R^{\rm lower}/(B+1)\ge C_0/\mu_0$ rejects the design outright. The decisive control is a small target-conditioned drafter given the same anchor information and matched in compute, memory traffic, training data, verifier width and target calls; if it does as well, the supported claim is better target-conditioned drafting, not cheap repair of a trajectory. This is analysis that fixes the measurements, not a result.
