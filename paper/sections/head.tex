\section{The Certified Head}\label{sec:selfevidence}\label{sec:head}
The certified head finds the \Rstock{} token from an int8 copy of $W$ evaluated at the target's own head input.

\subsection{An Error Bound for the Int8 Copy}\label{sec:envelope}\label{sec:accumulation}
The deployed copy stores each row as int8 codes $q_i$ with one scale $s_i$, so $\bar w_i=s_iq_i$ approximates $w_i$ with a known error $e_i=w_i-\bar w_i$. The pass that evaluates the copy multiplies the codes by the BF16 head input and sums in FP32 on tensor cores. Their adders align several addends to the largest, truncate them and round once~\cite{fasi2021}, so the standard bound for IEEE additions~\cite{higham2002} does not apply. We bound the sum's error by $\gamma_{\rm copy}\sum_j|\bar w_{ij}h_j|$ with the conservative constant, $\gamma_{\rm copy}=\gcons$, whichever model is assumed for the stock kernel. Lemma~\ref{lem:tree} and the adder model of Appendix~\ref{app:accumulation} show that it covers the accumulations listed for the \modelcons{} in Section~\ref{sec:headcontracts}.

\begin{theorem}[Error bound of the copy]\label{thm:envelope}
If every product is exact in FP32 and the pass's accumulation error is at most $\gamma_{\rm copy}\sum_j|\bar w_{ij}h_j|$ plus an underflow floor $\eta'$, its value $\tilde z_i$ satisfies $|z_i-\tilde z_i|\le\beta_i=\norm{e_i}_2\norm h_2+\gamma_{\rm copy}\norm{\bar w_i}_2\norm h_2+\eta'$.
\end{theorem}
We call $\beta_i$ the copy's \emph{error bound}: under the theorem's assumptions, entry $i$'s real logit lies in the interval $\tilde z_i\mp\beta_i$. The kernel rounds the norms of $h$ up and the interval ends outward, so every comparison it computes is sound, provided it evaluates the analysed operations (Table~\ref{tab:assumptions}). The screen thus also rests on a hardware assumption, always the conservative one; the \modelhopper{} enters only through the stock kernel's $G_i$. The bound assumes the worst alignment of $e_i$ and $h$, so it is loose: its median on captured states is 1.00 logit, about 75 times the median realized error \ev{stats}. Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} proves a general form (Theorem~\ref{thm:envelope-general}) and gives $\eta'$.

\subsection{Refinement Against the Stock Kernel}\label{sec:ladder}
The certified head decides each position in five steps.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item Evaluate the int8 copy for every entry and form the intervals $[\lo_i,\hi_i]=\tilde z_i\mp\beta_i$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:envelope}).
\item Keep as \emph{candidates} the entries with $\hi_i\ge\max_j\lo_j-\delta_{\max}$; given valid intervals, no other entry can be the stock token (Section~\ref{sec:headcontracts}).
\item Recompute each candidate's real logit from its BF16 row in FP64 (whose tiny rounding error is added) and widen it by $G_i$ to an interval for the stock kernel's accumulator.
\item Round these intervals to BF16 and apply Proposition~\ref{prop:stockinterval}; if it fixes a token, emit it.
\item Otherwise send the position to a fallback (Section~\ref{sec:kernel-fallback}).
\end{enumerate}
The screen in step 2 changes which entries are recomputed, never which decisions stay open, because an entry it drops loses to the stock token under every admissible accumulation. The same steps, with $\delta_{\max}$ removed, decide the \Rreal{} token; Appendix~\ref{app:ladder} states the general refinement and what of it is machine-checked.

\subsection{Seeded Sampling and Fixed-Noise Verification}\label{sec:decisions}\label{sec:race}\label{sec:fixednoise}
With a seed and no top-$k$ or top-$p$ truncation, SGLang samples a token as the winner of a race: it adds Gumbel noise, computed from a 32-bit hash of (seed, token position in the sequence, token), to the log-probabilities and takes the argmax~\cite{sglangsampler}. Because the noise is fixed before any score is known, the same five steps certify the race's winner, with the bounds applied to the noisy scores (Appendix~\ref{app:sampling}). The stock seeded sampler exists only in SGLang's deterministic-inference mode, whose head kernel is DeepGEMM's, not cuBLAS; the \modelcons{} covers it, and the \modelhopper{} is unchecked for it. With its fallback, the certified decision returned the sampler's token at all 60,000 kernel-replay positions at $T=0.7$ and 1, on the same logits \ev{kernel-seeded}, and at all 21,016 verification positions of the engine check at $T=0.7$ \ev{kernel-engine}. We also implemented \emph{fixed-noise verification}~\cite{daliri2025}, which accepts a drafted token exactly when it wins the target's race at its position. Against the rejection sampling of SGLang's speculative sampler, which it replaces, it accepts deterministic drafts (SGLang's default) as often and sampled drafts as little as half as often (Appendix~\ref{app:coupling}); it follows the target's distribution only for idealized continuous noise and prefix-only logits, which the engine does not guarantee (Appendix~\ref{app:fixednoise}), and its acceptance on real drafts was not measured.

\subsection{The Kernel}\label{sec:kernel-design}
The certified head runs as a short pipeline of GPU launches whose grids depend only on $M$, recorded with its fallback as one CUDA graph per batch size. The int8 pass multiplies the int8 weights by the BF16 head inputs (W8A16), accumulates in FP32 on tensor cores and computes each interval in its epilogue with directed rounding. Instead of writing the $M\times V$ logits, each GPU program keeps, per position and chunk of vocabulary entries, the four largest upper ends and the largest among the rest; a selection step then keeps every stored entry that reaches the screen's threshold, and the whole chunk if the rest's largest does, so no candidate is lost. A position whose candidates overflow the list's fixed capacity goes to the whole-batch fallback. A kernel inherits the error bound only if it computes the modelled arithmetic, which is checked, not proved: each compiled variant must pass a self-test before it certifies, and every call checks eight exactly computed vocabulary entries per position \ev{kernel-impl}. The checks caught one fault, wrong products from Triton 3.7.1's int8 tiles loaded through a 64-byte TMA box, now refused (Appendix~\ref{app:kernel-checks}) \ev{kernel-tma,triton-tma}. The int8 copy takes 0.64~GB beside the resident BF16 head (derived).
