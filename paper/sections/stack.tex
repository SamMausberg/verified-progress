\section{Stacking every axis}\label{sec:stack}
The certified head and a better drafter each attack one term of the verifier's cost. When the head is a sixth of a plain step's bytes and the recurrent state dominates at high concurrency, a paper about avoided work also has to ask what the whole stack can gain when every term is attacked, which of those gains keep the target's decisions, and what the others cost in quality. Table~\ref{tab:axes} lists the axes and levers in scope. Each lever is built as a separate flag or arm so that it composes, and compositions are measured in the baseline, $A$, $B$, $A{+}B$ pattern rather than by multiplying isolated speedups: a lossy target lowers acceptance, a cheaper verifier makes drafting relatively more expensive, and quality losses from separate approximations can compound.

\paragraph{Two frontiers.} The \emph{exact stack} uses only techniques that keep the target's decisions: the certified head, draft-side approximations (the target still verifies), better drafting, and capacity and system work. The \emph{lossy stack} uses everything, under a quality budget declared before the measurement: at most 1.0 point of GSM8K accuracy lost, at least 98\% teacher-forced top-1 agreement with the BF16 reference, and at most 0.01 nats of mean top-20 KL divergence from it. The quality cost of the combined lossy stack is measured directly, not summed from its parts. Proposition~\ref{prop:kl} explains why the budget is empirical: a per-token logit error small enough to certify a long output as close in total variation is far below what any low-precision lever delivers.

\paragraph{Writing the recurrent state less often.} Rounding-preserving live replay (Section~\ref{sec:minimal-state}) is exact and cuts dense-state traffic per step from $2D$ (read and write) to $D+D/m$ when the state is flushed every $m$ steps, $0.625$ of the original at $m=4$, for a few percent more storage (derived). If the recurrent kernel is a fraction $f$ of a decode step, a kernel twice as fast gives at most $1/(1-f/2)$, removing it entirely $1/(1-f)$, and the $m=4$ traffic cut alone $1/(1-0.375f)$ (Amdahl). From bytes alone, $f$ would be about $0.55$ at batch 128 and a 512-token context (12.9~GB of FP32 state against 8.41~GB of weights and 2.1~GB of attention KV; derived, Table~\ref{tab:bytes}), which would give $1.38\times$, $2.2\times$ and $1.26\times$; the measured time share is \pending{profile: GDN kernel share of a batch-128 step}. The pre-registered test decodes 512 tokens greedily at batch 128 from 2,048-token prompts with FP32 state and no speculation, first compares every state word and GDN output of the replayed and dense paths (one differing word rejects bit-exactness), and then times both: the claim is at least $1.10\times$ end-to-end decode throughput, rejected if the upper end of the paired 95\% confidence interval falls below $1.10\times$. SGLang's own replay path qualifies only if it passes the bit-exactness check. \pending{moonshot: bit-exactness check and paired timing}

\paragraph{Sharing computation across requests.} Factoring hidden or recurrent state across unrelated requests does not survive analysis. An exact factorization with a dense residual costs the full projection, so it saves only if $r(d+B)+z<Bd$ with a zero or sparse residual; SiLU and gating raise rank; full-rank pairwise state differences force private factors at least as large as the dense state; one gated-delta step takes four identical states to batch rank four (\code{tests/test\_state\_structure.py}, \nolinkurl{evidence/state_structure/}); online factorization costs of order $B^2$ times the update; and reassociation breaks equality with the reference. The one exact candidate is structural: the first GDN layer's input projections (12,352 outputs) depend only on the current token, so they could be read from a precomputed per-token table (5.71~GiB for the whole vocabulary) with convolution and recurrence kept private. Its ceiling is about 0.75\% of the dense projection work per token (derived), and at batch 128 even a 99\% table hit rate leaves about 72\% of batches with a miss. The pre-registered criterion rejects it unless the stage's measured share of a decode step allows a 1\% end-to-end gain \pending{profile: measured share of the layer-0 input projections, PR~\#13}.
