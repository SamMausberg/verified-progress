\begin{abstract}
In speculative decoding a target model checks tokens that a cheaper drafter proposes. Like greedy decoding, a greedy or seeded verifier needs one fact from the output head at each position, the winning token, yet the engine scores the whole vocabulary. That token can be found more cheaply: approximate every score from an int8 copy of the head, bound each approximation's error, recompute exactly the few entries that could still win, and fall back to the engine's own head when the bounds cannot decide. Inside a production engine the token must be the one the engine's head kernel returns at the same batch shape, after BF16 rounding and its tie rule. We prove when bounds on the kernel's FP32 sums fix this token, given an assumed bound on its rounding error. For Qwen3.5-4B in SGLang on a GH200, on 6,005 positions captured from greedy decoding, the int8 copy recomputes 1.3--1.6 entries per decision and leaves 0.35\% of positions to the stock head under a published model of Hopper accumulation, or 1.40\% under a conservative model; no certified token differed from the engine's. Timed alone, the certified head takes about 0.7 of the stock head's time up to 16 positions and is slower from 128; its effect on serving is unmeasured. Bounds carried over from the drafter's head computation fail for both public drafters under every bound family and tiling tested: their head inputs differ from the target's by a median 92\% and 95\%, where about 1\% would be needed.
\end{abstract}
