\begin{abstract}
A greedy or seeded speculative verifier needs one fact from the target's output head, the winning token, yet computes every logit. Certifying that winner from a cheap copy of the head is known; we state the contract it must meet inside a production engine and measure its limits. For Qwen3.5-4B served by SGLang on a GH200, that is agreement with the token the engine's own head kernel returns at the same batch shape, BF16 rounding and first-index tie rule included, and we prove when enclosures of that kernel's FP32 accumulator, under a named error model, fix it; every other row runs the stock kernel. On 6,005 captured decode states, an int8 copy of the head with a proved envelope re-scores 1.3--1.6 rows per decision on average and leaves 0.35\% of rows to the stock kernel under a published model of Hopper tensor-core accumulation, or 1.40\% under a conservative model, and no certified token differed from the engine's; the head-path runtime is pending. Transport, which would reuse a drafter's per-tile head summaries at the target's state, fails on both public drafters: their head inputs differ from the target's by a median 92\% and 95\%, where transport needs agreement to about 1\%, so for every bound family and tiling tested its certificates skip at most 0.65\% of the vocabulary.
\end{abstract}
