\begin{abstract}
In speculative decoding a target model checks tokens that a cheaper drafter proposes. At each position a greedy verifier, like greedy decoding, needs one fact from the output head, the winning token, yet the engine scores the whole vocabulary. That token can be found more cheaply: approximate every score from an int8 copy of the head, bound each approximation's error, recompute exactly the few entries that could still win, and fall back to the engine's own head when the bounds cannot decide. Inside a production engine the token must be the one the engine's head returns for a batch of the same size, after BF16 rounding and its tie rule. We prove when bounds on the head's FP32 sums fix this token, given an assumed bound on its rounding error, and extend the method to seeded sampling. For Qwen3.5-4B in SGLang on a GH200, replaying 60,000 positions captured from greedy decoding, the GPU kernel recomputes about 1.8 entries per decision and leaves 0.28\% of positions to the engine's head under a published model of Hopper accumulation, or 1.46\% under a conservative model; with that fallback every token equals the engine's. In a 64-prompt check inside SGLang under the conservative model no certified token differed from the engine's head, but for one public drafter 90\% of verify batches still needed the fallback. Timed alone, with fallbacks costed at their measured rates, the certified head is expected to take about 0.7 times the engine head's time for calls of up to 16 positions, which would save at most about 3.4--3.5\% of a batch-1 decode step (derived). Served in three sessions, it raises throughput at one concurrent request by 2.5\% for plain decoding, 4.6\% for MTP drafting and 1.2\% for DFlash, with identical tokens, but loses 1.0--3.0\% once DFlash's certified batches reach 28--64 rows, so the best served configuration improves only at one request. At 1 to 8 concurrent requests the largest exact serving gain came from host work: making attention planning cheaper and free of blocking reads raised MTP drafting's throughput with FlashInfer attention by 9.4--9.6\% (one session, token-identical outputs). Bounds carried over from the drafter's own head computation are sound but too loose to skip more than 0.7\% of the vocabulary in any variant tested: the distance between draft and target head inputs, relative to the target's, has a median of 92\% and 95\% for the two public drafters, where about 1\% is needed.
\end{abstract}
