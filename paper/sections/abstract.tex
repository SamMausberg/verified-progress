\begin{abstract}
A greedy verifier in speculative decoding needs only the LM head's winning token, yet the engine scores the whole vocabulary. We ask how little of that work an exact shortcut needs. Exact must mean the engine's own token, BF16 rounding and tie rule included: for Qwen3.5-4B in SGLang on a GH200, the exact-arithmetic winner differs from it at 0.5\% of greedy positions. A certified head bounds every score from an int8 copy of the head and recomputes exactly the few that could win; undecided positions fall back to the engine's head. We prove when the bounds fix the token, under an error model of the closed-source kernel that is tested, not proved. It recomputes about 1.8 entries per decision, and every token matches the engine head's on the same batch, in replay and, with the stock head beside it, while serving. Against tuned stock configurations it raises throughput 1.2--4.6\% at one request, but slows the fastest configuration at a few concurrent requests, where most verify calls fall back. Bounds carried from the drafters' heads skip almost no vocabulary in any variant tested, because draft and target head inputs differ by a median 92--95\% of the target's norm. Attention, recurrent-state and admission changes gain more.
\end{abstract}
