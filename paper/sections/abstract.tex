\begin{abstract}
In speculative decoding a target model checks tokens that a cheaper drafter proposes. At each position a greedy verifier, like greedy decoding, needs one fact from the output head, the winning token, yet the engine scores the whole vocabulary. That token can be found more cheaply: approximate every score from an int8 copy of the head, bound each approximation's error, recompute exactly the few entries that could still win, and fall back to the engine's own head when the bounds cannot decide. Inside a production engine the token must be the one the engine's head returns for a batch of the same size, after BF16 rounding and its tie rule. We prove when bounds on the head's FP32 sums fix this token, given an assumed bound on its rounding error, and extend the method to seeded sampling. For Qwen3.5-4B in SGLang on a GH200, replaying 60,000 positions captured from greedy decoding, the GPU kernel recomputes about 1.8 entries per decision. It leaves 0.28\% of positions to the engine's head under a published model of Hopper accumulation, or 1.46\% under a conservative model, and with that fallback every token equals the engine's. Timed alone, its expected time, computed from measured times and fallback rates, is about 0.7 times the engine head's for calls of up to 16 positions, with a per-position fallback that the engine exercised only in plain decoding. That would save at most about 3.4--3.5\% of a batch-1 decode step (derived); from 128 positions it is slower, and its effect on serving is not yet measured. In serving, the largest exact gain we measured came from host work: planning attention without blocking reads raised MTP drafting's throughput 9.4--9.6\% at 1 to 8 concurrent requests (one session, token-identical outputs). In a 64-prompt check inside SGLang under the conservative model no certified token differed from the engine's head, but for one public drafter 90\% of verify batches still needed the fallback. Bounds carried over from the drafter's own head computation are sound but too loose to skip more than 0.7\% of the vocabulary, for every variant tested: the distance between draft and target head inputs, relative to the target's, has a median of 92\% and 95\% for the two public drafters, where about 1\% is needed.
\end{abstract}
