\paragraph{Recurrent state and attention} Removing per-request state traffic pays as the batch grows. \emph{Replayed GDN decoding}, SGLang's buffered decode, keeps recent inputs in a ring and writes the full recurrent state only every 16 tokens. It gains 8.1\% at $c=128$ (one run pair) \ev{bench-frontier}. \emph{Fold verify}, SGLang's fold-every-commit protocol, verifies DFlash blocks without per-position state snapshots. In one session it costs 3.2\% at $c=1$ and gains 6.1\% at $c=8$ \ev{drafter-fold}. Composed with routing and the head, it fell below the range declared before the run at $c=2$--8 (Appendix~\ref{app:stack-composition}). Up to $c=32$ it gains most with \emph{narrow verify tiles} (the stock verify's tiles, used by the fold's kernel at one or two requests) and \emph{FA4} (FlashAttention~4) attention for the target and, on 16-token blocks, the drafter. FA4 needs a backported fix to its paged-KV loader to compile at the target's head dimension of 256 on this GPU. On the envelope's DFlash arms the three changes together raise the per-request rate 8.4--9.3\% at $c=1$--4 and throughput 12.4--13.7\% at 8--32 in three sessions, exact up to rounding as classed at $c=1$ \ev{lowc-confirm}.

\paragraph{Admission} From $c=48$ speculation loses on admissions, since its requests finish and are prefilled one or two at a time. SGLang's \emph{prefill delay} holds new prefills while the waiting queue is short or few slots are free. With each prefill batch capped at 16 requests, it lets MTP serve 1.18--1.32 times the best non-speculative arm at $c=48$--128 in three sessions, exact up to rounding (classed at $c=64$ and 128). The price is a 99th-percentile time to first token of 2.6--3.1 times plain decoding's \ev{admission}.

\paragraph{Host and GEMM time} The \emph{host-gap patches} remove blocking GPU reads from FlashInfer's attention planning, host work that idles the MTP cycle (Figure~\ref{fig:breakdown}). In one session they raise MTP's throughput 9.4--9.6\% at $c=1$--8 \ev{hostgap}. Stock MTP with Triton attention, which plans nothing on the host, is still faster \ev{bench-frontier}. \emph{Backbone GEMM routing} sends each backbone weight GEMM to the kernel a microbenchmark found fastest for its shape. It gains 3.4--3.5\% for plain decoding at $c=1$ but not for MTP \ev{backbone}.

\paragraph{Declared approximations} Two declared approximations change the model's arithmetic, and both miss the quality budget set in advance (GSM8K within 1 point; a logit probe) \ev{lossy}. FP16 recurrent state serves 1.16--1.17 times the best non-lossy arm timed with it at $c=64$--256 (three sessions). It scores 1.0--1.4 GSM8K points below plain decoding's reference runs, within the exact arms' own spread. An int4 target with its int4 drafter~\cite{nota2026} fails the probe and is slower above $c=1$.
