\section{Transport on real hidden states}\label{sec:transport}
Transport was the central mechanism of the earlier revisions. This section states it with the hypotheses that the proofs need, derives the condition under which it can beat the alternative, and reports what happened when real draft and target states were compared.

\subsection{Tile geometry and already-paid summaries}\label{sec:transport-def}
Let $W\in\R^{V\times D}$ be the head used for both a draft hidden vector $h^d$ and an aligned target hidden vector $h^t$, both taken at the head input (after the final norm). Write $\Delta=h^t-h^d$, $z_i^d=\langle w_i,h^d\rangle$ and $z_i^t=\langle w_i,h^t\rangle$. A vocabulary partition $\mathcal C$ covers all admitted tokens with disjoint tiles. For each tile $c$ choose a centre $\mu_c$ and a radius
\begin{equation}
r_c\ge\max_{i\in c}\norm{w_i-\mu_c}_\infty,\qquad a_c=\langle\mu_c,\Delta\rangle,\qquad\epsilon_c=r_c\norm\Delta_1.\label{eq:geometry}
\end{equation}
The draft pass emits $M_c^d=\max_{i\in c}z_i^d$ and $Z_c^d=\sum_{i\in c}\exp(z_i^d)$; stable implementations store a maximum and a scaled mass. The centres and radii are checkpoint metadata; the maxima and masses belong to one request, position and draft vector.

\begin{theorem}[Transported tile certificates]\label{thm:transport}
In real arithmetic, with the same head matrix for both passes and both masses taken at the same temperature, every $i\in c$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
z_i^d+a_c-\epsilon_c\le z_i^t\le z_i^d+a_c+\epsilon_c.\label{eq:transport-row}
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{align}
\max_{i\in c}z_i^t&\le M_c^d+a_c+\epsilon_c=:U_c,\label{eq:maxbound}\\
e^{a_c-\epsilon_c}Z_c^d&\le Z_c^t\le e^{a_c+\epsilon_c}Z_c^d.\label{eq:massbound}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$z_i^t-z_i^d-a_c=\langle w_i-\mu_c,\Delta\rangle$, whose absolute value is at most $r_c\norm\Delta_1$ by Hölder's inequality. Maximization gives~\eqref{eq:maxbound}; monotonicity of the exponential and summation give~\eqref{eq:massbound}.
\end{proof}
In the engine the draft maxima and masses come from BF16-rounded draft logits, so~\eqref{eq:maxbound} and~\eqref{eq:massbound} must be widened by the draft kernel's error before they enclose anything the engine computes; Section~\ref{sec:selfevidence} gives the terms. The Euclidean form replaces $r_c$ by $r^{(2)}_c\ge\max_{i\in c}\norm{w_i-\mu_c}_2$ and $\norm\Delta_1$ by $\norm\Delta_2$.

\subsection{A summary that supports both selection and mass}
For a set of labelled logits, keep its top $k$ items under descending score then ascending token index, and the mass of every discarded item. Two such summaries merge by taking the top $k$ of their retained union and adding the newly discarded mass to the two old tails.

\begin{proposition}[Top-$k$ plus tail is a compositional summary]\label{prop:summary}
For disjoint finite domains the merge produces the same top-$k$ items and tail mass as summarizing the union, and it is associative and commutative in exact arithmetic under the common total order.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
An item omitted from a local top-$k$ has at least $k$ locally superior items and cannot enter the union's top-$k$, so every possible winner is retained. The original tails and the newly discarded items partition the final tail, so summing them gives its mass without subtracting nearly equal totals. Every parenthesization and order represents the same union.
\end{proof}
This is a sufficient-statistic construction from familiar reductions, and floating-point tail masses are not associative. Its value is an interface: one epilogue can serve candidate generation, bounds and completion. Retaining a small set $R_c$ per tile also tightens the mass bounds: recompute its target logits exactly and transport only the rest,
\begin{equation}
\sum_{i\in R_c}e^{z_i^t}+e^{a_c-\epsilon_c}Z^d_{c\setminus R_c}\le Z_c^t\le\sum_{i\in R_c}e^{z_i^t}+e^{a_c+\epsilon_c}Z^d_{c\setminus R_c},\label{eq:tail}
\end{equation}
which is never looser than transporting the whole tile, at the cost of $|R_c|$ extra row projections.

\subsection{Geometry decides}\label{sec:geometry}
A good draft does not imply a small $\norm{h^t-h^d}$. The head can ignore directions in which the states differ, and probabilities ignore a common shift of all logits, yet a crude hidden-space norm charges both. With $W=((1,0),(0,0))$ and $\Delta=(0,L)$ both logits are unchanged for every $L$, while $r_c\norm\Delta_1$ grows with $L$; coordinate radii remove that particular failure. The bounds form a hierarchy: a scalar radius; coordinate radii with $\epsilon_c=\sum_jr_{cj}|\Delta_j|$; grouped norms
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_c=\sum_gr_{cg}\norm{\Delta_g}_2,\qquad r_{cg}\ge\max_{i\in c}\norm{(w_i-\mu_c)_g}_2;\label{eq:groupbound}
\end{equation}
and a low-rank split $w_i=\mu_c+Qu_i+e_i$ with the projected part evaluated exactly and a norm bound on $e_i$. Richer geometry costs metadata and bound arithmetic, and a learned centre or subspace does not certify the residual radius; that radius must cover the stored weights.

\subsection{Greedy and top-$k$ admission}
Suppose at least $k$ distinct target rows have been evaluated and $\tau$ is the $k$-th best evaluated score. A tile may be skipped only when $U_c<\tau$; then at least $k$ evaluated items outrank every skipped one. The strict inequality preserves unresolved ties. This establishes exact selection; it does not establish an exact unrestricted softmax, because an omitted logit still contributes to the normalizer even when it cannot win.

\subsection{What transport must beat}\label{sec:crossover}
The alternative source of evidence (Section~\ref{sec:selfevidence}) evaluates a low-precision copy $\bar w_i=w_i-e_i$ of each row at the target state. In real arithmetic its error for row $i$ is $\langle e_i,h^t\rangle$, bounded by $\norm{e_i}_2\norm{h^t}_2$, where $e_i$ is known offline. Transport's error is $\langle w_i-\mu_c,\Delta\rangle$. The two can be compared exactly.

\begin{proposition}[No better bound from the same information]\label{prop:suprema}
For fixed $h$ and radii $\epsilon_b\ge0$ over a partition of the coordinates into blocks $b$,
$\sup\{|\langle e,h\rangle|:\norm{e_b}_2\le\epsilon_b\ \forall b\}=\sum_b\epsilon_b\norm{h_b}_2$ and
$\sup\{|\langle e,h\rangle|:\norm{e_b}_\infty\le\epsilon_b\ \forall b\}=\sum_b\epsilon_b\norm{h_b}_1$.
Likewise $\sup\{\langle w-\mu,\Delta\rangle:\norm{w-\mu}_\infty\le r\}=r\norm\Delta_1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The inequalities are Cauchy--Schwarz and Hölder. Equality holds at $e_b=\epsilon_bh_b/\norm{h_b}_2$, at $e_b=\epsilon_b\operatorname{sign}(h_b)$ and at $w=\mu+r\operatorname{sign}(\Delta)$.
\end{proof}
Both envelopes are therefore optimal for the metadata they use. For errors not aligned with $h$ the realized $|\langle e_i,h\rangle|$ is typically about $\norm{e_i}_2\norm h_2/\sqrt D$; closing that gap needs more arithmetic, not a better inequality.

\begin{proposition}[Exclusion conditions]\label{prop:exclusion}
After the winner $a$ is scored exactly, let $m_i=z_a-z_i$. Row-level transport excludes $i$ if and only if $m_i>r_c\norm\Delta_1-\langle w_i-\mu_c,\Delta\rangle$; tile-level transport excludes tile $c$ if and only if $z_a>M^d_c+\langle\mu_c,\Delta\rangle+r_c\norm\Delta_1$; self-evidence (in real arithmetic, with envelope $\beta_i$) excludes $i$ if and only if $m_i>\beta_i-\langle e_i,h\rangle$. The slack terms lie in $[0,2r_c\norm\Delta_1]$ and $[0,2\beta_i]$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Substitute $z_i^d+\langle\mu_c,\Delta\rangle=z_i-\langle w_i-\mu_c,\Delta\rangle$ and $\tilde z_i=z_i-\langle e_i,h\rangle$ into the upper bounds.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Transport cannot beat self-evidence unless drift is small]\label{thm:crossover}
For every tile $c$ and every centre $\mu_c$, $r_c=\max_{k\in c}\norm{w_k-\mu_c}_\infty\ge\tfrac12\operatorname{diam}_\infty(c)$ and $r^{(2)}_c=\max_{k\in c}\norm{w_k-\mu_c}_2\ge\tfrac12\operatorname{diam}_2(c)$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item (Hölder family.) With $b$-bit round-to-nearest per-row codes, $s_i=\norm{w_i}_\infty/(2^{b-1}-1)$ and no clipping, $|\langle e_i,h\rangle|\le\tfrac{s_i}2\norm h_1$, and $\tfrac{s_i}2\norm h_1\le r_c\norm\Delta_1$ whenever $\norm\Delta_1/\norm h_1\ge\norm{w_i}_\infty/\bigl((2^{b-1}-1)\operatorname{diam}_\infty(c)\bigr)$. If some row of $c$ has the opposite sign, or zero, in the coordinate where $|w_{ij}|$ peaks, then $\operatorname{diam}_\infty(c)\ge\norm{w_i}_\infty$ and the condition is implied by $\norm\Delta_1/\norm h_1\ge1/(2^{b-1}-1)$: $1/127$ for int8 and $1/7$ for int4.
\item (Euclidean family.) $\norm{e_i}_2\norm h_2\le r^{(2)}_c\norm\Delta_2$ if and only if $\norm\Delta_2/\norm h_2\ge\delta^\ast_i:=\norm{e_i}_2/r^{(2)}_c$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For rows $k,l$ of the tile, $\norm{w_k-w_l}\le\norm{w_k-\mu_c}+\norm{w_l-\mu_c}\le2r_c$. Round-to-nearest codes give $|e_{ij}|\le s_i/2$, and Hölder gives the first inequality. The rest is algebra, and opposite signs at the peak coordinate give $|w_{ij}-w_{kj}|\ge|w_{ij}|$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Transport against the static screen]\label{prop:static}
If $\mu_c$ lies in the convex hull of the tile's rows, then $U^{\rm T}_c-U^{\rm static}_c\ge r_c(\norm\Delta_1-\norm h_1)$, where $U^{\rm T}_c=M^d_c+\langle\mu_c,\Delta\rangle+r_c\norm\Delta_1$ and $U^{\rm static}_c=\langle\mu_c,h\rangle+r_c\norm h_1$. Transport is therefore no tighter than the static screen with the same geometry once $\norm\Delta_1\ge\norm h_1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$U^{\rm T}_c-U^{\rm static}_c=(M^d_c-\langle\mu_c,h^d\rangle)+r_c(\norm\Delta_1-\norm h_1)$, and $M^d_c=\max_k\langle w_k,h^d\rangle\ge\langle\mu_c,h^d\rangle$ for a convex combination $\mu_c$.
\end{proof}

The quantity that decides the comparison is the \emph{drift ratio}
\begin{equation}
\rho=\frac{\norm{h^t-h^d}_2}{\norm{h^t}_2},\label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
measured on the exact head inputs behind the same verified draft token. In the Euclidean family, with 64-row tiles of contiguous token indices and $\mu_c$ the tile mean, transport's envelope for row $i$ is narrower than the per-row int8 self-evidence envelope if and only if $\rho<\delta^\ast_i=\norm{e_i}_2/r^{(2)}_c(i)$. Table~\ref{tab:headconstants} evaluates $\delta^\ast$ on the Qwen3.5-4B head from the weights alone. Its median is 0.85\% against per-row int8 and 15.5\% against per-row int4. For the manuscript's original pairing of an $\ell_\infty$ radius with $\norm\Delta_1$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:crossover}, part~1, with the tile radius measured rather than bounded by the diameter), the corresponding threshold on $\norm\Delta_1/\norm h_1$ has a median of 0.30\%. The comparison is of envelope widths, not of certification rates, candidate counts or runtime; it uses no hidden states, and it ignores the BF16 rounding of the draft logits that transport also has to carry. The measured drift decides this width comparison, not which mechanism is faster. The Hölder condition of Theorem~\ref{thm:crossover} needs $\operatorname{diam}_\infty(c)\ge\norm{w_i}_\infty$, which holds for 99.9\% of rows with 64-row tiles (\nolinkurl{evidence/precision/head_constants.json}). It says that transport can compete with an int8 copy only if draft and target states agree to better than about one part in a hundred at the head input, which a drafter trained to predict tokens has no particular reason to achieve. The static tile screen is weak on this head for a related reason: the 64-row tile radius exceeds the row norm (median $r^{(2)}_c/\norm{w_i}_2=1.14$), so $\langle\mu_c,h\rangle+r^{(2)}_c\norm h_2$ is wider than the plain row bound $\norm{w_i}_2\norm h_2$, and tiles in token order are barely tighter than random ones. A scalar $\ell_\infty$ radius paired with $\norm\Delta_1$ is about four times looser again than the Euclidean pairing for a spread-out $\Delta$ (derived from the same constants, $r_c\approx1.30\norm{w_i}_\infty$ and $\norm\Delta_1\approx0.8\sqrt D\norm\Delta_2$). The enclosures of the two mechanisms can be intersected, so a hybrid is never looser than either; the question is only whether transport's cheaper evidence excludes enough to pay for itself.

\subsection{Capturing the head inputs the engine used}\label{sec:transport-capture}
Transport is a statement about two vectors fed to the same head: the draft input $h^d$ that produced a proposal and the target input $h^t$ that decides it. The serving engine exposes neither for the MTP drafter, so a capture-only patch (\nolinkurl{engine/sglang/patches/0001-head-capture-replay-dumps.patch}) stashes the tensor \code{LogitsProcessor} hands to the head and joins it, inside the same verify step, with the proposed tokens, the target's argmax and the accepted lengths. Hidden vectors are stored as BF16 bit patterns, so the replay reads exactly what the head read. The hooks run in Python between forwards, so the capture server runs without CUDA graphs and without the overlap scheduler; it is a correctness configuration, not a performance one. The prompts are 320 public prompts split by prompt into analysis and held-out halves before any fitting, decoded greedily for 384 new tokens.

The pairing follows the verify layout. A verify step for one request holds $[x_0,d_1,\dots,d_S]$ with $x_0$ the last committed token; draft token $d_k$ came from the draft input $h^d_k$ and is decided by the target input at verify position $k-1$. Each pair is labelled accepted, rejected or unreached, and the target input at the last verify position, which produces the bonus token, has no draft anchor at all: a transport-only verifier still streams the whole head for that row.

The pairing was confirmed by recomputation rather than assumed. For plain decoding, the FP64 argmax of the captured head input equals the engine's token at 99.51\% of 105,269 positions, and all 519 disagreements lie within one BF16 spacing of the FP64 winner (the largest gap is 0.118 logits where the spacing is 0.125). The argmax of the exact logits rounded to BF16, with the first-index rule, matches the engine at 99.998\%; it is not the engine's computation (Section~\ref{sec:headcontracts}), and the remaining positions are where the two differ. The engine's own top two logits were equal in BF16 at 1.0\% of positions, where the stock decision is set by the tie rule. R-real and R-stock therefore differ at about 0.5\% of greedy positions here (\nolinkurl{evidence/head_geometry/alignment_plain4b.json}).

\subsection{The measured drift}\label{sec:transport-measured}
The decisive measurement evaluates $\rho$ (Equation~\eqref{eq:rho}) on aligned held-out pairs, with the certification rates of every transport variant against the static screen at $h^t$ and against self-evidence on the same states. The bound families are those of Section~\ref{sec:geometry} (a scalar $\ell_\infty$ radius, coordinate radii, grouped $\ell_2$ radii with groups of 32 and 128, and low-rank residual radii), each on contiguous, randomly permuted and $k$-means tiles of 64, 256 and 1,024 rows; with $k$-means tiles and an $\ell_2$ radius the static screen is CSV-Decode's. A tile is skipped when its transported upper bound falls below the exactly computed target score of the draft token, and the oracle threshold $\max_iz^t_i$ is reported as a ceiling. Two controls bound what any geometry could achieve: oracle radii, the realized per-tile deviation, which is the tightest radius any certified geometry could have; and a drift-scaling replay, $h^d(s)=h^t-s\Delta$, which scales $\rho$ and every radius term by $s$ and asks how far a drafter would have to move before transport certifies a useful share of decisions. For sampled verification the partition interval $\log(Z^+/Z^-)$ and $P_?$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:guards} are reported at temperatures 1 and 0.7. Head-metric versions of the drift, $\norm{W\Delta}_2/\norm{Wh^t}_2$ after removing each position's mean logit and the share of $\Delta$ in the leading right singular subspaces of $W$, test whether the drift hides in directions the head ignores.
