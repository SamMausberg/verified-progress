\section{Transport and Why It Fails}\label{sec:transport}
\emph{Transport} carries a drafter's tile summaries (Section~\ref{sec:intro}) to the target's head input with a bound on how far each score can move, so the verifier projects only the tiles whose bounds leave its decision open.

\subsection{Transported Tile Certificates}\label{sec:transport-def}
Let $h^d$ and $h^t$ be aligned draft and target head inputs (each the final norm's output) behind the same verified draft token, $\Delta=h^t-h^d$, $z^d_i=\langle w_i,h^d\rangle$ and $z^t_i=\langle w_i,h^t\rangle$. A partition of the vocabulary into tiles $c$ carries, as checkpoint metadata, a centre $\mu_c$ and a radius $r_c\ge\max_{i\in c}\norm{w_i-\mu_c}_2$. The draft pass emits $m^d_c=\max_{i\in c}z^d_i$ and $Z^d_c=\sum_{i\in c}e^{z^d_i}$.

\begin{theorem}[Transported certificates]\label{thm:transport}
In real arithmetic, with $a_c=\langle\mu_c,\Delta\rangle$ and $\epsilon_c=r_c\norm\Delta_2$, every $i\in c$ satisfies $|z^t_i-z^d_i-a_c|\le\epsilon_c$. Hence $\max_{i\in c}z^t_i\le m^d_c+a_c+\epsilon_c=:U_c$ and $e^{a_c-\epsilon_c}Z^d_c\le Z^t_c\le e^{a_c+\epsilon_c}Z^d_c$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$z^t_i-z^d_i-a_c=\langle w_i-\mu_c,\Delta\rangle$, which Cauchy--Schwarz bounds by $\epsilon_c$. Maximizing over $c$ gives the first bound; exponentiating and summing gives the second.
\end{proof}
A greedy verifier may skip tile $c$ when $U_c<\tau$ for an exactly evaluated target score $\tau$, such as the draft token's. Other ways of measuring a tile's radius (\emph{bound families}: Hölder, coordinate, grouped and low-rank) trade metadata for width, and \emph{tilings} group the entries contiguously, at random or by $k$-means (Appendix~\ref{app:transport}).

\subsection{What Transport Must Beat}\label{sec:crossover}\label{sec:geometry}
The int8 copy's error for entry $i$ is $\langle e_i,h^t\rangle$, bounded by $\norm{e_i}_2\norm{h^t}_2$ with $e_i$ known offline; transport's is $\langle w_i-\mu_c,\Delta\rangle$, bounded by $r_c\norm\Delta_2$. Each bound is attained by some admissible vector, so neither can be tightened without more information (Proposition~\ref{prop:suprema}), and the comparison reduces to one ratio, the \emph{drift ratio}
\begin{equation}
\rho=\norm{h^t-h^d}_2/\norm{h^t}_2.\label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Crossover]\label{thm:crossover}
For every tile and centre, $r_c\ge\tfrac12\diam_2(c)$. Transport's bound for entry $i\in c$ is narrower than the int8 copy's, $r_c\norm\Delta_2<\norm{e_i}_2\norm{h^t}_2$, if and only if $\rho<\delta^\ast_i:=\norm{e_i}_2/r_c$.
\end{proposition}

The weights alone fix $\delta^\ast$. On this head, with 64-entry tiles of contiguous token indices and tile-mean centres, its median over all entries is 0.85\% against int8 with one scale per entry \ev{head-constants}: draft and target head inputs must agree to about one part in a hundred, which a drafter trained to predict tokens has no reason to achieve. Transport must also beat the \emph{static screen}, the tile bound $\langle\mu_c,h\rangle+r_c\norm h_2$ at the target's head input with no draft at all. Since tile radii exceed row norms (median ratio 1.14), transport is no tighter than the static screen once the drift is as large as $h$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:static}).

\subsection{The Measured Drift}\label{sec:transport-measured}
The drift is measured on 40,000 held-out \emph{drift pairs} per drafter, all aligned pairs of the held-out prompts; certification (below) runs on the \emph{transport pairs}, the pairs of whole held-out capture steps (4,020 for DFlash-4B, 16,016 for MTP-4B). Its median is 0.917 for DFlash-4B \ev{rho} and 0.954 for MTP-4B \ev{rho-mtp}, two orders of magnitude above the median $\delta^\ast$ (Figure~\ref{fig:rho}b, c).

\paragraph{Why the drift is large} Two properties of the DFlash drafter set its $\rho$ (Figure~\ref{fig:rho}a) \ev{stats-dflash}. Its final RMSNorm has unit gain, so its head input always has norm $\sqrt D\approx50.6$, about a third of the target's median of 159; the triangle inequality alone then gives $\rho\ge0.68$. The angle between the two is large as well: even the best rescaling of $h^d$ leaves $\rho$ near 0.91, and the drafter is trained to rank the right token first under $Wh^d$, not to reproduce the target's head input. Nor does the drift hide in directions the head ignores: with each position's mean logit removed, $\norm{W\Delta}_2/\norm{Wh^t}_2$ has a median of 0.85 \ev{rho}. The MTP layer's head input is slightly longer than the target's, so its drift is mainly in direction \ev{transport-mtp}.
\paragraph{What certification achieves} Over every bound family and tiling tested, and their elementwise minimum (Table~\ref{tab:transport} in Appendix~\ref{app:transport}), the best certified bound lets a greedy verifier skip at most 0.59\% of the vocabulary for DFlash-4B and 0.64\% for MTP-4B, about as much as the static screen, which ignores the draft \ev{transport,transport-mtp}. A variant with a radius for each entry instead of each tile reaches 0.65\% for MTP-4B; it is not in the table. A sampled draft fares no better: the test that decides its acceptance from the transported bounds almost always stays undecided ($P_?$). On the same DFlash-4B verification positions the int8 copy needs about 1.6 candidates per decision (Appendix~\ref{app:candidates}).

\paragraph{Why certification gains so little} Two measured facts explain it. First, the drift is two orders of magnitude above the threshold for certified bounds, which must cover the worst vector in each tile. Second, a verifier that shares weight reads across a batch must read every tile any of its positions needs, which over real verify batches is at least 99.4\% of the vocabulary for every certified family. Even \emph{oracle radii}, the realized per-tile deviations that no certified bound can undercut, would skip most of the vocabulary for a single pair (Table~\ref{tab:transport}) but need 97\% of it for random groups of 16 DFlash-4B pairs (Appendix~\ref{app:transport-sampled}) \ev{transport}. Appendix~\ref{app:transport-scaling} extrapolates, with a model rather than a measured drafter, how small the drift would have to be.
