\section{Transport and Why It Fails}\label{sec:transport}
\emph{Transport} carries a drafter's tile summaries (Section~\ref{sec:intro}) to the target's head input with a bound on how far each score can move, so the verifier projects only the tiles whose bounds leave its decision open.

\subsection{Transported Tile Certificates}\label{sec:transport-def}
Let $h^d$ and $h^t$ be aligned draft and target head inputs (each the final norm's output) behind the same verified draft token, $\Delta=h^t-h^d$, $z^d_i=\langle w_i,h^d\rangle$ and $z^t_i=\langle w_i,h^t\rangle$. A partition of the vocabulary into tiles $c$ carries, as checkpoint metadata, a centre $\mu_c$ and a radius $r_c\ge\max_{i\in c}\norm{w_i-\mu_c}_2$. The draft pass emits $M^d_c=\max_{i\in c}z^d_i$ and $Z^d_c=\sum_{i\in c}e^{z^d_i}$.

\begin{theorem}[Transported certificates]\label{thm:transport}
In real arithmetic, with $a_c=\langle\mu_c,\Delta\rangle$ and $\epsilon_c=r_c\norm\Delta_2$, every $i\in c$ satisfies $|z^t_i-z^d_i-a_c|\le\epsilon_c$. Hence $\max_{i\in c}z^t_i\le M^d_c+a_c+\epsilon_c=:U_c$ and $e^{a_c-\epsilon_c}Z^d_c\le Z^t_c\le e^{a_c+\epsilon_c}Z^d_c$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$z^t_i-z^d_i-a_c=\langle w_i-\mu_c,\Delta\rangle$, which Cauchy--Schwarz bounds by $\epsilon_c$. Maximizing over $c$ gives the first bound; exponentiating and summing gives the second.
\end{proof}
A greedy verifier may skip tile $c$ when $U_c<\tau$ for an exactly evaluated target score $\tau$, such as the draft token's. Hölder, coordinate, grouped and low-rank radii trade metadata for width (Appendix~\ref{app:transport}).

\subsection{What Transport Must Beat}\label{sec:crossover}\label{sec:geometry}
The int8 copy's error for entry $i$ is $\langle e_i,h^t\rangle$, bounded by $\norm{e_i}_2\norm{h^t}_2$ with $e_i$ known offline; transport's is $\langle w_i-\mu_c,\Delta\rangle$, bounded by $r_c\norm\Delta_2$. Each bound is attained by some admissible vector, so each is optimal for the information it uses (Proposition~\ref{prop:suprema}), and the comparison reduces to one ratio, the \emph{drift ratio}
\begin{equation}
\rho=\norm{h^t-h^d}_2/\norm{h^t}_2.\label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Crossover]\label{thm:crossover}
For every tile and centre, $r_c\ge\tfrac12\diam_2(c)$. Transport's bound for entry $i\in c$ is narrower than the int8 copy's, $r_c\norm\Delta_2<\norm{e_i}_2\norm{h^t}_2$, if and only if $\rho<\delta^\ast_i:=\norm{e_i}_2/r_c$.
\end{theorem}

The weights alone fix $\delta^\ast$. On the Qwen3.5-4B head, with 64-entry tiles of contiguous token indices and tile-mean centres, $\delta^\ast$ has a median of 0.85\% over all entries against int8 with one scale per row \ev{head-constants}. Transport can compete with an int8 copy only if draft and target head inputs agree to about one part in a hundred, which a drafter trained to predict tokens has no reason to achieve. The tile radius itself exceeds the norm of a row of $W$ (median $r_c/\norm{w_i}_2=1.14$), so the \emph{static screen}, the tile bound $\langle\mu_c,h\rangle+r_c\norm h_2$ evaluated at the target's head input with no draft, is wider than the plain per-entry bound, and transport is no tighter than the static screen once the drift is as large as $h$ itself (Proposition~\ref{prop:static}).

\subsection{The Measured Drift}\label{sec:transport-measured}
Over 40,000 held-out pairs of each drafter, $\rho$ has a median of 0.917 for the public DFlash drafter at block size 16 \ev{rho} and 0.954 for the MTP layer with four draft steps \ev{rho-mtp}, two orders of magnitude above the median $\delta^\ast$; even the closest DFlash pair has $\rho=0.823$ (Figure~\ref{fig:rho}b). Accepted DFlash drafts are no closer than rejected ones (Appendix~\ref{app:transport-drift}).

\paragraph{Why the drift is large} Two properties of the DFlash drafter set its $\rho$ (Figure~\ref{fig:rho}a) \ev{stats-dflash}. Its final RMSNorm has unit gain, so its head input always has norm $\sqrt{2{,}560}$, a third of the target's, and the triangle inequality alone gives $\rho\ge0.68$; the angle between the two is large as well, so even the best scalar rescaling of $h^d$ would leave $\rho$ near 0.91. The drafter is trained to rank the right token first under $Wh^d$, not to reproduce the target's head input, and its scores are softer (median top-token probability 0.17 against 0.69). Nor does the drift hide in directions the head ignores: after removing each position's mean logit, $\norm{W\Delta}_2/\norm{Wh^t}_2$ has a median of 0.85 \ev{rho}. The MTP layer's head input is slightly longer than the target's (median norms 176 and 160), so its drift is mainly one of direction \ev{transport-mtp}.

\paragraph{What certification achieves} Table~\ref{tab:transport} applies every certified bound family of Appendix~\ref{app:transport}, and their elementwise minimum. For greedy verification the best family and tiling skips at most 0.59\% of the vocabulary on DFlash-4B pairs and 0.64\% on MTP-4B pairs (0.65\% with a radius per entry), no more than the static screen, which ignores the draft \ev{transport,transport-mtp}. Sampled acceptance stays undecided with probability 0.964 or more. On the same DFlash-4B states the int8 copy needs 1.57 candidates per decision (Section~\ref{sec:candidates}).

\paragraph{What limits it} Three measured facts account for the failure; we claim nothing more about its cause. The drift is two orders of magnitude above the threshold for certified bounds, which must cover the worst vector in each tile. The realized per-tile deviations are far smaller: as oracle radii they would skip 68--98.5\% of the vocabulary for a single pair. But a verifier that shares weight reads across a batch must read every tile any of its positions needs, which over real verify batches is at least 99.4\% of the vocabulary for every certified family, and 97\% even with oracle radii for random groups of 16 DFlash-4B pairs (Appendix~\ref{app:transport-sampled}) \ev{transport}. Only an extrapolation, not a measured drafter, shows how far a drafter would have to move: with the measured drift scaled down tenfold, greedy transport would skip 49\% (DFlash-4B) and 80\% (MTP-4B) of the vocabulary (Appendix~\ref{app:transport-scaling}) \ev{transport,transport-mtp}.
