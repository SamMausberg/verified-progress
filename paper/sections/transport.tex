\section{Transport and Why It Fails}\label{sec:transport}
A drafter that projects through the target's head has already computed each vocabulary tile's largest score and probability mass. \emph{Transport} carries those summaries to the target's head input through a Hölder bound, so the verifier projects only tiles whose bounds leave its decision open.

\subsection{Transported Tile Certificates}\label{sec:transport-def}
Let $h^d$ and $h^t$ be aligned draft and target head inputs (each the final norm's output) behind the same verified draft token, $\Delta=h^t-h^d$, $z^d_i=\langle w_i,h^d\rangle$ and $z^t_i=\langle w_i,h^t\rangle$. A partition of the vocabulary into tiles $c$ carries, as checkpoint metadata, a centre $\mu_c$ and a radius $r_c\ge\max_{i\in c}\norm{w_i-\mu_c}_2$. The draft pass emits $M^d_c=\max_{i\in c}z^d_i$ and $Z^d_c=\sum_{i\in c}e^{z^d_i}$.

\begin{theorem}[Transported certificates]\label{thm:transport}
In real arithmetic, with $a_c=\langle\mu_c,\Delta\rangle$ and $\epsilon_c=r_c\norm\Delta_2$, every $i\in c$ satisfies $|z^t_i-z^d_i-a_c|\le\epsilon_c$. Hence $\max_{i\in c}z^t_i\le M^d_c+a_c+\epsilon_c=:U_c$ and $e^{a_c-\epsilon_c}Z^d_c\le Z^t_c\le e^{a_c+\epsilon_c}Z^d_c$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$z^t_i-z^d_i-a_c=\langle w_i-\mu_c,\Delta\rangle$, which Cauchy--Schwarz bounds by $\epsilon_c$. Maximizing over $c$ gives the first bound; exponentiating and summing gives the second.
\end{proof}
Hölder, coordinate, grouped and low-rank radii trade metadata for width (Appendix~\ref{app:transport}). A greedy verifier may skip tile $c$ when $U_c<\tau$ for an exactly evaluated target score $\tau$, such as the draft token's.

\subsection{What Transport Must Beat}\label{sec:crossover}\label{sec:geometry}
Self-evidence's error for row $i$ is $\langle e_i,h^t\rangle$, bounded by $\norm{e_i}_2\norm{h^t}_2$ with $e_i$ known offline; transport's is $\langle w_i-\mu_c,\Delta\rangle$, bounded by $r_c\norm\Delta_2$. Each bound is attained by some admissible vector, so each is optimal for the information it uses (Proposition~\ref{prop:suprema}), and the comparison reduces to one ratio, the \emph{drift ratio}
\begin{equation}
\rho=\norm{h^t-h^d}_2/\norm{h^t}_2.\label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Crossover]\label{thm:crossover}
For every tile and centre, $r_c\ge\tfrac12\diam_2(c)$. Transport's envelope for row $i\in c$ is narrower than self-evidence's, $r_c\norm\Delta_2<\norm{e_i}_2\norm{h^t}_2$, if and only if $\rho<\delta^\ast_i:=\norm{e_i}_2/r_c$.
\end{theorem}

The weights alone fix $\delta^\ast$. On the Qwen3.5-4B head, with 64-row tiles of contiguous token indices and tile-mean centres, $\delta^\ast$ has a median of 0.85\% over all rows (p10 0.67\%, p90 1.15\%) against int8 with one scale per row, 0.61\% against 128-column groups and 15.5\% against int4 \ev{head-constants}. Transport can compete with an int8 copy only if draft and target head inputs agree to about one part in a hundred, which a drafter trained to predict tokens has no reason to achieve. The tile radius itself exceeds the row norm (median $r_c/\norm{w_i}_2=1.14$), so a static tile bound $\langle\mu_c,h\rangle+r_c\norm h_2$ is wider than the plain row bound, and transport is no tighter than that static screen once the drift is as large as $h$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:static}).

\subsection{The Measured Drift}\label{sec:transport-measured}
For the public DFlash drafter at block size 16, over 40,000 held-out pairs, $\rho$ has a median of 0.917 (p10 0.875, p90 0.964, smallest 0.823) \ev{rho}. For the model's own MTP layer with four draft steps, over 40,000 held-out pairs, the median is 0.954 (p10 0.767, p90 1.174) \ev{rho-mtp}; both medians lie two orders of magnitude above that of $\delta^\ast$ (Figure~\ref{fig:rho}b). Accepted DFlash drafts are no closer than rejected ones (medians 0.909 and 0.891). Transport's envelope is the narrower one only on 0.08\% of rows, three tiles of identical unused-token rows with zero radius.

\paragraph{Why the drift is large} Two properties of the DFlash drafter set its $\rho$ \ev{stats-dflash}. Its head input always has norm $50.6=\sqrt{2{,}560}$, because the drafter's final RMSNorm has unit gain, while the target's is about 159; the triangle inequality alone then gives $\rho\ge0.68$. The angle between them is large as well (median cosine 0.41), so even the best scalar rescaling of $h^d$ would leave $\rho$ near 0.91. The drafter is trained to rank the right token first under $Wh^d$, not to reproduce the target's head input, and its logits are softer (median top-token probability 0.17 against 0.69). Nor does the drift hide in directions the head ignores: after removing each position's mean logit, $\norm{W\Delta}_2/\norm{Wh^t}_2$ has a median of 0.85, and the largest realized transport error in a tile is a median 77 times the largest realized int8 error \ev{rho}. The MTP layer's head input is slightly longer than the target's (median norms 176 and 160), so its drift is mainly one of direction \ev{transport-mtp}.

\paragraph{What certification achieves} Table~\ref{tab:transport} applies every certified bound family of Appendix~\ref{app:transport}, and their elementwise minimum, to contiguous, random and $k$-means tiles. For greedy verification the best certified family skips at most 0.59\% of the vocabulary on average on DFlash-4B pairs and 0.64\% on MTP-4B pairs (0.65\% for a row-level variant), no more than the static screen, which does not use the draft at all \ev{transport,transport-mtp}. Oracle radii would skip 68--98.5\% for one pair, but a verifier that reuses weights across a batch reads the union of the tiles its rows need: over the capture's real verify batches every certified family and tiling needs at least 99.48\% (DFlash-4B) and 99.4\% (MTP-4B) of the vocabulary. For sampled acceptance the partition interval spans 133--268 nats at its median, and the test stays undecided with probability 0.964 or more on average.

A drift-scaling replay, $h^d(s)=h^t-s\Delta$, an extrapolation, not a measured drafter, shows how far a drafter would have to move: with the best certified family on $k$-means 256-row tiles, greedy transport skips 49\% (DFlash-4B) and 80\% (MTP-4B) of the vocabulary at $s=0.1$, while sampled acceptance stays undecided with mean probability 1.00 there and 0.66 at $s=0.01$ for DFlash-4B \ev{transport,transport-mtp}.

\paragraph{The verdict} On both drafters and for every bound family and tiling tested, transport skips almost nothing, no more than a static screen that ignores the draft, and the batch union erases the rest. The cause is geometric: the head inputs differ by order one, not one part in a hundred. On the same DFlash-4B states the int8 copy needs 1.57 candidate rows (Section~\ref{sec:rstock-share}).
