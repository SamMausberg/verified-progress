\section{Transport and Why It Fails}\label{sec:transport}
\emph{Transport} carries a drafter's tile summaries to the target's head input, bounding how far each score can move, so the verifier projects only the tiles whose bounds leave its decision open.

\subsection{Transported Tile Certificates}\label{sec:transport-def}
Let $h^d$ and $h^t$ be aligned draft and target head inputs (each the final norm's output) behind the same verified draft token, $\Delta=h^t-h^d$, $z^d_i=\langle w_i,h^d\rangle$ and $z^t_i=\langle w_i,h^t\rangle$. A partition of the vocabulary into tiles $g$ carries, as checkpoint metadata, a centre $\mu_g$ and a radius $r_g\ge\max_{i\in g}\norm{w_i-\mu_g}_2$. The draft pass emits $m^d_g=\max_{i\in g}z^d_i$ and $Z^d_g=\sum_{i\in g}e^{z^d_i}$.

\begin{theorem}[Transported certificates]\label{thm:transport}
In real arithmetic, with $a_g=\langle\mu_g,\Delta\rangle$ and $\epsilon_g=r_g\norm\Delta_2$, every $i\in g$ satisfies $|z^t_i-z^d_i-a_g|\le\epsilon_g$. Hence $\max_{i\in g}z^t_i\le m^d_g+a_g+\epsilon_g=:U_g$ and $e^{a_g-\epsilon_g}Z^d_g\le Z^t_g\le e^{a_g+\epsilon_g}Z^d_g$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$z^t_i-z^d_i-a_g=\langle w_i-\mu_g,\Delta\rangle$, which Cauchy--Schwarz bounds by $\epsilon_g$. Maximizing over $i\in g$ gives the first bound; exponentiating and summing gives the second.
\end{proof}
A greedy verifier may skip tile $g$ when $U_g<\tau$ for an exact target score $\tau$, such as the draft token's. \emph{Bound families} replace the radius by Hölder, coordinate, grouped or low-rank radii, trading metadata for width, and \emph{tilings} group the entries contiguously, at random or by $k$-means (Appendix~\ref{app:transport}).

\subsection{What Transport Must Beat}\label{sec:crossover}\label{sec:geometry}
The int8 copy's error for entry $i$ is $\langle e_i,h^t\rangle$, bounded by $\norm{e_i}_2\norm{h^t}_2$; transport's is $\langle w_i-\mu_g,\Delta\rangle$, bounded by $r_g\norm\Delta_2$. Each bound is attained by some admissible vector, so neither can be tightened without more information (Proposition~\ref{prop:suprema}). Both are a weight-side constant times an input-side norm, so the comparison reduces to one ratio, the \emph{drift}
\begin{equation}
\rho=\norm{h^t-h^d}_2/\norm{h^t}_2.\label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Crossover]\label{thm:crossover}
For every tile and centre, $r_g\ge\tfrac12\diam_2(g)$. Transport's bound for entry $i\in g$ is narrower than the int8 copy's, $r_g\norm\Delta_2<\norm{e_i}_2\norm{h^t}_2$, if and only if $\rho<\delta^\ast_i:=\norm{e_i}_2/r_g$.
\end{proposition}

The weights alone fix $\delta^\ast$. On this head, with 64-entry tiles of contiguous token indices and tile-mean centres, its median over all entries is 0.85\% against int8 with one scale per entry \ev{head-constants}. Draft and target head inputs must therefore agree to about one part in a hundred, which a drafter trained to predict tokens need not achieve. Transport must also beat the \emph{static screen}, the tile bound $\langle\mu_g,h\rangle+r_g\norm h_2$ at the target's head input with no draft, and once the drift reaches 1 it is no tighter (Proposition~\ref{prop:static}).

\subsection{The Measured Drift}\label{sec:transport-measured}
The drift is measured on 40,000 aligned pairs per drafter, drawn at random from the held-out prompts; certification (below) runs on the pairs from whole held-out capture steps (4,020 for DFlash-4B, 16,016 for MTP-4B). Its median is 0.917 for DFlash-4B \ev{rho} and 0.954 for MTP-4B \ev{rho-mtp}, two orders of magnitude above the median $\delta^\ast$ (Figure~\ref{fig:rho}b, c).

\paragraph{Why the drift is large} Two properties of the DFlash drafter set its $\rho$ (Figure~\ref{fig:rho}a) \ev{stats-dflash}. Its final RMSNorm has unit gain, so its head input always has norm $\sqrt D\approx50.6$, about a third of the target's median of 159, which alone gives $\rho\ge0.68$. The angle is large as well: even the best rescaling of $h^d$ leaves $\rho$ near 0.91, since the drafter is trained to rank the right token first under $Wh^d$, not to reproduce $h^t$, and the head's own metric does not hide the drift (Appendix~\ref{app:transport-drift}) \ev{rho}. MTP-4B's head input is slightly longer than the target's, so its drift is mainly in direction \ev{transport-mtp}.
\paragraph{What certification achieves} Over every bound family and tiling tested, and their elementwise minimum (Table~\ref{tab:transport}), the best certified bound lets a greedy verifier skip at most 0.59\% of the vocabulary for DFlash-4B and 0.64\% for MTP-4B (0.65\% bounding each entry from its own draft score, Appendix~\ref{app:transport-families}), about as much as the static screen, which ignores the draft \ev{transport,transport-mtp}, while the int8 copy needs about 1.6 candidates per decision on the same DFlash-4B positions (Appendix~\ref{app:candidates}). A sampled draft fares no better: transported bounds alone leave its acceptance test undecided with mean probability $P_?$ of 0.92--1.00 (Appendix~\ref{app:transport-sampled}).

\paragraph{Why certification gains so little} Two measured facts explain it. First, the drift is far above the threshold, which is low because the int8 copy's error is about 1\% of a row's norm while a tile's radius exceeds that norm (Table~\ref{tab:headconstants}). Second, a verifier sharing weight reads across a batch must read every tile any of its positions needs: over real verify batches, at least 99.4\% of the vocabulary for every certified family. Even \emph{oracle radii}, the realized per-tile deviations no certified bound can undercut, would skip most of the vocabulary for a single pair (Table~\ref{tab:transport}) but need 97\% for random groups of 16 DFlash-4B pairs (Appendix~\ref{app:transport-sampled}) \ev{transport}.
