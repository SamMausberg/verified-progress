\section{What the Engine Computes and What a Certificate Must Match}\label{sec:contract}\label{sec:semantics}
A certificate is a statement about a specified computation, so ``exact'' means little until the computation is named.

\subsection{What the Engine Computes}\label{sec:stock}
Qwen3.5-4B ties its output head to its input embedding: $W\in\BF^{V\times D}$, with $V=248{,}320$ vocabulary entries of width $D=2{,}560$, where $\BF$ denotes the BF16 numbers. Row $w_i$ of $W$ belongs to vocabulary entry $i$. We call one head input, with the token decided from it, a \emph{position}. A decode step or a verification pass sends $M$ positions through the head in one call, and $M$ is the \emph{batch shape}. At the pinned commit SGLang computes the head as \code{torch.matmul} of the BF16 head inputs with $W^\top$: a cuBLAS kernel, chosen by the batch shape, sums each logit's $D$ products in an FP32 \emph{accumulator} and rounds the result to BF16. The logits are widened exactly to FP32, and greedy decoding and greedy verification take \code{torch.argmax}, which returns the smallest index among equal maxima.

\subsection{References for One Decision}\label{sec:headcontracts}
Let $h\in\BF^D$ be a position's head input, $z_i=\langle w_i,h\rangle$ the \emph{real logit} of entry $i$, computed in exact arithmetic over the stored operands, $\hat y_i$ the stock kernel's FP32 accumulator and $L_i=\rnb(\hat y_i)$ its BF16 output, where $\rnb$ rounds to the nearest BF16 value (ties to even) and $\ulpb(x)$ is the BF16 spacing at $|x|$.

\begin{definition}[References for one decision]\label{def:contracts}
For $T>0$ and a noise vector $g\in\R^V$, \Rreal{} returns $\min\{i:z_i=\max_jz_j\}$; \Rbf{} returns $\min\{i:\rnb(z_i)=\max_j\rnb(z_j)\}$; \Rstock{} returns $\min\{i:L_i=\max_jL_j\}$; and \Srace{} returns $\min\{i:z_i/T+g_i=\max_j(z_j/T+g_j)\}$.
\end{definition}
\Rreal{} and \Rbf{} depend on $(W,h)$ alone. \Rstock{} is what an unmodified server returns; the kernel's summation order, and so its token, can depend on the batch shape. \Rbf{} is batch-invariant but still not the engine's result, because the engine rounds its own FP32 sum, which can fall on the other side of a rounding boundary; the exact reference has a three-column example where the two differ \ev{precision}. A certified head therefore targets \Rstock{}; \Srace{} serves sampling (Section~\ref{sec:decisions}).

\begin{lemma}[BF16 separation]\label{lem:bf16gap}
For real $y_a,y_b$, if $y_a-y_b>\ulpb(\max(|y_a|,|y_b|))$ then $\rnb(y_a)>\rnb(y_b)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Gap condition]\label{thm:stock}
Let $|\hat y_i-z_i|\le G_i$ for every $i$, and let $a$ be the \Rreal{} token. If for all $b\ne a$
\begin{align*}z_a-z_b>\delta_{ab}:={}&G_a+G_b\\&+\ulpb\bigl(\max(|z_a|,|z_b|)+\max(G_a,G_b)\bigr),\end{align*}
then \Rstock{} returns $a$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\hat y_a-\hat y_b>\ulpb(\max(|z_a|,|z_b|)+\max(G_a,G_b))\ge\ulpb(\max(|\hat y_a|,|\hat y_b|))$, since $\ulpb$ is nondecreasing in the magnitude; Lemma~\ref{lem:bf16gap} gives $L_a>L_b$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The stock kernel's error} Theorem~\ref{thm:stock} needs a bound $G_i$ on how far the stock kernel's FP32 sum can lie from the real logit. The kernel is closed source, does not expose its accumulator, and no vendor documents its rounding, so $G_i$ is an assumption. We write it as $G_i=\gamma\sum_j|w_{ij}h_j|+\eta$, where the floor $\eta=2^{-113}$ covers underflow and flush to zero of products and partial sums, and take $\gamma$ from one of two named \emph{error models}. The \emph{\modelcons} charges $2D$ roundings of relative size $2^{-23}$ in any order, $\gamma=\gcons$; it covers any summation order, split-$K$ with FP32 partial sums, and adders that truncate. The \emph{\modelhopper} takes Khattak and Mikaitis's measurement-based model of Hopper's BF16 tensor-core instruction, \code{wgmma}~\cite{khattak2026}, and adds an allowance for FP32 split-$K$, $\gamma=\ghop$. Neither is a vendor contract, and neither covers split-$K$ partial sums stored in BF16, which PyTorch permits by default. Every fallback share in this paper names the model it assumes.

\paragraph{A screen needs a margin} The condition must hold against every competitor, including entries that are never recomputed, and a screen knows only intervals $lo_i\le z_i\le hi_i$. An entry that loses in real arithmetic can still tie or win once the stock kernel's error and rounding are applied, so a competitor $j$ may be excluded only when $hi_j<lo_a-\hat\delta_{aj}$, where $\hat\delta_{aj}\ge\delta_{aj}$ replaces $|z|$ by the largest endpoint magnitude, not merely when $hi_j<lo_a$. One margin covers every pair: with $M_{\rm e}$ the largest endpoint magnitude, $\delta_{\max}=2G_{\max}+\ulpb(M_{\rm e}+G_{\max})$ bounds every $\hat\delta_{aj}$, so, given valid intervals, an entry with $hi_j<\max_ilo_i-\delta_{\max}$ can be dropped for good (Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}a).

\subsection{The Interval Form and the Fallback}\label{sec:kernel-fallback}
The gap condition demands a margin of more than one BF16 spacing even when $G$ is negligible, and 1.95\% of captured positions lack it (Section~\ref{sec:rstock-share}). The rounding boundaries can be used exactly instead. Rounding is monotone, so an interval $[\hat y_i^-,\hat y_i^+]$ that contains entry $i$'s accumulator puts $L_i$ in $[L_i^-,L_i^+]=[\rnb(\hat y_i^-),\rnb(\hat y_i^+)]$ (Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}b).

\begin{proposition}[Interval form]\label{prop:stockinterval}
Let $C$ be a set of entries such that every $j\notin C$ has $L_j^+<\max_{i\in C}L_i^-$, and let $k=\min\{i\in C:L_i^-=\max_{j\in C}L_j^-\}$. If $L_j^+<L_k^-$ for every $j\in C$ with $j<k$, and $L_j^+\le L_k^-$ for every $j\in C$ with $j>k$, then \Rstock{} returns $k$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $j<k$ in $C$, $L_j\le L_j^+<L_k^-\le L_k$; for $j>k$ in $C$, $L_j\le L_j^+\le L_k^-\le L_k$, so a later index can at most tie and the first-index rule returns $k$; entries outside $C$ satisfy $L_j<L_k^-\le L_k$.
\end{proof}
Two entries whose logits round to the same BF16 value under every admissible accumulation are thus certified, the tie broken by index; only a tie that the accumulation error could make or unmake stays open.

\paragraph{The contract and the fallback} A certified head claims \Rstock{} at the batch shape it serves: for each position, the token the stock head returns for the same head input in a batch of the same shape. It certifies a position only when Theorem~\ref{thm:stock} or Proposition~\ref{prop:stockinterval} fixes that token for every accumulator value the named error model admits, and sends every other position to a fallback that runs the stock kernel. The \emph{whole-batch fallback} reruns the same \code{torch.matmul} on the whole batch at the same $M$, so cuBLAS selects the same kernel; it needs no error model, but a batch with one undecided position pays for the whole stock head. The \emph{per-position fallback} (the column fallback in the evidence) applies to a position left open only by a near tie among a complete candidate list, and finishes it with one stock \code{matmul} over its gathered candidate entries. It is exact only if a position's BF16 logits do not depend on the other positions of the batch or on which entries are present; on this build they do not, bitwise, at 20 batch sizes from 1 to 256, and a start-up self-test re-checks the deployed shapes \ev{kernel-invariance}. Either fallback is a conditional node of the captured CUDA graph, paid only when it runs.

\subsection{Comparisons at the Same Batch Shape}\label{sec:same-shape}
Stock configurations that ought to be equivalent already disagree, which is why the contract fixes the batch shape. On one server capped at 16 running requests, plain greedy decoding at client concurrency 1 and at 32 emits different tokens in 167 of 320 prompts within 256 tokens, 3.42 divergences per 1,000 compared tokens, although repeating either configuration reproduced every token \ev{state-noise}. MTP speculation diverges from plain decoding at 3.5 to 4.0 per 1,000 even with identical pools, always at an exact tie or within two BF16 spacings \ev{state-pinned}. That is more than the plain floor at a cap of 8 (0.63 per 1,000) mainly because speculation perturbs every prompt, not because a perturbed prompt diverges more often: on that measure no excess is detected, though a modest one is not excluded (Appendix~\ref{sec:serving-equality}) \ev{state-perturb}. Neither the tie rule nor the head GEMM caused any divergence; the runs part at batch-dependent kernels, or, for MTP, at layer~0's recurrence, whose decode and verify kernels differ \ev{state-mech}. A certified head is therefore compared with the stock head at the same batch shape and configuration, and a speculative arm with stock speculative verification, not with plain decoding. Appendix~\ref{app:divergence} names the kernels and their possible causes and gives the repeats and cache-level checks.
