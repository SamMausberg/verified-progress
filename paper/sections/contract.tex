\section{The Stock Head and the Exactness Contract}\label{sec:contract}\label{sec:semantics}
A certificate is a statement about a specified computation, so ``exact'' is incomplete until it names that computation. This section names it for SGLang's output head.

\subsection{What the Engine Computes}\label{sec:stock}
Qwen3.5-4B ties its output head to its input embedding: $W\in\BF^{V\times D}$ with $V=248{,}320$ and $D=2{,}560$, where $\BF$ denotes the BF16 numbers. At the pinned commit SGLang computes the head as \code{torch.matmul} of the BF16 head inputs with $W^\top$, a cuBLAS GEMM that accumulates in FP32, rounds each output to BF16 and chooses its kernel by the batch shape $M$. The logits are widened exactly to FP32, and greedy decoding and greedy verification take \code{torch.argmax}, which returns the smallest maximal index. BF16 keeps eight significant bits, so logits in $[16,32)$ are $0.125$ apart, and two real logits closer than about one spacing can round to the same value, leaving the index to decide. With no top-$k$, top-$p$ or min-$p$ the seeded sampler is already a race: after an FP32 softmax and logarithm it returns $\argmax_i(\log p_i+G_i)$ in FP64, with Gumbel noise $G_i$ computed from a 32-bit hash of (seed, position, token), a \emph{counter-keyed field} that can be recomputed for any token in any order. It exists only with \code{-{}-enable-deterministic-inference}, whose batch-invariant \code{mm} runs, at the pin's defaults on this GPU, DeepGEMM's deterministic BF16 GEMM (one FP32 \code{wgmma} accumulator, no split-$K$, round-to-nearest output); with a seed the stock head is that kernel. \subsection{Four References for One Decision}\label{sec:headcontracts}
Let $h\in\BF^D$ be the head input, $z_i=\langle w_i,h\rangle$ the \emph{real logit} of row $i$ in exact arithmetic, $\hat y_i$ the stock kernel's FP32 accumulator and $L_i=\rnb(\hat y_i)$ its BF16 output, where $\rnb$ rounds to the nearest BF16 value (ties to even) and $\ulpb(x)$ is the BF16 spacing at $|x|$.

\begin{definition}[References for one decision]\label{def:contracts}
For $T>0$ and a noise vector $g\in\R^V$, \Rreal{} returns $\min\{i:z_i=\max_jz_j\}$; \Rbf{} returns $\min\{i:\rnb(z_i)=\max_j\rnb(z_j)\}$; \Rstock{} returns $\min\{i:L_i=\max_jL_j\}$; and \Srace{} returns $\min\{i:z_i/T+g_i=\max_j(z_j/T+g_j)\}$.
\end{definition}
\Rreal{} and \Rbf{} depend on $(W,h)$ alone; \Rstock{} is what an unmodified server returns, and it can depend on the reduction order and so on the batch shape. The \Rreal{} token differs from the engine's at about 0.5\% of captured greedy positions (Section~\ref{sec:setup}). \Rbf{} is batch-invariant but not the engine's computation either, because the stock kernel rounds its own FP32 accumulation, which can land on the other side of a BF16 boundary; the exact reference has a three-column witness where the two choose different tokens \ev{precision}.

\begin{lemma}[BF16 separation]\label{lem:bf16gap}
For real $y_a,y_b$, if $y_a-y_b>\ulpb(\max(|y_a|,|y_b|))$ then $\rnb(y_a)>\rnb(y_b)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Gap condition]\label{thm:stock}
Let $|\hat y_i-z_i|\le G_i$ for every $i$, and let $a$ be the \Rreal{} token. If for all $b\ne a$
\begin{align*}z_a-z_b>\delta_{ab}:={}&G_a+G_b\\&+\ulpb\bigl(\max(|z_a|,|z_b|)+\max(G_a,G_b)\bigr),\end{align*}
then \Rstock{} returns $a$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\hat y_a-\hat y_b>\ulpb(\max(|z_a|,|z_b|)+\max(G_a,G_b))\ge\ulpb(\max(|\hat y_a|,|\hat y_b|))$, since $\ulpb$ is nondecreasing in the magnitude; Lemma~\ref{lem:bf16gap} gives $L_a>L_b$.
\end{proof}

Everything rests on $G_i$, a bound on a closed-source kernel's accumulation error, written $G_i=\gamma\sum_j|w_{ij}h_j|+\eta$, where the floor $\eta=2^{-113}$ covers underflow and flush to zero of products and partial sums (at most $2D\cdot2^{-126}(1+\gamma)$), under two named models of $\gamma$. The \emph{\modelcons} charges $2D$ roundings of relative size $2^{-23}$ in any order, $\gamma=\gcons$; it covers any reduction tree, split-$K$ with FP32 partials, and truncating adders. The \emph{\modelhopper} takes Khattak and Mikaitis's measurement-based model of Hopper's BF16 \code{wgmma}~\cite{khattak2026} and adds an FP32 split-$K$ allowance, $\gamma=\ghop$. Neither is a vendor contract, and neither covers split-$K$ partials stored in BF16, which PyTorch permits by default.

The margin must hold against every competitor, including rows never re-scored. With enclosures $lo_i\le z_i\le hi_i$, a competitor $j$ may be excluded only when $hi_j<lo_a-\hat\delta_{aj}$, where $\hat\delta_{aj}\ge\delta_{aj}$ replaces $|z|$ by the largest endpoint magnitude, not merely when $hi_j<lo_a$. Over all rows, $\delta_{\max}=2G_{\max}+\ulpb(M_{\rm e}+G_{\max})$, with $M_{\rm e}$ the largest endpoint magnitude, bounds every $\hat\delta_{aj}$, so, given valid enclosures, a row with $hi_j<\max_ilo_i-\delta_{\max}$ can be dropped for good (Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}a).

\subsection{The Interval Form and the Fallback}\label{sec:kernel-fallback}
The gap condition demands a margin above one BF16 spacing even when $G$ is negligible, which 1.95\% of captured positions lack (Section~\ref{sec:rstock-share}). The rounding boundaries can be used exactly instead. Rounding is monotone, so an enclosure $[\hat y_i^-,\hat y_i^+]$ of row $i$'s accumulator puts $L_i$ in $[L_i^-,L_i^+]=[\rnb(\hat y_i^-),\rnb(\hat y_i^+)]$ (Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}b).

\begin{proposition}[Interval form]\label{prop:stockinterval}
Let $C$ be a set of rows such that every $j\notin C$ has $L_j^+<\max_{i\in C}L_i^-$, and let $k=\min\{i\in C:L_i^-=\max_{j\in C}L_j^-\}$. If $L_j^+<L_k^-$ for every $j\in C$ with $j<k$, and $L_j^+\le L_k^-$ for every $j\in C$ with $j>k$, then \Rstock{} returns $k$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $j<k$ in $C$, $L_j\le L_j^+<L_k^-\le L_k$; for $j>k$ in $C$, $L_j\le L_j^+\le L_k^-\le L_k$, so a later index can at most tie and the first-index rule returns $k$; rows outside $C$ satisfy $L_j<L_k^-\le L_k$.
\end{proof}
Two rows whose logits round to the same BF16 value under every admissible accumulation are thus certified, the tie broken by index; only a tie the accumulation error could make or unmake stays open.

\paragraph{The contract} A certified head claims \Rstock{} at the batch shape it serves: for each row, the token the stock head returns for the same head input in a batch of the same shape. It certifies a row only when Theorem~\ref{thm:stock} or Proposition~\ref{prop:stockinterval} fixes that token for every accumulator value the named error model admits. Every other row runs the stock kernel. The assumption-free fallback reruns the same \code{torch.matmul} on the whole batch at the same $M$, so cuBLAS selects the same kernel; it costs time, never agreement, but a batch with one undecided row pays for the whole stock head. A row-wise mode finishes an undecided row with one stock \code{matmul} over its gathered candidate rows, which is exact only if a row's BF16 logits do not depend on the rest of the batch or on which head rows are present \pend{kernel-invariance}. Either fallback is a conditional node of the captured CUDA graph, paid only when it runs.

\subsection{Comparisons at the Same Batch Shape}\label{sec:same-shape}
Stock configurations that ought to be equivalent already disagree. On one server, plain greedy decoding at client concurrency 1 and 32 (decode batches of up to 16) emits different tokens in 167 of 320 prompts within 256 tokens, 3.42 divergences per 1,000 compared tokens, while repeating either configuration there reproduced every token \ev{state-noise}. With the prefix cache on, a repeat is not an equality reference in general: with smaller, pinned pools and a cap of 8 running requests instead of 16, a same-server repeat at concurrency 1 differs in 17 of 320 prompts (0.25 per 1,000) and a fresh-server repeat at concurrency 32 in 4, all at exact ties or within one BF16 spacing; the working explanation, not checked at the level of the cache, is history dependence through the prefix cache (Appendix~\ref{app:divergence}) \ev{state-pinned}. A bit-exact tap of every module output, each pair on two servers with different pools, finds the runs parting at batch-dependent kernels (a gated RMSNorm, a batched prefill GEMM, and decode attention, for which the pools and the prefix cache's history are possible causes too; Appendix~\ref{app:divergence}), and plain decoding and MTP speculation parting at layer~0's recurrence, whose decode and verify kernels differ; the tie rule and the head GEMM caused none of the divergences \ev{state-mech}. For plain decoding against MTP, every cache entering every forward agrees up to that recurrence on 40 prompts rerun with cache hashes \ev{state-cache}; for the concurrency pair, whether the caches agree is \pend{state-mech}. A certified head is therefore compared with the stock head at the same batch shape and configuration, and a speculative arm with stock speculative verification, not with plain decoding, from which MTP speculation diverges at 3.5 to 4.0 per 1,000 compared tokens even with identical pools, always at an exact tie or within two BF16 spacings \ev{state-pinned}.
