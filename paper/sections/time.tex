\section{Where the time goes}\label{sec:time}
A component that occupies a fraction $f$ of a step can speed the step up by at most $1/(1-f)$, so the head's share of the time bounds what any head certificate can deliver, and the other shares say where the rest of the stack leaves room. This section measures those shares for SGLang serving Qwen3.5-4B in its production configuration: CUDA graphs for prefill and decode, the overlap scheduler, FlashInfer attention and greedy decoding, at batch sizes from 1 to 128 for plain decoding and from 1 to 32 for MTP speculation.

Decode on this GPU is dominated by streaming bytes, so the byte counts of Table~\ref{tab:bytes} give a first picture before any trace. Figure~\ref{fig:bytes-derived} draws the derived bytes of one plain decode step against the batch size at a fixed context. The weights are streamed once whatever the batch; the recurrent state and the attention cache are per request. With FP32 GDN state that the decode kernel reads and writes once per step, the per-request term equals the weights near a batch of 70 at a 512-token context and dominates beyond it. The head's share of the bytes falls accordingly, from 15\% at batch one.
