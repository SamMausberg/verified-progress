\section{Where prior work already reaches}\label{sec:prior}
A source audit should remove false novelty before experiments make it expensive to abandon. This section is organized by the mechanism each body of work shares with ours, and it ends with the narrow claim that remains. Descriptions come from the literature review that accompanies this revision (\code{sources/literature\_review.md}); performance figures quoted from other work are the authors' own and were not reproduced.

\subsection{The head as a cost}
For a small model with a large vocabulary the output head is a large part of each step. FR-Spec profiles EAGLE-2 drafting on Llama-3-8B, with a 128k vocabulary, and finds the head projection at 49\% of draft time and the softmax over its output at a further 13\%~\cite{frspec}; a trimmed-vocabulary study finds the head at 64\% of an EAGLE-3-style drafter's FLOPs~\cite{benshoham2026}. Vocabulary sizes have grown with compute budgets~\cite{tao2024vocab}, which is why 4B models now ship 250k-row heads. Table~\ref{tab:bytes} gives the corresponding byte shares for Qwen3.5-4B; Section~\ref{sec:time} measures the time shares.

\subsection{Screening, exact search and bounds}\label{sec:prior-screening}
Computing cheap scores for every row, bounding the error, and re-computing only the rows that could still matter is an old idea in similarity search. The VA-file filters with quantized cell approximations and refines survivors exactly~\cite{vafile}; FEXIPRO combines an SVD transform with scaled-integer upper bounds and exact floating-point refinement~\cite{fexipro}; LEMP prunes norm buckets with length and Cauchy--Schwarz bounds~\cite{lemp}; ball and cone trees bound $\max\langle q,p\rangle$ by $\langle q,\mu\rangle+R\norm q$~\cite{ramgray2012}. These methods are exact in real arithmetic. A warning comes with them: Maximus and Optimus found that blocked dense matrix multiplication often beats such indices end to end~\cite{maximus}, so a pruning method must beat a tuned dense kernel, not a count of floating-point operations.

In language models, SVD-Softmax computes preview logits from leading singular directions and recomputes a fixed top set in full~\cite{svdsoftmax}, and HiRE uses an int4 or low-rank copy of the head to choose a fixed top-$k'$ set that is recomputed in BF16~\cite{hire}. Both have the two-pass shape of self-evidence without a certificate: a fixed candidate count carries no guarantee that the true winner is among the candidates. CSV-Decode clusters the head's rows offline and bounds each cluster by $\langle\mu_c,h\rangle+R_c\norm h$, opening clusters until the top-$k$ is certified~\cite{csvdecode}. That is exactly the static tile screen that earlier revisions of this manuscript proposed as an ablation, and it is exact in real arithmetic; its text does not treat floating-point rounding. A public experiment on a Qwen3.8 head of the same vocabulary found that centroid-radius bounds prune almost nothing there~\cite{e142}. The transport bound differs from these screens only in where it starts: from a score already measured at the draft state, bounding the change $\langle w_i-\mu_c,\Delta\rangle$ rather than the score itself. Bounding an inner product under a bounded displacement of the query is also known, for example in sequential screening rules for the lasso~\cite{dppscreening} and in cached retrieval with drifting queries~\cite{frieder2024}.

\subsection{Certified low-precision heads}\label{sec:prior-certified}
The greedy half of the self-evidence mechanism is implemented publicly, several times over.
\begin{itemize}
\item sparkpipe keeps an E4M3 shadow of a BF16 head in 32-element groups, attaches a per-group certificate that includes $\gamma_n$ floating-point terms for CUDA-core FMA accumulation, and re-scores survivors exactly in BF16. It routes the decode, last prefill and MTP argmax rows of Qwen3.8-27B (a 248,320-row head) through this path and reports bit-exact tokens and scores~\cite{sparkpipehead}. Sampled requests take the full head read.
\item dgpp stores an int8 head as per-row bit planes, bounds every logit from the high planes, and re-reads only surviving rows in the production accumulation order, for MTP draft steps and greedy verification~\cite{dgpphead}. Its reference is the quantized head itself, and its floating-point term is a fixed relative slack.
\item A Metal implementation for Apple silicon screens with an MXFP8 coarse head whose bound covers quantization and both kernels' rounding, and re-scores candidates with a textual replica of the stock BF16 kernel so that rows are bit-identical~\cite{lagunaprune}.
\item knlp certifies greedy decoding with an int8 shadow in a PCA basis and a Cauchy--Schwarz bound on the out-of-basis part; its own table reports an argmax match of 0.998 at 14B, so floating point is not fully handled~\cite{knlphead}.
\end{itemize}
In retrieval, EAHR bounds int8 inner products by $\norm{e_q}\norm v+\norm{\hat q}\norm{e_v}$ with a floating-point guard and rechecks ambiguous items in FP32~\cite{eahr}. Du et al.\ show why the reference must be named: BF16 and FP16 greedy decoding diverge on 49--100\% of prompts in their experiments, a flip is decided at the head by the top-two margin, and recomputing in FP32 when the margin is small (a heuristic threshold, not a certificate) repairs much of it~\cite{precisioninvariant}. Measured head sensitivity sets expectations for re-scoring: a row-wise int8 copy of the Qwen3-8B head agrees with the BF16 argmax on 97.56\% of positions~\cite{archead}, so an exact re-score must catch the rest.

\subsection{Exact sampling, fused sampling and determinism}\label{sec:prior-sampling}
The Gumbel-max identity~\cite{gumbel1954,papandreou2011} makes a categorical sample the argmax of perturbed scores, and its top-$k$ form samples without replacement~\cite{kool2019}. A* Sampling uses bounds to instantiate only the relevant perturbations and still returns an exact sample~\cite{astar}. Mussmann, Levy and Ermon draw Gumbels only for a retrieved top set, bound every other score by the smallest retrieved one, and lazily instantiate the few tail perturbations that could win; with an approximate top set they widen the cutoff by the approximation error~\cite{mussmann2017,mussmann2016}. That is the direct precedent for exact Gumbel-max sampling from bounded scores. The version used here differs in two engineering respects: a dense low-precision pass bounds every row instead of a sublinear index, and the noise is a fixed field keyed by token, so the bounded race returns the same token as a dense race with the same field, not only a sample with the right law.

FlashSampling fuses Gumbel-max sampling into the head projection, keeping one perturbed maximizer per row and tile so logits never reach memory; it computes every tile, accumulates in FP32 and is exact in distribution~\cite{flashsampling}. Its noise offset depends on the tiling, so a token's noise cannot be recomputed on its own. FlashInfer samples top-$k$, top-$p$ and min-$p$ without sorting and implements chain speculative sampling~\cite{flashinfer,flashinfersamplingblog}. SonicSampler fuses the post-logit sampling and verification kernels over a bounded top-128 pool and describes top-$p$ under that pool as theoretically lossy~\cite{sonic}. On determinism, batch-invariant kernels make temperature-0 outputs reproducible across batch compositions~\cite{he2025nondeterminism}; SGLang's deterministic mode replaces the default matrix multiplication with a persistent Triton kernel and forces the PyTorch sampler~\cite{sglangdeterministic}, which changes the kernel a certificate would have to reproduce. Floating-point nondeterminism in inference has also been characterized and partly mitigated~\cite{yuan2025nondeterminism}.

\subsection{Speculative decoding and verification policies}\label{sec:prior-spec}
Speculative sampling accepts a proposal $x\sim q$ with probability $\min(1,p_x/q_x)$ and otherwise samples the normalized residual $(p-q)_+$; the output has the target law~\cite{specdecode,chen2023specsampling}, and the acceptance probability $\sum_i\min(p_i,q_i)$ is that of a maximal coupling. SpecTr and block verification extend the rule to several drafts and to whole blocks~\cite{spectr,blockverify}. Drafter-invariant speculative decoding couples draft and target through shared Gumbel noise, so the committed token is $\argmax_i(\log p_i+G_i)$ whatever the drafter proposes~\cite{daliri2025}, and the construction extends to several drafts~\cite{rowan2025}. Under that coupling sampled verification is an argmax, which is why it matters here (Section~\ref{sec:fixednoise}).

Drafters that project through a head pay for it at every draft step: Medusa and the EAGLE family~\cite{medusa,eagle,eagle2,eagle3}, native multi-token prediction~\cite{gloeckle2024,deepseekv3}, and DFlash's block-diffusion drafter~\cite{dflashpaper}, whose public 4B checkpoint drafts through the target's head~\cite{dflash4bcard}. DFlash~2 adds a context-gated bilinear selector over candidate lattices~\cite{dflash,dflashcard}; LiLiCorr reranks DFlash candidates and finds that an exact best-path search over a surrogate score decodes worse than the greedy rule~\cite{lilicorr}. The model card of the 4B DFlash drafter reports, on one B200 with greedy decoding, 3.40--4.60$\times$ over autoregressive decoding at concurrency~1 with blocks of 16 and 2.15--2.61$\times$ at concurrency~32 with blocks of 8, against at most 2.31$\times$ and 1.80$\times$ for the MTP layer~\cite{dflash4bcard}. These are the authors' numbers on different hardware; Section~\ref{sec:drafting} measures the drafter here.

Verification-depth policies choose how many drafted tokens to verify. OPT-Tree searches for the tree that maximizes expected acceptance~\cite{opttree}; Sequoia sizes trees for the hardware~\cite{sequoia}; SpecDec++ stops drafting with a learned acceptance head~\cite{specdecpp}. DSpark schedules verification from confidences and a profiled throughput curve~\cite{dspark}, D-cut prunes draft blocks against a profiled cost table aligned with the engine's CUDA-graph shapes~\cite{dcut}, and an open SGLang pull request ports the DSpark scheduler to DFlash~2 with greedy benchmarks~\cite{pr36136}. For sampled decoding, DSpark states that lossless speculation requires admission decisions that do not depend on future candidate tokens, with a counterexample, and D-cut gives a two-token counterexample and a shifted-confidence repair~\cite{dspark,dcut}. The counterexample in Section~\ref{sec:stopping} illustrates their condition; it is not a contribution of this paper.

\subsection{Draft-vocabulary reduction}
FR-Spec restricts the draft softmax to a frequency-ranked subset of the head~\cite{frspec}, VocabTrim trims by token counts on target-generated text~\cite{vocabtrim}, SpecVocab selects a per-step subset with low-rank scores and computes exact draft logits on it~\cite{specvocab}, DynaSpec routes contexts to clusters of rows~\cite{dynaspec}, and NanoSpec (titled MicroSpec in its first version) builds a small in-context vocabulary without training~\cite{microspec}. FastMTP compresses only the draft vocabulary and keeps full-vocabulary verification~\cite{fastmtp}. All of these change only the drafter, so the target's decision is unchanged when the verifier uses the drafter's actual proposal law, or when verification is target-only. They trade acceptance for draft cost; a certified head returns the full-vocabulary draft token while reading fewer bytes per row, and it also applies on the target side, where truncation does not.

\subsection{Hybrid recurrent state under speculation}
Gated DeltaNet's recurrence~\cite{gdn,deltanet} makes rollback after a rejection a state problem. The Mamba-in-Llama work advances one cached state lazily and recomputes after a rejection~\cite{mambainllama}; STree, TreeWY, Bole, GDN Tree-Scan and SpecLA verify trees or chains on SSM and GDN hybrids~\cite{stree,treewy,bole,gdntreescan,specla}, and TreeWY says explicitly that its outputs are not bit-identical to the baseline. ReplaySSM caches recent inputs instead of writing the state at every step, equivalent to ordinary decoding up to floating-point error~\cite{replayssm}; SGLang has its own replay fold behind flags that are off by default~\cite{gdncode}. State precision matters for quality: DAMP measures FP16 recurrent state as nearly lossless and BF16 as several points worse on a reasoning benchmark for another GDN model~\cite{damp}, and LeapQuant re-quantizes the state once per 16-token window~\cite{leapquant}.

We do not claim norm or quantization bounds, bound-then-refine search, certified greedy screening of an LM head, top-$k$ merging, Gumbel-max sampling from bounded scores, drafter-invariant verification, the non-anticipating condition for adaptive depth, function scans, recurrent replay or graph-aware batching. Table~\ref{tab:prior} lists the closest work and the question left for this programme. What remains, as far as the review found as of 30 September 2026 with the coverage limits it records, is:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a floating-point envelope for a low-precision head pass that is sound for the accumulation the GPU actually performs (NVIDIA tensor cores align addends to the largest term and truncate~\cite{fasi2021}, and Khattak and Mikaitis model Hopper's BF16 and FP8 paths in detail~\cite{khattak2026}; existing certified heads use CUDA-core FMA, replay the production order, or assume FP32 is exact);
\item an exactness contract against the stock kernel at the same batch shape, with a stated gap condition and fallback;
\item extension of the certified head from greedy selection to exact sampling under a fixed counter-keyed noise field (pathwise equal to the dense race) and to fixed-noise verification with the engine's own seeded noise, which keeps seeded outputs token-identical to non-speculative decoding; partition brackets for standard acceptance are analysed but not built;
\item a measurement inside SGLang on HBM3 with CUDA graphs, applied to draft steps, verification and plain decoding, against the stock head chain and FlashSampling;
\item for transport, the only mechanism not found in prior work (draft-side tile summaries carried through a shared head to prune the verifier's head), a measurement of whether it survives real geometry.
\end{enumerate}
Items 1--3 are modest extensions of known principles; their value depends on measurement. The contribution is mostly soundness, integration and measurement, and a well-explained negative result would also be informative.
