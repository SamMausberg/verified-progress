\section{The certified head: assumptions, proofs and checks}\label{app:proofs}\label{app:derivations}
This appendix collects what the main text's contract and certified head rest on. It lists the numerical assumptions in full (Appendix~\ref{app:assumptions}) and proves the results of Sections~\ref{sec:headcontracts} and~\ref{sec:selfevidence} whose proofs are not in the main text. It also states and proves the general form of the copy's error bound (Appendix~\ref{app:envelope}) and the refinement ladder (Appendix~\ref{app:ladder}), with the secondary results they need: the constants of the two error models, the integer pass, and the computed norms and outward rounding that turn a real error bound into an interval of computed values. It then bounds and measures how many vocabulary entries a decision re-scores (Appendix~\ref{app:candidates}), says how the GPU kernel is checked (Appendix~\ref{app:kernel-checks}), and separates what is machine-checked from what is tested and what is proved only on paper (Appendix~\ref{app:checked}).

\subsection{The BF16 separation lemma and the gap condition}\label{app:bf16gap}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:bf16gap}]
Rounding is nondecreasing, so if $\rnb(y_a)\le\rnb(y_b)$ while $y_a>y_b$, both values round to the same $v$ and lie in its preimage. When $|v|$ is not a power of two, the preimage has length $\ulpb(v)$ and both values lie in the binade of $v$. When $|v|=2^e$ the preimage is $[v-\ulpb(v)/4,\,v+\ulpb(v)/2]$ for $v>0$ (symmetrically for $v<0$): two values on one side of $v$ are within half the spacing of their binade, and two values on both sides are within $\tfrac34\ulpb(v)\le\ulpb(y_a)$. At $v=0$ the preimage has the subnormal spacing. In every case $y_a-y_b\le\ulpb(\max(|y_a|,|y_b|))$, contradicting the hypothesis.
\end{proof}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:stock}]
For every $b\ne a$, $\hat y_a-\hat y_b\ge z_a-z_b-G_a-G_b>\ulpb(\max(|z_a|,|z_b|)+\max(G_a,G_b))\ge\ulpb(\max(|\hat y_a|,|\hat y_b|))$, because $|\hat y_i|\le|z_i|+G_i$ and $\ulpb$ is nondecreasing in the magnitude. Lemma~\ref{lem:bf16gap} gives $L_a>L_b$, so $a$ is the only maximal index and \Rstock{} returns $a$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Accumulation trees and the constants of the error models}\label{app:accumulation}
The bound below covers every accumulation order the error models need; Section~\ref{sec:envelope} applies it to the low-precision pass.
\begin{lemma}[Accumulation trees]\label{lem:tree}
Let $s=\sum_\ell p_\ell$ be evaluated by a tree whose internal nodes $v$ return $\hat s_v$ with $|\hat s_v-\sum_c\hat s_c|\le u_v\sum_c|\hat s_c|+\eta_v$ over their children $c$. With $d$ the largest number of internal nodes on a leaf-to-root path, $N$ the number of internal nodes, $u\ge\max u_v$, $\eta\ge\max\eta_v$ and $du<1$: $|\hat s-s|\le\gamma_d\sum_\ell|p_\ell|+N\eta(1+\gamma_d)$, where $\gamma_d=(1+u)^d-1\le du/(1-du)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:tree}]
For a node $v$ and a leaf $\ell$ below it, let $P_{v\ell}=\prod(1+u_x)$ over the internal nodes $x$ on the path from $\ell$ up to $v$, and for an internal node $w$ below $v$ let $P'_{vw}$ be the same product over the nodes strictly above $w$ up to $v$, with $P'_{vv}=1$. By induction on height,
\[|\hat s_v|\le\sum_\ell|p_\ell|P_{v\ell}+\eta\sum_wP'_{vw},\qquad|\hat s_v-s_v|\le\sum_\ell|p_\ell|(P_{v\ell}-1)+\eta\sum_wP'_{vw}.\]
Leaves satisfy both with equality. For an internal node, $|\hat s_v|\le(1+u_v)\sum_c|\hat s_c|+\eta$ and $|\hat s_v-s_v|\le\sum_c|\hat s_c-s_c|+u_v\sum_c|\hat s_c|+\eta$; substituting the hypotheses for the children multiplies every $P$ and $P'$ by $(1+u_v)$ and adds $P'_{vv}=1$. Finally $P_{v\ell}\le(1+u)^d$, $P'_{vw}\le(1+u)^{d-1}$, and there are $N$ internal nodes.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Depths and node units} The tree's shape is all that distinguishes CUDA-core, tensor-core and split-$K$ accumulation. An IEEE rounded addition has $u_v=u$, since $|a+b|\le|a|+|b|$~\cite{higham2002}. Sequential accumulation from zero has depth $d=n$, pairwise summation $\lceil\log_2n\rceil$, blocks of $B$ summed as a tree and then accumulated $\lceil n/B\rceil+\lceil\log_2B\rceil$, and split-$K$ adds the number of partials. A multi-term adder that aligns $k+1$ addends to the largest, truncates each to $F$ fractional bits below its leading bit, adds exactly and rounds once has $u_v=(k+1)2^{-F}+2^{1-p}$ with truncation, and the accumulation path has $\lceil n/k\rceil$ such nodes. Tensor cores behave this way rather than as IEEE adders~\cite{fasi2021}. A node that multiplies its only child by a constant and rounds adds one level of depth.

\paragraph{The constants used in this paper} The stock kernel's constant is $\gamma_{\rm stock}$, set by one of the two models below; the int8 pass's own accumulation is bounded by $\gamma_{\rm copy}$, the conservative constant, whichever model is assumed for the stock kernel. Both models of the stock kernel add the absolute floor $\eta=2^{-113}$, which covers underflow and flush to zero of products and partial sums, at most $2D\cdot2^{-126}(1+\gamma_{\rm stock})$. Each model below except the IEEE one is an assumption about hardware that no vendor documents; Table~\ref{tab:assumptions-full} lists where each is used. The values are for $D=2560$.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The \modelcons{}}, $\gamma_{\rm stock}=\gamma_{\rm copy}=\gcons$. At most $2D$ roundings, each of relative size $2^{-23}$ of the magnitudes it combines, in any order: $\gamma_{2D}(2^{-23})=6.107\times10^{-4}$. It covers any reduction tree, split-$K$ with FP32 partials, and multi-term adders over blocks of 8 to 32 terms that keep at least 23 fractional bits and round to nearest or truncate; with the adder unit $u_v$ above, 22 fractional bits are not covered. The committed replay analyses use $6.11\times10^{-4}$, the largest value over the exact reference's fused-adder models with blocks of 2 to 64 products, rounding to nearest or toward zero, plus one scale multiply~\ev{selfevidence,rstock}; the two agree to the precision the paper quotes.
\item \emph{The \modelhopper{}}, $\gamma_{\rm stock}=\ghop$. \citet{khattak2026} model Hopper's BF16 tensor cores with FP32 output, from measurements of the warp-level \code{mma} path (SASS \code{HMMA}), as a $k=16$ adder with $F=25$ and truncation, so $u_v=17\cdot2^{-25}+2^{-23}=6.26\times10^{-7}$ over $D/16=160$ nodes, and $(1+u_v)^{160}-1\approx1.001\times10^{-4}$, which the \modelcons{} covers about sixfold for any reduction order. The two stock head kernels whose SASS could be read ($M=1$ and 32) issue the warpgroup \code{wgmma} instead (only \code{HGMMA}); the other twelve could not be read statically. On crafted inputs whose exact sums are known, Triton's \code{wgmma} and all fourteen stock head kernels accumulate the same way: blocks of 16 products with the FP32 accumulator inside the aligned sum, 25 fractional bits below the largest addend, dropped bits truncated toward zero, and the sum truncated to FP32. Inputs off the rounding ties rule out rounding to nearest, and the stock kernels' BF16 epilogue rounds to nearest even \ev{profile-wgmma}. The operands were powers of two and short sums of them, with one operand of each product equal to 1, on one GH200 (driver 570.195.03, CUDA 13.0, PyTorch 2.13's cuBLAS); full-significand products, subnormals, offsets beyond 128 terms and other GPUs or libraries were not probed, so this is a measurement on probes, not a vendor contract. An allowance of up to 160 further truncating FP32 additions for split-$K$ partials gives $(1+u_v)^{160}(1+2^{-23})^{160}-1=1.1922\times10^{-4}$, which the paper quotes rounded up as $\ghop$. The replays of the head and of the stock divergences compute this value in exact rational arithmetic and round it up to the next double~\ev{rstock,state-mech}.
\item \emph{FP8 tensor cores.} The same published model gives FP8 \code{wgmma} only 13 fractional bits in 32-term blocks, consistent with the accumulation limits reported for FP8 training~\cite{deepseekv3}; an FP8 tensor-core pass needs periodic promotion to FP32 and its own analysis, and none is used here.
\item \emph{IEEE FP32 on CUDA cores.} A balanced tree over blocks of 128 products, a sequential sum across the 20 blocks and one scale multiply give depth 28 with $u=2^{-24}$, so $\gamma=1.67\times10^{-6}$. This model does not describe the stock tensor-core GEMM; the paper uses it only as a sensitivity value of $\gamma_{\rm stock}$ (Table~\ref{tab:rstock}).
\end{itemize}
Since the Hopper model is a published measurement-based model and not a documented contract, it rests on those measurements and on the crafted-input probes above~\ev{profile-wgmma}; the exact reference of Appendix~\ref{app:checked} checks the bounds and decisions against exact arithmetic only under the conservative model's adders~\ev{precision}. On 2,048 captured head inputs, the FP32-output form of the stock head GEMM (the call \code{-{}-enable-fp32-lm-head} makes) showed a largest error of $2.07\times10^{-6}$ of $\sum_j|w_{ij}h_j|$, 295 times inside the \modelcons{} and 58 times inside the \modelhopper{}; the BF16-output kernel that SGLang runs does not expose its FP32 accumulator, so for it the model remains an assumption~\ev{kernel-error}.

\subsection{The error bound of the low-precision copy}\label{app:envelope}
Theorem~\ref{thm:envelope} states the error bound for the deployed copy, int8 codes with one scale per entry. The general form below also covers group scales, columns kept exact, blockwise bounds on the quantization error and any summation tree of Lemma~\ref{lem:tree}; the copies with column groups in Table~\ref{tab:candidates} use it.
\begin{theorem}[Error bound of the copy, general form]\label{thm:envelope-general}
Let row $i$ of the copy have group scales $s_{ig}$, integer codes and a set of columns kept exact, with dequantized row $\bar w_i$ and exact error $e_i=w_i-\bar w_i$. If every product is exact in the accumulator format and the pass's accumulation obeys Lemma~\ref{lem:tree}, with relative bound $\gamma_d$, $N$ internal nodes and absolute floor $\eta$, its value $\tilde z_i$ satisfies $|z_i-\tilde z_i|\le\beta_i=Q_i+\gamma_d\norm{\bar w_i}_2\norm h_2+N\eta(1+\gamma_d)\max(1,\max_gs_{ig})$, where $Q_i$ is $\norm{e_i}_2\norm h_2$ or any blockwise Cauchy--Schwarz or Hölder form.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorems~\ref{thm:envelope} and~\ref{thm:envelope-general}]
$z_i-\tilde z_i=\langle e_i,h\rangle+(\langle\bar w_i,h\rangle-\tilde z_i)$. The first term is bounded blockwise by Cauchy--Schwarz or Hölder, which gives each of the four forms of $Q_i$. The second is the rounding error of evaluating $\langle\bar w_i,h\rangle$ by a tree whose leaves are the exact products $s_{ig}q_{ij}h_j$ and $w_{ij}h_j$; the group-scale multiplications are nodes with one child, which Lemma~\ref{lem:tree} admits after multiplying the leaves below them by $s_{ig}$, so the underflow term carries the factor $\max(1,\max_gs_{ig})$. Lemma~\ref{lem:tree} bounds this term by $\gamma_d\sum_j|\bar w_{ij}h_j|+N\eta(1+\gamma_d)\max(1,\max_gs_{ig})$, and $\sum_j|\bar w_{ij}h_j|\le\norm{\bar w_i}_2\norm h_2$ completes the general bound. Theorem~\ref{thm:envelope} is the case of one scale $s_i$ per entry, no exact columns and $Q_i=\norm{e_i}_2\norm h_2$, with the pass's accumulation error bounded by $\gamma_{\rm copy}\sum_j|\bar w_{ij}h_j|+\eta'$. For a summation tree that the \modelcons{} covers, $\gamma_d\le\gamma_{\rm copy}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:accumulation}), and Lemma~\ref{lem:tree} gives the floor $\eta'=N\eta(1+\gamma_{\rm copy})\max(1,s_i)$, with $N$ the internal nodes of the tree and $\eta$ its per-node floor.
\end{proof}
The componentwise midpoint--radius form $|z-\tilde z|\le|e|^\top|h|$ is tighter than any of the four relaxations and costs the same to evaluate when $|e|$ is stored per group~\cite{rump2010}; the norm forms are used because they need one constant per vocabulary entry or block.

\subsection{The integer pass}\label{app:integer}
Quantizing the activation as well as the weights removes the floating-point accumulation from the pass altogether, because int8 products accumulated in int32 are exact for this shape~\cite{abdelfattah2025}. The price is an activation-quantization term. In retrieval, EAHR bounds int8 inner products by $\norm{e_q}\norm v+\norm{\hat q}\norm{e_v}$ with a floating-point guard and rechecks ambiguous items in FP32~\cite{eahr}; the theorem below is the corresponding statement for a head with exact int32 accumulation.
\begin{theorem}[Integer pass]\label{thm:integer}
Let $s_h>0$ be any activation scale in the output format, $q_h$ integer codes, and $e_h=h-s_hq_h$ the exact residual. Let $I_i=\sum_jq_{ij}q_{h,j}$ be accumulated exactly in 32-bit integers ($D\,q^{\rm w}_{\max}q^{\rm h}_{\max}<2^{31}$; $2560\cdot127^2$ qualifies) and $\tilde z_i=\fl(\fl(s_i\fl(I_i))\,s_h)$ without overflow or underflow. Then
\[|z_i-\tilde z_i|\le Q_i+\norm{s_iq_i}_2\norm{e_h}_2+\gamma_k\norm{s_iq_i}_2\norm{s_hq_h}_2,\]
with $k=3$ in general and $k=2$ when $s_h$ is a power of two.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$z_i=s_is_hI_i+s_i\langle q_i,e_h\rangle+\langle e_i,h\rangle$ holds in exact arithmetic for any $s_h$ and any codes, because $e_h$ is defined as the residual. The computed value applies at most three roundings to the product chain (the conversion of $I_i$ and the two multiplications), so $|\tilde z_i-s_is_hI_i|\le\gamma_3s_is_h|I_i|$; when $s_h$ is a power of two the last multiplication is exact and $\gamma_2$ suffices. Apply Cauchy--Schwarz to $I_i=\langle q_i,q_h\rangle$ and to $\langle q_i,e_h\rangle$, and bound $\langle e_i,h\rangle$ by $Q_i$ as in Theorem~\ref{thm:envelope-general}.
\end{proof}
The bound holds however the scale and codes of $h$ were chosen, so an implementation must bound $\norm{e_h}_2$ from above for the codes it actually produced; $\sqrt D\,s_h/2$ is valid only if every code is the nearest integer to $h_j/s_h$ and none is clipped.

\subsection{Computed norms and outward rounding}\label{app:outward}
The error bound $\beta_i$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:envelope} is a real number, and the kernel evaluates it in floating point. The two lemmas below make the evaluated interval an enclosure: the first computes norms of $h$ that are never below the exact norms, and the second rounds the endpoints outward so that every comparison between computed endpoints is sound. Offline constants ($\norm{e_i}_2$, $\norm{\bar w_i}_2$, $\norm{w_i}_2$) are computed exactly and stored rounded up.

\begin{lemma}[Computed norms]\label{lem:norms}
In FP32 with round to nearest and $D\le2^{14}$, let $\hat\sigma$ be any computed sum of the rounded squares $\fl(h_j^2)$ with depth $d\le2^{14}$, $\hat r\ge(1-2^{-16})\sqrt{\hat\sigma}$ any computed square root, and $N_2=\fl(\fl(\hat r(1+2^{-10}))+2^{-50})$. Then $N_2\ge\norm h_2$. Likewise, if $\hat\sigma_1$ is any computed sum of the $|h_j|$ with depth $d\le2^{13}$, then $N_1=\fl(\fl(\hat\sigma_1(1+2^{-10}))+2^{-50})\ge\norm h_1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A rounded nonnegative value, and a rounded addition of nonnegative values, returns at least $(1-u)$ times its exact value less $\mu$ (which also covers flush-to-zero). For $N_2$ the squares add one rounding to the $d$ levels of the sum, so $\norm h_2^2\le(1-u)^{-(d+1)}\hat\sigma+3D\mu$ and $\norm h_2\le(1-u)^{-(d+1)/2}(1-2^{-16})^{-1}\hat r+2^{-54}$ for $D\le2^{14}$. The computed $N_2\ge(1-u)^2(1+2^{-10})\hat r+2^{-51}$, and $(1-u)^2(1+2^{-10})\ge(1-u)^{-(d+1)/2}(1-2^{-16})^{-1}$ because $(d+1)u/2\le2^{-11}+2^{-25}$. For $N_1$ the terms $|h_j|$ are exact, so $\norm h_1\le(1-u)^{-d}(\hat\sigma_1+D\mu)$, and $(1-u)^2(1+2^{-10})\ge(1-u)^{-d}$ holds for $d\le2^{13}$ (then $du\le2^{-11}$) but fails at $d=2^{14}$, where the margin is about $-6\times10^{-7}$; there $N_1$ must be inflated by $1+2^{-9}$ instead.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Outward rounding of the computed interval]\label{lem:outward}
In a format with $p\ge12$ and round to nearest, let $\tilde z$ be representable, let $\beta^\ast$ be a real bound with $|z-\tilde z|\le\beta^\ast$, and let $b$ be computed from representable upper bounds by at most $k\le59$ rounded operations ($+$, $\times$, $\sqrt{\ }$) on nonnegative values along any path of the expression, so that $b\ge(1-u)^k\beta^\ast-k\mu$. Set
\[\hat b=\fl\bigl(\fl(\fl(b(1+2^{6-p}))+\fl(2^{4-p}|\tilde z|))+2^{e_{\min}+26}\bigr),\quad lo=\fl(\tilde z-\hat b),\quad hi=\fl(\tilde z+\hat b).\]
Then $lo\le z\le hi$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each rounding of a nonnegative value loses at most a factor $(1-u)$ and $\mu$, so $\hat b(1-u)\ge(1-u)^4(1+64u)b+16u(1-u)^3|\tilde z|+(1-u)^22^{e_{\min}+26}-4\mu$. Also $lo\le\tilde z-\hat b(1-u)+u|\tilde z|+\mu$. It suffices that $\hat b(1-u)\ge\beta^\ast+u|\tilde z|+\mu$: the $|\tilde z|$ terms satisfy $16u(1-u)^3\ge u$, the $b$ terms satisfy $(1-u)^{4+k}(1+64u)\ge(1-63u)(1+64u)\ge1$ for $u\le2^{-12}$, and $2^{26}\mu$ absorbs the absolute terms. The upper endpoint is symmetric.
\end{proof}
For FP32 the constants are $1+2^{-18}$, $2^{-20}|\tilde z|$ and $2^{-100}$. The exact reference contains witnesses that each ingredient is necessary: dropping the accumulation term of the error bound, the outward rounding or the norm inflation produces an interval that misses the exact logit (575 cases for the accumulation term, 58 of 300 checks for the norm inflation, and a constructed witness for the outward rounding)~\ev{precision}.

\subsection{The refinement ladder}\label{app:ladder}
The certified head's screen and the certified race (Theorem~\ref{thm:race}) are instances of one refinement procedure over a sequence of enclosures.
\begin{theorem}[Refinement ladder]\label{thm:ladder}
Let levels $\ell=0,\dots,L$ supply enclosures $lo^\ell_i\le z_i\le hi^\ell_i$, exact at level $L$, and let each $f_i$ be nondecreasing. Starting from entries $S$, at each level let $\tau=\max_{i\in S}f_i(lo^\ell_i)$ and keep $\{i\in S:f_i(hi^\ell_i)\ge\tau\}$; stop when one entry remains, every kept interval is a point, or $\ell=L$, and return the smallest kept index. The result is $\min\argmax_{i\in S}f_i(z_i)$, whatever the precision or reduction order of each level; $f_i(x)=x$ gives \Rreal{} and $f_i(x)=x/T+g_i$ gives \Srace{} (Definition~\ref{def:srace}).
\end{theorem}
The Lean proof assistant checks the theorem's decision logic and its filter (it keeps every maximizer and drops only beaten entries) over scaled integers, not under IEEE rounding~\ev{lean}.
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:ladder}]
$[f_i(\lo_i),f_i(\hi_i)]$ encloses $f_i(z_i)$ because $f_i$ is nondecreasing. A maximizer $m$ is never dropped: for the entry $j$ that attains $\tau$, $f_m(\hi_m)\ge f_m(z_m)\ge f_j(z_j)\ge f_j(\lo_j)=\tau$. A dropped entry is strictly below the entry attaining $\tau$, so it is not a maximizer. If one entry remains, it is the maximizer. If every kept interval is a point, each kept value is at least $\tau$ and at most $\tau$, so every kept entry is a maximizer, and every maximizer is kept. The exact level produces points. The returned index is a function of the maximizer set alone, so it does not depend on the precision or reduction order of any level.
\end{proof}

\subsection{How many entries a decision re-scores}\label{app:candidates}
The ladder says which vocabulary entries survive; the proposition below bounds how many do, which is what decides whether re-scoring is cheap.
\begin{proposition}[Candidate counts]\label{prop:candidates}
Let the level-0 intervals have widths $\omega_i=\hi_i-\lo_i\le\omega$ (with outward rounding, $\omega_i\approx2\beta_i$) and let $C$ be the entries kept at level 0.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Greedy: $C\subseteq\{i:z_i\ge z_{\max}-\omega_i-\omega_a\}$ for any maximizer $a$, hence $|C|\le e^{2\omega/T'}/p^{(T')}_{\max}$ for every $T'>0$, where $p^{(T')}=\softmax(z/T')$.
\item Race: if the $g_i$ are i.i.d.\ standard Gumbel and $p=\softmax(z/T)$, then with $t=2\omega/T$,
\[\E|C|\le\sum_i\frac{p_ie^t}{1+p_i(e^t-1)}\le\min(V,e^t).\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(1) $\hi_i\le z_i+\omega_i$ and $\lo_a\ge z_a-\omega_a$, so a kept entry satisfies $z_i+\omega_i\ge\tau\ge z_a-\omega_a$. Each such entry has $p^{(T')}_i\ge p^{(T')}_{\max}e^{-2\omega/T'}$, and the probabilities sum to at most one. (2) In score space the widths are $\omega_i/T$, so $C\subseteq\{i:y_i\ge M-t\}$ with $y_i=z_i/T+g_i$ and $M=\max_jy_j$. Entry $i$ belongs to that set if and only if $y_i+t\ge\max_{j\ne i}y_j$. The maximum of independent Gumbels with locations $z_j/T$ is Gumbel with location $\log\sum_{j\ne i}e^{z_j/T}$, and two independent Gumbels $X_a$ and $X_b$ with locations $a$ and $b$ satisfy $P(X_a\ge X_b)=e^a/(e^a+e^b)$. Hence the probability is $p_ie^t/(p_ie^t+1-p_i)$; sum over $i$.
\end{proof}
At $T=1$ and error bound $\beta$ (so $t\approx4\beta$), the bound on the expected re-scoring set of an exact race is 55 entries for $\beta=1$, 2,981 for $\beta=2$ and up to $1.6\times10^5$ for $\beta=3$. Exact sampling through the cheap pass is therefore guaranteed to be cheap only if the bound is about two logits or less. Greedy selection has no distribution-free bound, since a flat distribution (small $p_{\max}$) keeps many entries. In logits the bound is $\beta_i\approx\norm{e_i}_2\norm h_2$; with the head's int8 constants for one scale per entry (median $\norm{e_i}_2/\norm{w_i}_2=0.97\%$ and $\norm{w_i}_2=0.652$) that is about $0.0063\norm h_2$, so the norm of the head input decides whether $\beta$ is a fraction of a logit or several. On the captured plain-decode states that norm has a median of 159 (1st percentile 132, 99th 169), which puts the int8 error bound at about one logit~\ev{selfevidence,head-constants}.

Measured on captured states, the bound's median is 1.00 logit, 75 times the median realized error of 0.013, because the bound assumes the worst alignment of $e_i$ and $h$~\ev{stats}. The measured candidate sets are far below the bound because most top-two margins are several logits: the gap between the two largest logits has a median of 7.3 logits and a 10th percentile of 0.68~\ev{stats}. Tables~\ref{tab:candidates} and~\ref{tab:rstock} give the candidate sets per copy and the positions and steps the contract leaves to the stock kernel, which Section~\ref{sec:geometry-selfevidence} summarizes.

\paragraph{Fallback by call size} On calls of 128 consecutive positions the kernel replay measures 27\% and 77\% of calls with a fallback (Hopper, conservative; Section~\ref{sec:rstock-share})~\ev{kernel-path}. Independence at the kernel replay's own rates would give 30\% and 85\% (derived)~\ev{kernel-replay}, so the measured shares are consistent with undecided positions clustering in fewer calls than independence predicts.

\paragraph{Other copies on plain decoding} The same replay covers heads the main text summarizes in a sentence~\ev{selfevidence}. Int4 alone is useless as a certificate: with one scale per 32 weights a decision needs 20,751 candidate tokens on average (p99 230,238, maximum 248,192) and 0.02\% of positions are decided by one entry, and with one scale per 128 weights the median set is 13,991 entries. A two-plane cascade, an int4 plane for every entry and an int4 residual plane only for entries the first cannot exclude, reads 0.32 of the BF16 head's bytes for one position and 0.41--0.42 over capture steps with 5 to 16 held-out positions, at the price of an irregular second gather. Seeded races at $T=1$ and 0.7 behave like greedy selection, with a mean of 1.33 candidates and a p99 of 5 for the copy with one scale per 128 weights~\ev{selfevidence-csv}.

Re-scoring candidates with IEEE FP32 accumulation on CUDA cores leaves no position undecided; with the tensor-core error model applied to the re-scoring as well, 2.1\% of positions still overlap and need FP64. Re-scoring also reads little: on capture steps with 5 to 16 held-out positions the candidates of the copy with one scale per 128 weights cover 9.5--12.5 distinct vocabulary entries on average~\ev{selfevidence}, so the gather of their BF16 rows reads tens of kilobytes beside a 636~MB int8 pass (derived). Tightening the error bound helps little: 16 to 64 exact outlier columns or a 64-dimensional PCA rotation of $h$ lower the mean count by 3--7\% for 1--7\% more pass bytes, and centring $W$ raises it by 10\%~\ev{selfevidence-csv,stats}. Centring fails because the mean entry points along the bulk of the vocabulary (median cosine 0.39) but not along the entries that compete for the argmax (median cosine $-0.11$ over each position's top eight), so it lowers the median error norm over all entries by 14\% and raises it by 20\% on the entries that matter~\ev{stats}. For comparison, an int8 copy of the Qwen3-8B head with one scale per entry agrees with the BF16 argmax on 97.56\% of positions~\cite{archead}, so an exact re-score must catch the rest.

\subsection{The kernel and its checks}\label{app:kernel-checks}
The certified path of Section~\ref{sec:kernel-design} is sound only for a kernel that computes the modelled arithmetic (Table~\ref{tab:assumptions-full}), a property that is checked, not proved. Each compiled variant must pass a self-test before it certifies, and every call checks eight exactly computed vocabulary entries for each position of the batch~\ev{kernel-impl}. The package's 145 tests passed on the GPU, where all 148 kernel variants compiled for sm\_90 and each default tile configuration had no misses in about a million checks; the host-side refusals added after that run have CPU tests~\ev{kernel-impl}. Two violations of the modelled arithmetic were measured. Under Triton 3.7.1, an int8 tile of the W8A16 pass loaded through a TMA box with a 64-byte inner dimension gave wrong raw products against FP64 in every such configuration tested, at some or all of $M=1$, 16, 64, 128 and 256, while pointer loads and 128-byte boxes were exact; three tiles with \code{block\_m} 128 missed the error bound in some runs, and both families are refused~\ev{kernel-tma}. Of seven Triton and \code{ptxas} builds tested, the four without both Triton $\ge$3.8.0 and \code{ptxas} $\ge$13.3 gave wrong products and the three with both did not, a version pattern that matches the closed issue triton-lang/triton\#9433~\ev{triton-tma}.

\paragraph{Expected time under the \modelhopper{}} Section~\ref{sec:kernel-runtime} gives the head's expected time with each fallback under the \modelcons{}. Under the \modelhopper{}, with its lower fallback rates, both expected times lie between those and the all-certified time at every measured $M$~\ev{kernel-path}.

\subsection{What is machine-checked, what is tested, and what is proved on paper}\label{app:checked}\label{app:cpu}
\paragraph{Machine-checked} Lean 4.19.0 elaborates two files of this paper's lemmas with no \code{sorry}, no \code{admit} and no added axiom~\ev{lean}. Three further files, on the drafting proposals of Section~\notessec{app:external-drafting}{5.8} of the research notes, elaborate the same way; nothing in this paper rests on them. The paper's two files work over integers, which stand for scores after clearing a common positive denominator. \code{formal/CertifiedArgmax.lean} proves that a quantization bound and an accumulation bound add and that outward widening keeps an enclosure valid (\code{envelope\_compose}, \code{widen}); that disjoint enclosures decide comparisons, that a certified winner is the unique maximizer and that any two certificates from valid enclosures name the same token (\code{separation}, \code{certified\_argmax}, \code{certificate\_unique}), which is the decision logic of Theorem~\ref{thm:ladder}; that the filter $\hi_i\ge\max_j\lo_j$ keeps every maximizer and drops only beaten entries (\code{maximizer\_survives}, \code{dropped\_row\_loses}); that adding a fixed noise value or scaling by a positive temperature preserves an enclosure (\code{shift\_encloses}, \code{scale\_encloses}); the acceptance guards of Theorem~\ref{thm:guards} with an enclosed numerator and their exclusivity (\code{accept\_guard}, \code{reject\_guard}, \code{guards\_exclusive}); the residual-weight enclosure of Theorem~\ref{thm:residualself} (\code{residual\_interval}); the $\ell_\infty$/$\ell_1$ Hölder bound behind the Hölder form of transport (Appendix~\ref{app:transport-geometry}) and the Hölder form of the quantization error bound (\code{holder}); and the sequential case of Lemma~\ref{lem:tree} (\code{sequential\_sum\_bound}). \code{formal/DecisionGuards.lean} holds nine declarations: scaled-integer acceptance and rejection guards and their stability under refinement, associativity of chronological composition, deterministic replay of records from equal starts (the logical skeleton of the replay proposition for the recurrent state in Section~\notessec{app:state}{6.2} of the research notes), and guards that stop a stale epoch or prefix from publishing state. A deliberately false variant of \code{sequential\_sum\_bound} is rejected, so the check is not vacuous.

Outside the checker are IEEE rounding and the bridge from floating-point values to these integers; the tree, pairwise, blocked and fused forms of the accumulation bound; Cauchy--Schwarz; real exponentials and the transported mass bounds; the Gumbel-max law and every probability statement; the tensor-core adder model; and compiler lowering, race freedom on the GPU and the engine's state restoration.

\paragraph{Tested against exact arithmetic} An exact floating-point reference (\code{src/precision\_reference.py}) emulates every BF16, FP32 and FP64 rounding on rationals and checks each bound and decision of Sections~\ref{sec:selfevidence} and~\ref{sec:decisions} against real arithmetic~\ev{precision}. Its 21 test methods run 40,015 generated checks: 5,600 accumulation-bound checks over eight summation models (sequential, pairwise, blocked, split-$K$, round toward zero, flush to zero and the fused multi-term adder), where the largest observed error was 17--83\% of the bound depending on the model; 1,767 approximate-pass entries and 2,767 integer-pass entries enclosed; 420 certified argmax cases, 72 of them exact ties, of which 183 ended at the approximate pass, 191 after FP32 re-scoring, 24 in FP64 and 22 in exact arithmetic; 360 races under a fixed noise field (62 with a forced tie at the top), 200 top-$k$ sets, 300 acceptance decisions (148 with the uniform placed next to the threshold), 300 residual races and 300 verifications in SGLang's sibling form; 900 emulations of the stock head's rounding on adversarial near-tie heads with $D\le8$; 160 heads with subnormal weights and inputs; and the witnesses cited in the text for R-bf16 (Section~\ref{sec:headcontracts}), for BF16 split-$K$ partials and for a noise stream consumed in evaluation order. The heads are synthetic with small $V$ and $D$, and the fused adder is a model, not hardware.

A second exact reference, with bounded integer heads and base-2 masses represented as rationals (so that no floating-point exponential is trusted), tests the decision and race algorithms~\ev{inherited}. Its 18 decision and 3 race test methods pass on the instances listed in Section~\notessec{notes:rational}{8} of the research notes. The race tests use fixed positive rational priorities, so they establish pathwise equivalence of bounded and dense algorithms, not properties of continuous Gumbel noise. The exact witnesses that separate the contracts are in Appendix~\ref{app:contracts}; those for the recurrent state are in Section~\notessec{app:state}{6.2} of the research notes.

\paragraph{Proved on paper only} Every other statement of the paper, including the floating-point lemmas above, the transport theorem and the crossover proposition, the sampling results of Appendix~\ref{app:sampling} and the propositions of the research notes, is proved in the text and checked by the tests above only where a test is named. Tests are not proofs, and neither is a GPU measurement.
