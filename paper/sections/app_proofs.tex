\section{Proofs for the contract and the certified head}\label{app:proofs}\label{app:derivations}
This appendix proves the results of Sections~\ref{sec:headcontracts} and~\ref{sec:selfevidence} whose proofs are not in the main text, and states and proves the secondary results they rely on: the constants of the accumulation models, the integer pass, the computed norms and outward rounding that make an evaluated envelope an enclosure, the bounds on how many rows a decision re-scores, and the directional margin. Appendix~\ref{app:tail} applies the directional margin to a certified decoder tail, which its kill test rejected. Appendix~\ref{app:checked} separates what is machine-checked from what is tested and what is proved only on paper.

\subsection{The BF16 separation lemma}\label{app:bf16gap}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:bf16gap}]
Rounding is nondecreasing, so if $\rnb(y_a)\le\rnb(y_b)$ while $y_a>y_b$, both values round to the same $v$ and lie in its preimage. When $|v|$ is not a power of two, the preimage has length $\ulpb(v)$ and both values lie in the binade of $v$. When $|v|=2^e$ the preimage is $[v-\ulpb(v)/4,\,v+\ulpb(v)/2]$ for $v>0$ (symmetrically for $v<0$): two values on one side of $v$ are within half the spacing of their binade, and two values on both sides are within $\tfrac34\ulpb(v)\le\ulpb(y_a)$. At $v=0$ the preimage has the subnormal spacing. In every case $y_a-y_b\le\ulpb(\max(|y_a|,|y_b|))$, contradicting the hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Accumulation trees and the constants of the error models}\label{app:accumulation}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:tree}]
For a node $v$ and a leaf $\ell$ below it, let $P_{v\ell}=\prod(1+u_x)$ over the internal nodes $x$ on the path from $\ell$ up to $v$, and for an internal node $w$ below $v$ let $P'_{vw}$ be the same product over the nodes strictly above $w$ up to $v$, with $P'_{vv}=1$. By induction on height,
\[|\hat s_v|\le\sum_\ell|p_\ell|P_{v\ell}+\eta\sum_wP'_{vw},\qquad|\hat s_v-s_v|\le\sum_\ell|p_\ell|(P_{v\ell}-1)+\eta\sum_wP'_{vw}.\]
Leaves satisfy both with equality. For an internal node, $|\hat s_v|\le(1+u_v)\sum_c|\hat s_c|+\eta$ and $|\hat s_v-s_v|\le\sum_c|\hat s_c-s_c|+u_v\sum_c|\hat s_c|+\eta$; substituting the hypotheses for the children multiplies every $P$ and $P'$ by $(1+u_v)$ and adds $P'_{vv}=1$. Finally $P_{v\ell}\le(1+u)^d$, $P'_{vw}\le(1+u)^{d-1}$, and there are $N$ internal nodes.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Depths and node units} An IEEE rounded addition has $u_v=u$, since $|a+b|\le|a|+|b|$~\cite{higham2002}. Sequential accumulation from zero has depth $d=n$, pairwise summation $\lceil\log_2n\rceil$, blocks of $B$ summed as a tree and then accumulated $\lceil n/B\rceil+\lceil\log_2B\rceil$, and split-$K$ adds the number of partials. A multi-term adder that aligns $k+1$ addends to the largest, truncates each to $F$ fractional bits below its leading bit, adds exactly and rounds once has $u_v=(k+1)2^{-F}+2^{1-p}$ with truncation, and the accumulation path has $\lceil n/k\rceil$ such nodes. Tensor cores behave this way rather than as IEEE adders~\cite{fasi2021}. A node that multiplies its only child by a constant and rounds adds one level of depth.

\paragraph{The constants used in this paper} Each model below except the IEEE one is an assumption about hardware that no vendor documents; Table~\ref{tab:assumptions} lists where each is used. The values are for $D=2560$.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{The \modelcons{}}, $\gamma=\gcons$. At most $2D$ roundings, each of relative size $2^{-23}$ of the magnitudes it combines, in any order: $\gamma_{2D}(2^{-23})=6.107\times10^{-4}$. It covers any reduction tree, split-$K$ with FP32 partials, and multi-term adders that keep FP32's 24 fractional bits and round to nearest or truncate. The committed replay analyses use $6.11\times10^{-4}$, the largest value over the exact reference's fused-adder models with blocks of 2 to 64 products, rounding to nearest or toward zero, plus one scale multiply~\ev{precision}; the two agree to the precision the paper quotes.
\item \emph{The \modelhopper{}}, $\gamma=\ghop$. \citet{khattak2026} model Hopper's BF16 \code{wgmma} with FP32 output as a $k=16$ adder with $F=25$ and truncation, so $u_v=17\cdot2^{-25}+2^{-23}=6.26\times10^{-7}$ over $D/16=160$ nodes, and $(1+u_v)^{160}-1=1.001\times10^{-4}$. An allowance of up to 160 further truncating FP32 additions for split-$K$ partials gives $(1+u_v)^{160}(1+2^{-23})^{160}-1=1.1922\times10^{-4}$. The paper quotes $1.19\times10^{-4}$; the committed replays of the head and of the stock divergences used exactly that rounded value, 0.18\% below the one derived here, so the shares they report under this model are for $\gamma=1.19\times10^{-4}$.
\item \emph{FP8 tensor cores.} The same published model gives FP8 \code{wgmma} only 13 fractional bits in 32-term blocks, consistent with the accumulation limits reported for FP8 training~\cite{deepseekv3}; an FP8 tensor-core pass needs periodic promotion to FP32 and its own analysis, and none is used here.
\item \emph{IEEE FP32 on CUDA cores.} A balanced tree over blocks of 128 products, a sequential sum across the 20 blocks and one scale multiply give depth 28 with $u=2^{-24}$, so $\gamma=1.67\times10^{-6}$. This model does not describe the stock tensor-core GEMM; the paper uses it only as a sensitivity row.
\end{itemize}
Since the Hopper model is a published measurement-based model and not a documented contract, kernels built on it were validated against exact arithmetic on adversarial inputs~\ev{precision}, and the stock kernel's own error at the served shapes is \pend{kernel-error}.

\subsection{The envelope}\label{app:envelope}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:envelope}]
$z_i-\tilde z_i=\langle e_i,h\rangle+(\langle\bar w_i,h\rangle-\tilde z_i)$. The first term is bounded blockwise by Cauchy--Schwarz or Hölder, which gives each of the four forms of $Q_i$. The second is the rounding error of evaluating $\langle\bar w_i,h\rangle$ by a tree whose leaves are the exact products $s_{ig}q_{ij}h_j$ and $w_{ij}h_j$; the group-scale multiplications are nodes with one child, which Lemma~\ref{lem:tree} admits after multiplying the leaves below them by $s_{ig}$, so the underflow term carries the factor $\max(1,\max_gs_{ig})$. Lemma~\ref{lem:tree} and $\sum_j|\bar w_{ij}h_j|\le\norm{\bar w_i}_2\norm h_2$ complete the bound.
\end{proof}
The componentwise midpoint--radius form $|z-\tilde z|\le|e|^\top|h|$ is tighter than any of the four relaxations and costs the same to evaluate when $|e|$ is stored per group~\cite{rump2010}; the norm forms are used because they need one constant per row or block.

\subsection{The integer pass}\label{app:integer}
Quantizing the activation as well as the weights removes the floating-point accumulation from the pass altogether, because int8 products accumulated in int32 are exact for this shape~\cite{abdelfattah2025}. The price is an activation-quantization term. In retrieval, EAHR bounds int8 inner products by $\norm{e_q}\norm v+\norm{\hat q}\norm{e_v}$ with a floating-point guard and rechecks ambiguous items in FP32~\cite{eahr}; the theorem below is the corresponding statement for a head with exact int32 accumulation.
\begin{theorem}[Integer pass]\label{thm:integer}
Let $s_h>0$ be any activation scale in the output format, $q_h$ integer codes, and $e_h=h-s_hq_h$ the exact residual. Let $I_i=\sum_jq_{ij}q_{h,j}$ be accumulated exactly in 32-bit integers ($D\,q^{\rm w}_{\max}q^{\rm h}_{\max}<2^{31}$; $2560\cdot127^2$ qualifies) and $\tilde z_i=\fl(\fl(s_i\fl(I_i))\,s_h)$ without overflow or underflow. Then
\[|z_i-\tilde z_i|\le Q_i+\norm{s_iq_i}_2\norm{e_h}_2+\gamma_k\norm{s_iq_i}_2\norm{s_hq_h}_2,\]
with $k=3$ in general and $k=2$ when $s_h$ is a power of two.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$z_i=s_is_hI_i+s_i\langle q_i,e_h\rangle+\langle e_i,h\rangle$ holds in exact arithmetic for any $s_h$ and any codes, because $e_h$ is defined as the residual. The computed value applies at most three roundings to the product chain (the conversion of $I_i$ and the two multiplications), so $|\tilde z_i-s_is_hI_i|\le\gamma_3s_is_h|I_i|$; when $s_h$ is a power of two the last multiplication is exact and $\gamma_2$ suffices. Apply Cauchy--Schwarz to $I_i=\langle q_i,q_h\rangle$ and to $\langle q_i,e_h\rangle$, and bound $\langle e_i,h\rangle$ by $Q_i$ as in Theorem~\ref{thm:envelope}.
\end{proof}
The bound holds however the scale and codes of $h$ were chosen, so an implementation must bound $\norm{e_h}_2$ from above for the codes it actually produced; $\sqrt D\,s_h/2$ is valid only if every code is the nearest integer to $h_j/s_h$ and none is clipped.

\subsection{Computed norms and outward rounding}\label{app:outward}
The envelope of Theorem~\ref{thm:envelope} is a real number, and the kernel evaluates it in floating point. The two lemmas below make the evaluated interval an enclosure: the first computes norms of $h$ that are never below the exact norms, and the second rounds the endpoints outward so that every comparison between computed endpoints is sound. Offline constants ($\norm{e_i}_2$, $\norm{\bar w_i}_2$, $\norm{w_i}_2$) are computed exactly and stored rounded up.

\begin{lemma}[Computed norms]\label{lem:norms}
In FP32 with round to nearest and $D\le2^{14}$, let $\hat\sigma$ be any computed sum of the rounded squares $\fl(h_j^2)$ with depth $d\le2^{14}$, $\hat r\ge(1-2^{-16})\sqrt{\hat\sigma}$ any computed square root, and $N_2=\fl(\fl(\hat r(1+2^{-10}))+2^{-50})$. Then $N_2\ge\norm h_2$. Likewise, if $\hat\sigma_1$ is any computed sum of the $|h_j|$ with depth $d\le2^{13}$, then $N_1=\fl(\fl(\hat\sigma_1(1+2^{-10}))+2^{-50})\ge\norm h_1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A rounded nonnegative value, and a rounded addition of nonnegative values, returns at least $(1-u)$ times its exact value less $\mu$ (which also covers flush-to-zero). For $N_2$ the squares add one rounding to the $d$ levels of the sum, so $\norm h_2^2\le(1-u)^{-(d+1)}\hat\sigma+3D\mu$ and $\norm h_2\le(1-u)^{-(d+1)/2}(1-2^{-16})^{-1}\hat r+2^{-54}$ for $D\le2^{14}$. The computed $N_2\ge(1-u)^2(1+2^{-10})\hat r+2^{-51}$, and $(1-u)^2(1+2^{-10})\ge(1-u)^{-(d+1)/2}(1-2^{-16})^{-1}$ because $(d+1)u/2\le2^{-11}+2^{-25}$. For $N_1$ the terms $|h_j|$ are exact, so $\norm h_1\le(1-u)^{-d}(\hat\sigma_1+D\mu)$, and $(1-u)^2(1+2^{-10})\ge(1-u)^{-d}$ holds for $d\le2^{13}$ (then $du\le2^{-11}$) but fails at $d=2^{14}$, where the margin is about $-6\times10^{-7}$; there $N_1$ must be inflated by $1+2^{-9}$ instead.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Outward rounding of the computed interval]\label{lem:outward}
In a format with $p\ge12$ and round to nearest, let $\tilde z$ be representable, let $\beta^\ast$ be a real bound with $|z-\tilde z|\le\beta^\ast$, and let $b$ be computed from representable upper bounds by at most $k\le59$ rounded operations ($+$, $\times$, $\sqrt{\ }$) on nonnegative values along any path of the expression, so that $b\ge(1-u)^k\beta^\ast-k\mu$. Set
\[\hat b=\fl\bigl(\fl(\fl(b(1+2^{6-p}))+\fl(2^{4-p}|\tilde z|))+2^{e_{\min}+26}\bigr),\quad lo=\fl(\tilde z-\hat b),\quad hi=\fl(\tilde z+\hat b).\]
Then $lo\le z\le hi$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each rounding of a nonnegative value loses at most a factor $(1-u)$ and $\mu$, so $\hat b(1-u)\ge(1-u)^4(1+64u)b+16u(1-u)^3|\tilde z|+(1-u)^22^{e_{\min}+26}-4\mu$. Also $lo\le\tilde z-\hat b(1-u)+u|\tilde z|+\mu$. It suffices that $\hat b(1-u)\ge\beta^\ast+u|\tilde z|+\mu$: the $|\tilde z|$ terms satisfy $16u(1-u)^3\ge u$, the $b$ terms satisfy $(1-u)^{4+k}(1+64u)\ge(1-63u)(1+64u)\ge1$ for $u\le2^{-12}$, and $2^{26}\mu$ absorbs the absolute terms. The upper endpoint is symmetric.
\end{proof}
For FP32 the constants are $1+2^{-18}$, $2^{-20}|\tilde z|$ and $2^{-100}$. The exact reference contains witnesses that each ingredient is necessary: dropping the accumulation term of the envelope, the outward rounding or the norm inflation produces an interval that misses the exact logit (575 cases for the accumulation term, 58 for the other two)~\ev{precision}.

\subsection{The refinement ladder}\label{app:ladder}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:ladder}]
$[f_i(lo_i),f_i(hi_i)]$ encloses $f_i(z_i)$ because $f_i$ is nondecreasing. A maximizer $m$ is never dropped: for the row $j$ that attains $\tau$, $f_m(hi_m)\ge f_m(z_m)\ge f_j(z_j)\ge f_j(lo_j)=\tau$. A dropped row is strictly below the row attaining $\tau$, so it is not a maximizer. If one row remains, it is the maximizer. If every kept interval is a point, each kept value is at least $\tau$ and at most $\tau$, so every kept row is a maximizer, and every maximizer is kept. The exact level produces points. The returned index is a function of the maximizer set alone, so it does not depend on the precision or reduction order of any level.
\end{proof}

\subsection{How many rows a decision re-scores}\label{app:candidates}
The ladder says which rows survive; the proposition below bounds how many do, which is what decides whether re-scoring is cheap.
\begin{proposition}[Candidate counts]\label{prop:candidates}
Let the level-0 intervals have widths $\omega_i=hi_i-lo_i\le\omega$ (with outward rounding, $\omega_i\approx2\beta_i$) and let $C$ be the rows kept at level 0.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Greedy: $C\subseteq\{i:z_i\ge z_{\max}-\omega_i-\omega_a\}$ for any maximizer $a$, hence $|C|\le e^{2\omega/T'}/p^{(T')}_{\max}$ for every $T'>0$, where $p^{(T')}=\softmax(z/T')$.
\item Race: if the $g_i$ are i.i.d.\ standard Gumbel and $p=\softmax(z/T)$, then with $t=2\omega/T$,
\[\E|C|\le\sum_i\frac{p_ie^t}{1+p_i(e^t-1)}\le\min(V,e^t).\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(1) $hi_i\le z_i+\omega_i$ and $lo_a\ge z_a-\omega_a$, so a kept row satisfies $z_i+\omega_i\ge\tau\ge z_a-\omega_a$. Each such row has $p^{(T')}_i\ge p^{(T')}_{\max}e^{-2\omega/T'}$, and the probabilities sum to at most one. (2) In score space the widths are $\omega_i/T$, so $C\subseteq\{i:y_i\ge M-t\}$ with $y_i=z_i/T+g_i$ and $M=\max_jy_j$. Row $i$ belongs to that set if and only if $y_i+t\ge\max_{j\ne i}y_j$. The maximum of independent Gumbels with locations $z_j/T$ is Gumbel with location $\log\sum_{j\ne i}e^{z_j/T}$, and two independent Gumbels with locations $a$ and $b$ satisfy $P(G_a\ge G_b)=e^a/(e^a+e^b)$. Hence the probability is $p_ie^t/(p_ie^t+1-p_i)$; sum over $i$.
\end{proof}
At $T=1$ and envelope half-width $\beta$ (so $t\approx4\beta$), the bound on the expected re-scoring set of an exact race is 55 rows for $\beta=1$, 2,981 for $\beta=2$ and up to $1.6\times10^5$ for $\beta=3$. Exact sampling through the cheap pass is therefore guaranteed to be cheap only if the envelope is about two logits wide or less. Greedy selection has no distribution-free bound, since a flat distribution (small $p_{\max}$) keeps many rows. The envelope in logits is $\beta_i\approx\norm{e_i}_2\norm h_2$; with the int8 per-row constants of the head (median $\norm{e_i}_2/\norm{w_i}_2=0.97\%$ and $\norm{w_i}_2=0.652$) that is about $0.0063\norm h_2$, so the norm of the head input decides whether $\beta$ is a fraction of a logit or several. On the captured plain-decode states that norm has a median of 159 (1st percentile 132, 99th 169), which puts the int8 row envelope at about one logit~\ev{selfevidence,head-constants}. The measured candidate sets are far below the bound because most top-two margins are several logits.

\paragraph{Measured sets beyond Table~\ref{tab:candidates}} The same replay covers heads and states the main text summarizes in a sentence~\ev{selfevidence,selfevidence-dflash}. Int4 alone is useless as a certificate: with 32-column groups a decision needs 20,751 candidate rows on average (p99 230,238, maximum 248,192) and 0.02\% of positions are decided by one row, and with 128-column groups the median set is 13,991 rows. A two-plane cascade, an int4 plane for every row and an int4 residual plane only for rows the first cannot exclude, reads 0.32 of the BF16 head's bytes at batch one and 0.41--0.42 over real batches of 5 to 16 rows, at the price of an irregular second gather. Re-scoring candidates with IEEE FP32 accumulation on CUDA cores leaves no position undecided; with the tensor-core error model applied to the re-scoring as well, 2.1\% of positions still overlap and need FP64. Centring the head before quantizing it is instructive: the mean row points along the bulk of the vocabulary (median cosine 0.39) but not along the rows that compete for the argmax (median cosine $-0.11$ over each position's top eight), so it lowers the median error norm over all rows by 14\% and raises it by 20\% on the rows that matter. On DFlash-4B verify rows after the first rejection, which are conditioned on a wrong prefix and have flatter distributions, the int8 per-row copy needs 4.0 candidates on average, and on the draft head's own rows 1.28; FP8 needs 28.9 over all verify rows. No envelope was violated and no winner was missed in any of these sets. The gap condition, the only \Rstock{} rule evaluated on the DFlash-4B verify rows, leaves at least one row to the stock kernel in 78\% (\modelhopper) to 84\% (\modelcons) of the capture's verify steps, against 16.0\% and 21.6\% of plain-decode steps, so a whole-batch fallback would run in most verify passes unless the interval form does much better there, which is untested~\ev{selfevidence-dflash,rstock}. For comparison, a row-wise int8 copy of the Qwen3-8B head agrees with the BF16 argmax on 97.56\% of positions~\cite{archead}, so an exact re-score must catch the rest.

\subsection{The directional margin}\label{app:directional}
When the information is about the hidden state rather than the logits, a box of logit bounds is loose; the certificate below uses the direction of each competitor instead.
\begin{proposition}[Directional margin]\label{prop:directional}
Let the logits be $z=Wh$ in real arithmetic and suppose $\norm{h-\tilde h}_2\le\varepsilon_h$ for a known $\tilde h$. Then $c$ is the strict argmax of $Wh$ for every such $h$ if and only if
\[(w_c-w_j)^\top\tilde h>\norm{w_c-w_j}_2\,\varepsilon_h\quad\text{for all }j\neq c.\]
This condition is implied by, and can be much weaker than, the box condition obtained from the row bounds $|z_j-w_j^\top\tilde h|\le\norm{w_j}_2\varepsilon_h$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For fixed $j$, $\min_{\norm e_2\le\varepsilon_h}(w_c-w_j)^\top(\tilde h+e)=(w_c-w_j)^\top\tilde h-\norm{w_c-w_j}_2\varepsilon_h$ by Cauchy--Schwarz, with equality at $e=-\varepsilon_h(w_c-w_j)/\norm{w_c-w_j}_2$. So $z_c>z_j$ throughout the ball exactly when the stated inequality holds. The box condition reads $(w_c-w_j)^\top\tilde h>(\norm{w_c}_2+\norm{w_j}_2)\varepsilon_h$, and $\norm{w_c-w_j}_2\le\norm{w_c}_2+\norm{w_j}_2$.
\end{proof}
The directional certificate is not free: it needs $\norm{w_c-w_j}_2$ for every competitor, which for all $j$ costs as much as a head pass. It is useful after a box screen has reduced the competitors to a few rows, and for the stock contract the gap condition of Theorem~\ref{thm:stock} applies on top of it, as it does for the ladder. It is also a statement in real arithmetic; the floating-point terms of Appendix~\ref{app:accumulation} turn it into an enclosure of computed values.

\subsection{Beyond the head: a certified decoder tail (P1, rejected)}\label{app:tail}\label{sec:tail}\label{sec:skipped}
Skipping a computation removes its cost only if nothing downstream needs its result, so every skipped result falls into one of three classes. It is \emph{dead} when it has no remaining consumer. It is \emph{deferred} when a later consumer needs it and a reconstruction path exists; the cost is moved, not removed, and must be charged where it lands. It is \emph{soundly enclosed} when a certificate bounds it well enough for every consumer that remains. A claimed saving should name its class, because early exits and approximations can borrow time from future tokens.

The head is dead work in this sense: once a token is committed, no future computation reads the logits. In target-only decoding of Qwen3.5-4B the same is true of more. The last decoder layer is a full-attention layer; its attention writes the key--value cache before its MLP runs, and the final MLP, the final norm and the head have no consumer in future state. A certificate that decided the token before that tail could therefore remove about 1.41~GB of the 8.41~GB streamed per step (the head and the last layer's MLP, Table~\ref{tab:bytes}), permanently. The cut does not transfer to speculation without re-analysis: the MTP layer and the DFlash drafter consume target hidden states, so work that is dead in plain decoding is deferred there.

The hypothesis (P1) was that a cheap surrogate of that tail with a sound enclosure of the head input could let the commit rule decide the token before the tail runs. A one-shot surrogate that costs $C$ (surrogate plus certificate) and certifies a fraction $p$ of positions pays only if $C<p\,T_{\rm tail}$, and the ceiling for a tail that is a fraction $f$ of the step is $1/(1-f)$. The directional margin is the natural certificate when the surrogate predicts the head input with a radius. The kill test was deliberately optimistic: record the residual stream at the cut, the reference head input, the logits and the tokens; freeze an int8 surrogate of the tail; take the oracle radius $\norm{h-\tilde h}_2$; and apply the directional certificate with no overhead. If the oracle certification rate satisfied $p_{\rm oracle}T_{\rm tail}\le C_{\rm surrogate}$, the design was to be dropped; otherwise real bounds would be compared with the dense tail and with the certified head alone.

The kill test rejects the design~\ev{tail}. It used 2,064 positions from 16 held-out prompts (four per domain), with the head input taken from a BF16 forward pass of a reference implementation on the CPU, teacher-forced on the engine's greedy outputs; that head input differs from the engine's captured one by a median 1.8\% in relative $\ell_2$ norm, and its argmax equals the engine's token at 99.5\% of positions. An int8 per-row copy of the final MLP moves the head input by a median 0.94\%. With that realized error as an oracle radius and no overhead, the directional certificate decides 85.4\% of positions when the head is exact and 71.8\% when the head is int8 as well, while the int8 head alone, with the exact MLP, decides 79.3\% without any re-scoring. The MLP is 0.142~GB of the tail's 1.413~GB, so an int8 surrogate can save at most 0.071~GB per token, and it lowers the rate at which the certified head decides on its own. Certifying the head alone is the part worth building.

\subsection{What is machine-checked, what is tested, and what is proved on paper}\label{app:checked}\label{app:cpu}
\paragraph{Machine-checked} Lean 4.19.0 elaborates two files with no \code{sorry}, no \code{admit} and no added axiom~\ev{lean}. Both work over integers, which stand for scores after clearing a common positive denominator. \code{formal/CertifiedArgmax.lean} proves that a quantization bound and an accumulation bound add and that outward widening keeps an enclosure valid (\code{envelope\_compose}, \code{widen}); that disjoint enclosures decide comparisons, that a certified winner is the unique maximizer and that any two certificates from valid enclosures name the same token (\code{separation}, \code{certified\_argmax}, \code{certificate\_unique}), which is the decision logic of Theorem~\ref{thm:ladder}; that the filter $hi_i\ge\max_jlo_j$ keeps every maximizer and drops only beaten rows (\code{maximizer\_survives}, \code{dropped\_row\_loses}); that adding a fixed noise value or scaling by a positive temperature preserves an enclosure (\code{shift\_encloses}, \code{scale\_encloses}); the acceptance guards of Theorem~\ref{thm:guards} with an enclosed numerator and their exclusivity (\code{accept\_guard}, \code{reject\_guard}, \code{guards\_exclusive}); the residual-weight enclosure of Theorem~\ref{thm:residualself} (\code{residual\_interval}); the $\ell_\infty$/$\ell_1$ Hölder bound behind the Hölder form of transport (Appendix~\ref{app:transport-geometry}) and the Hölder form of the quantization envelope (\code{holder}); and the sequential case of Lemma~\ref{lem:tree} (\code{sequential\_sum\_bound}). \code{formal/DecisionGuards.lean} holds nine declarations: scaled-integer acceptance and rejection guards and their stability under refinement, associativity of chronological composition, deterministic replay of records from equal starts (the logical skeleton of Proposition~\ref{prop:gdn}), and guards that stop a stale epoch or prefix from publishing state. A deliberately false variant of \code{sequential\_sum\_bound} is rejected, so the check is not vacuous.

Outside the checker are IEEE rounding and the bridge from floating-point values to these integers; the tree, pairwise, blocked and fused forms of the accumulation bound; Cauchy--Schwarz; real exponentials and the transported mass bounds; the Gumbel-max law and every probability statement; the tensor-core adder model; and compiler lowering, race freedom on the GPU and the engine's state restoration.

\paragraph{Tested against exact arithmetic} An exact floating-point reference (\code{src/precision\_reference.py}) emulates every BF16, FP32 and FP64 rounding on rationals and checks each bound and decision of Sections~\ref{sec:selfevidence} and~\ref{sec:decisions} against real arithmetic~\ev{precision}. Its 21 test methods run 40,015 generated checks: 5,600 accumulation-bound checks over eight summation models (sequential, pairwise, blocked, split-$K$, round toward zero, flush to zero and the fused multi-term adder), where the largest observed error was 17--83\% of the bound depending on the model; 1,767 approximate-pass rows and 2,767 integer-pass rows enclosed; 420 certified argmax cases, 72 of them exact ties, of which 183 ended at the approximate pass, 191 after FP32 re-scoring, 24 in FP64 and 22 in exact arithmetic; 360 races under a fixed noise field (62 with a forced tie at the top), 200 top-$k$ sets, 300 acceptance decisions (148 with the uniform placed next to the threshold), 300 residual races and 300 verifications in SGLang's sibling form; 900 emulations of the stock head's rounding on adversarial near-tie heads with $D\le8$; 160 heads with subnormal weights and inputs; and the witnesses cited in the text for R-bf16 (Section~\ref{sec:headcontracts}), for BF16 split-$K$ partials and for a noise stream consumed in evaluation order. The heads are synthetic with small $V$ and $D$, and the fused adder is a model, not hardware.

A second exact reference, with bounded integer heads and base-2 masses represented as rationals (so that no floating-point exponential is trusted), tests the decision and race algorithms~\ev{inherited}. Its 18 decision and 3 race test methods pass on the instances of Table~\ref{tab:tests}. The race tests use fixed positive rational priorities, so they establish pathwise equivalence of bounded and dense algorithms, not properties of continuous Gumbel noise. The exact witnesses that separate the contracts and those for the recurrent state are in Appendices~\ref{app:contracts} and~\ref{app:minimal-state}.
