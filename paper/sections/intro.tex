\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}\label{sec:question}
This paper asks how little of the output head's work a greedy verifier needs if it must return exactly the engine's token, and what ``exactly'' has to mean inside a production engine. In speculative decoding a cheap drafter proposes several tokens and the target model checks them all in one forward pass~\cite{specdecode,chen2023specsampling}. At each checked \emph{position} the target multiplies its final hidden state, the \emph{head input}, by the head, giving a score (\emph{logit}) for every vocabulary entry. Plain (non-speculative) greedy decoding emits the entry with the largest logit, and a greedy verifier accepts a drafted token if and only if it is that entry.

The speed at stake is modest. Qwen3.5-4B's head has 248,320 entries; in BF16 it is 15\% of the weights a plain decode step reads (derived from the checkpoint; Appendix~\ref{app:bytes}). On a GH200 at batch 1 the head takes 10.3\% of such a step \ev{profile-head}, so even a free head would speed it up by only about 11\%. With the model's one-layer multi-token-prediction (MTP) drafter, MTP-4B, the target's verification head takes 4.4\% of a profiled draft-and-verify cycle at batch 1 \ev{profile-attr}, and the drafter's own projections through the same head a further 12.9\% (Figure~\ref{fig:breakdown}). A drafter's head need not be exact, but a shortcut that returns the engine's token leaves the drafts, and so the acceptance rate, unchanged.

Most of the head's computation goes to entries that cannot win, and bounds can skip it (Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}): approximate every score cheaply, here from an int8 copy of the head, which reads half the bytes; bound each error, giving every entry an interval containing its exact score; recompute exactly the few entries whose intervals do not lie wholly below another's; and fall back to the full computation when bounds cannot single out the winner. A \emph{certified head} built this way pays when the intervals are narrow compared with the gap between the two best scores, as on this model (Section~\ref{sec:candidates}). Certified greedy screening of an LM head has public implementations~\cite{sparkpipehead,dgpphead,lagunaprune,knlphead}, and exact sampling from bounded scores is older~\cite{astar,mussmann2017}; Section~\ref{sec:related} compares them.

The hard part is deciding what the cheap computation must agree with: the engine does not compute exact scores. It sums each logit in FP32, in an order its kernel chooses, rounds the sum to BF16, and returns the first index among equal maxima. BF16 keeps eight significant bits, so logits between 16 and 32 are 0.125 apart: in a hypothetical case, logits of 20.02 and 20.05 both round to 20.0, and the engine returns whichever entry has the lower index, even if its exact score is the smaller. On this model's captured greedy positions, the engine's two largest logits tie in BF16 at 1.0\% of them, and the exact-arithmetic winner differs from the engine's token at about 0.5\% \ev{alignment}.

We therefore take the engine's own head as the reference: a certified head must return the token the unmodified (\emph{stock}) head returns for the same head input and \emph{batch shape}, the number of positions in one head call. Its correctness is then testable by bit-equality against the stock engine in the same configuration. Our proof that intervals around the stock kernel's FP32 sums, before rounding, fix that token (Section~\ref{sec:contract}) needs a bound on the kernel's rounding error, which no vendor documents, so we assume one under two named \emph{error models} and test it. Positions the intervals cannot decide run the stock head, so a failed certificate costs time, not agreement; a certified token can be wrong only if an assumption of Table~\ref{tab:assumptions} fails.

A second source of bounds would reuse the drafter's work. MTP-4B and DFlash-4B (the public block drafter for this model) project through the target's head, computing every logit at their own head input, and per tile of 64 entries could record the largest score and, for sampling, the sum of exponentiated scores at little extra cost. A Cauchy--Schwarz bound carries these tile summaries from the drafter's head input to the target's; we call this \emph{transport}. Its bounds are sound, but narrower than the int8 copy's only if the \emph{drift}, the distance between draft and target head inputs relative to the target's norm, is below about 1\% (Section~\ref{sec:crossover}). The median drift is 92\% for DFlash-4B and 95\% for MTP-4B.

\subsection{Contributions}\label{sec:contributions}
What is new is certifying a closed-source production kernel's token: a public Metal implementation recomputes candidates with a replica of its engine's kernel~\cite{lagunaprune}, but cuBLAS cannot be replicated, so we bound its error with a model.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=.3ex]
\item \emph{A contract against the engine's own head} (Section~\ref{sec:contract}): proved conditions for certifying the stock kernel's token at the same batch shape, under an error model assumed and tested, not proved, with the stock head as fallback. Exact arithmetic is the wrong reference (Section~\ref{sec:setup}).
\item \emph{An error bound for tensor-core accumulation, and an extension to seeded sampling} (Section~\ref{sec:head}): the int8 copy's error bound covers truncating tensor-core adders that keep at least 23 fractional bits, and the same decision certifies the token of SGLang's seeded sampler.
\item \emph{What the certified head buys} (Section~\ref{sec:measure}): recomputation, fallback and token agreement on the engine's own states; served in three sessions against tuned stock configurations, the head gains 1.2--4.6\% at one request, short of its head-only prediction, and the fastest configuration at each of 2 to 8 requests loses 1.0--3.0\%.
\item \emph{A negative result} (Section~\ref{sec:transport}): for both public drafters and every bound variant and tiling tested, certified transport skips under 0.7\% of the vocabulary.
\item \emph{Engine changes that gain more} (Section~\ref{sec:levers}): changes to attention, recurrent state and admission whose outputs first diverge from stock only at near ties gain more than the certified head; two declared approximations miss their quality budget.
\end{enumerate}
