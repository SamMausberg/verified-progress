\section{Introduction}\label{sec:question}
At every position a speculative decoder verifies, the target model projects its hidden state through the output head and produces a score for each token of the vocabulary. A greedy verifier then uses one fact from that tensor, the index of its maximum. A sampled verifier whose noise is fixed in advance uses one more: the maximum of a perturbed score. A tensor that is convenient to produce is not necessarily the right interface, and whether the rest of the projection is worth computing depends on what it costs. For Qwen3.5-4B the tied $248{,}320\times2{,}560$ head is 1.27~GB in BF16. It is 15\% of the weight bytes a plain decode step streams and 84\% of those of a multi-token-prediction (MTP) draft step, which runs one small layer and then the full head (derived from the checkpoint, Table~\ref{tab:bytes}).

The known way to do less computes cheap scores for every row, bounds their error, and re-scores in full precision only the rows that could still win. Public implementations already do this for greedy language-model heads~\cite{sparkpipehead,dgpphead,lagunaprune,knlphead}, and sampling exactly from bounded scores is older~\cite{astar,mussmann2017}. What they leave open is what ``exact'' should mean inside a production engine. SGLang's stock head is a cuBLAS BF16 GEMM that accumulates in FP32, chooses its algorithm by batch shape and rounds its output to BF16; on this model the engine's two best logits are equal in BF16 at 1.0\% of greedy positions, where the first-index rule of \code{torch.argmax} decides, and the real-arithmetic winner differs from the engine's token at about 0.5\% of positions (Section~\ref{sec:transport-capture}). A certificate of the real-arithmetic winner is therefore a certificate of a computation the engine does not perform, and an error bound that treats FP32 accumulation as exact, or models it as IEEE additions when the GPU uses tensor cores, does not enclose the values the engine produces.

This paper states and tests the contract a certified head can meet in such an engine, and the conditions it rests on. The reference is the token the stock head kernel returns for the same hidden state at the same batch shape. A row is certified only when interval enclosures of that kernel's BF16 outputs, under a stated model of its FP32 accumulation error, determine the token its argmax and tie rule return; every other row runs the stock kernel, so a failed certificate costs time and never agreement (Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}). The evidence for the certificate comes from a low-precision copy of the head, which reads about half the bytes of the BF16 head.

The earlier revisions of this manuscript were built on a different source of evidence. A drafter that projects through the same head can summarize each vocabulary tile, and a Hölder bound can \emph{transport} those summaries to the target's hidden state, so that the verifier reuses work already paid for. The idea fails on real states, and the reason is specific. Transport can be tighter than an int8 envelope only if draft and target head inputs agree to about one part in a hundred. The public DFlash drafter's head inputs differ from the target's by a median 92\% in relative $\ell_2$ distance, because its final norm fixes their length at a third of the target's and their direction differs as well; the native MTP layer's differ by 95\%, mostly in direction. Their certificates therefore skip almost nothing (Section~\ref{sec:transport}). Because the head is one term of a step's cost, the paper also measures the rest of the serving stack of this hybrid model: the recurrent state, drafting, the levers that trade bytes for quality, and the latency--throughput frontier.

\subsection{Contributions}\label{sec:contributions}
The contributions, at the strength the evidence currently supports, are these.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=.5ex]
\item \emph{A stock-kernel contract for certified heads} (Section~\ref{sec:semantics}). A gap condition (Theorem~\ref{thm:stock}) and its sharper interval form, which rounds each accumulator enclosure into BF16 buckets and applies the first-index rule (Proposition~\ref{prop:stockinterval}), state when a certified token must equal the stock token; the fallback is the stock kernel itself. These are proved. That the stock kernel's accumulation error stays within the modelled bound at the shapes served is an assumption, tested rather than proved.
\item \emph{An envelope that is sound for Hopper accumulation} (Section~\ref{sec:selfevidence}). The envelope of the low-precision pass covers tensor-core accumulation under the published model of Khattak and Mikaitis~\cite{khattak2026}, which is not a vendor contract, or needs no accumulation model when the pass is an integer product. One refinement procedure decides greedy selection and a race under a fixed noise field, with bounds on how many rows it re-scores.
\item \emph{Candidate sets and fallback shares on captured engine states} (Sections~\ref{sec:geometry-selfevidence} and~\ref{sec:rstock-share}). On 6,005 plain-decode head inputs captured from the engine, an int8 head certifies the real-arithmetic winner with 1.3--1.6 candidate rows per decision on average. Under the interval form, the stock kernel is needed for 0.35\% of rows (2.8\% of the capture run's batches of up to 16 requests) if that kernel follows the Khattak--Mikaitis model, and for 1.40\% of rows (10.9\% of those batches) under a more conservative model; no certified token differed from the engine's. At a serving batch of 128, a whole-batch fallback would be paid in roughly 36--84\% of batches if rows fell back independently, so the runtime case depends on a row-wise fallback whose invariance assumption is \pend{kernel-invariance}.
\item \emph{Certified sampling under a keyed noise field, and a scoped verification contract} (Section~\ref{sec:decisions}). The same refinement returns exactly the token of a dense Gumbel race with the same counter-keyed field. Fixed-noise verification~\cite{daliri2025} with the engine's own seeded noise makes sampled verification an argmax with no partition function; its committed tokens are the stock seeded sampler's tokens for the verify pass's logits, not those of plain seeded decoding. The contract is proved and adopted for the certified path; its measurement is \pend{kernel-seeded}.
\item \emph{The measured failure of transport} (Section~\ref{sec:transport}). From the weights alone, transport's envelope is narrower than a per-row int8 envelope only if the relative drift between draft and target head inputs is below a threshold whose median over rows is 0.85\% (Euclidean envelopes, 64-row tiles). On 40,000 aligned DFlash-4B pairs the drift has a median of 91.7\%, and on 16,016 MTP-4B pairs 95\%. For every bound family and tiling tested, certified transport skips at most 0.59\% (DFlash) and 0.65\% (MTP) of the vocabulary on average, no more than a static screen; the verify-batch union needs at least 99.4\% of the head; and sampled acceptance stays undecided with probability 0.92--0.96 (DFlash) and at least 0.997 (MTP). On the DFlash verify rows a greedy verifier needs, int8 self-evidence needs 1.57 candidate rows.
\item \emph{Where the stock engine disagrees with itself} (Section~\ref{sec:divergence}). A bit-exact tap locates the first differing module output between configurations that should agree: layer 0's GDN recurrence for plain decoding against MTP speculation, where decode and verify use different kernels, and batch-dependent kernels (a gated RMSNorm, FlashInfer decode attention, a batched prefill GEMM) for batch 1 against 32. The tie rule and the head GEMM cause none of the divergences; whether a divergence is a rounding or an order flip depends on the stock kernel's accumulation model, and under the conservative model it cannot be decided for most. That the caches entering those kernels agree is \pend{state-mech}.
\item \emph{Other negative results with mechanisms.} A certified decoder tail failed its optimistic kill test (Section~\ref{sec:tail}); sharing exact computation across unrelated requests fails in analysis (Section~\ref{sec:stack-state-need}); FP8 weights gave no consistent gain and an FP8 KV cache none in first single runs (Section~\ref{sec:stack-levers}); and on constructed heads a cheap acceptance decision did not make verification cheap (Appendix~\ref{app:synthetic}).
\item \emph{The rest of the stack} (Sections~\ref{sec:stack}--\ref{sec:serving}). A byte-and-arithmetic model of the step with stated execution and rate assumptions, first single-run lever measurements, the public DFlash drafter's acceptance by block position on this GPU, and a serving protocol for two frontiers, one exact and one lossy under a declared quality budget. Attribution, repeated lever runs and the frontiers are \pendtext{}.
\end{enumerate}
Three further proposals are stated as hypotheses with pre-registered kill tests and no result yet: exact repair of long draft windows (P2, P3; Section~\ref{sec:repair}), writing the recurrent state less often (P4; Section~\ref{sec:stack-state-need}), and a draft selector trained for rate (P6; Section~\ref{sec:drafter-selector}). Section~\ref{sec:related} places the work against prior art; the claim that remains after that comparison is narrow, and Section~\ref{sec:prior-novelty} states it.
