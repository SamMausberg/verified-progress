\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}\label{sec:question}
At every position a speculative decoder verifies, the target model projects its hidden state through the output head and scores every token of the vocabulary, although a greedy verifier uses only the index of the maximum, and a sampled verifier with noise fixed in advance only the maximum of a perturbed score. For Qwen3.5-4B the tied head has 248,320 rows of width 2,560, 1.27~GB in BF16: 15\% of the weight bytes of a plain decode step and 84\% of those of a multi-token-prediction (MTP) draft step (derived from the checkpoint, Table~\ref{tab:bytes}). Measured on a GH200, the head's GEMM, FP32 copy and argmax take 10.3\% of a plain decode step at batch 1, 6.1\% at batch 128, and 17.2\% of an MTP speculative cycle at batch 1 \ev{profile-head}, so even a free head would speed up a plain step at batch 1 by at most 11\%. The question here is narrower than speed: how much of the projection a verifier needs, and against what it must be exact.

The known way to do less computes cheap scores for every row, bounds their error and re-scores only the rows that could still win; public implementations do this for greedy heads~\cite{sparkpipehead,dgpphead,lagunaprune,knlphead}, and exact sampling from bounded scores is older~\cite{astar,mussmann2017}. What they leave open is what ``exact'' means inside a production engine. SGLang's head is a cuBLAS BF16 GEMM that accumulates in FP32, chooses its kernel by batch shape and rounds to BF16, followed by an argmax that returns the first maximal index. On this model the engine's two largest logits tie in BF16 at 1.0\% of greedy positions, and the real-arithmetic winner differs from the engine's token at about 0.5\% \ev{alignment}. Certifying the real-arithmetic winner certifies a computation the engine does not perform, and a bound that treats FP32 accumulation as exact, or as IEEE additions, does not enclose what tensor cores produce.

We state and test the contract a certified head can meet in such an engine: the token the stock head kernel returns for the same hidden state at the same batch shape. A row is certified only when enclosures of that kernel's FP32 accumulator, under a named error model, round into BF16 values that fix its argmax and tie rule; every other row runs the stock kernel, so a failed certificate costs time and never agreement (Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}). The enclosures come from an int8 copy of the head, which reads half its bytes.

We also test \emph{transport}, the mechanism this work set out to build: a drafter that projects through the same head has computed a maximum and a mass for every vocabulary tile, and a Hölder bound can carry them to the target's state, so the verifier would reuse work already paid for. From the weights alone, transport beats an int8 envelope only if draft and target head inputs agree to about one part in a hundred. On real states they differ by a median 92\% for the public DFlash drafter, whose final norm fixes their length at a third of the target's, and 95\% for the model's own MTP layer, and the certificates skip almost nothing.

\subsection{Contributions}\label{sec:contributions}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=.3ex]
\item \emph{A contract for certified heads against the stock kernel} (Section~\ref{sec:contract}): a gap condition and a sharper interval form, both proved, state when a certified token equals the stock token at the same batch shape, with the stock kernel as the fallback. That the kernel's accumulation error stays within the named model is an assumption, tested rather than proved; Table~\ref{tab:assumptions} lists it with every other numerical assumption.
\item \emph{A certified head that meets it} (Section~\ref{sec:head}), with an envelope sound for tensor-core accumulation under Khattak and Mikaitis's published model of Hopper~\cite{khattak2026}, or model-free for an integer pass, and one refinement for greedy selection, seeded sampling and fixed-noise verification (proved; measurement \pend{kernel-seeded}). The greedy form is prior art; our claim is narrower (Section~\ref{sec:kernel-design}).
\item \emph{Measurements on the engine's own states} (Section~\ref{sec:rstock-share}): on 6,005 captured plain-decode head inputs, an int8 copy re-scores 1.3--1.6 candidate rows per decision on average and leaves 0.35\% of rows to the stock kernel under the Hopper model and 1.40\% under a conservative one, and no certified token differed from the engine's. The runtime is \pend{kernel-path}; at serving batch sizes it rests on a row-wise fallback.
\item \emph{A negative result with its mechanism} (Section~\ref{sec:transport}): on both public drafters for this model, the drift between draft and target head inputs is two orders of magnitude above the threshold the weights set, and certified transport skips at most 0.65\% of the vocabulary, no more than a static screen.
\end{enumerate}
