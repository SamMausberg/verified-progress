\section{Evaluation Setup}\label{sec:setup}
\paragraph{System} One NVIDIA GH200 (96~GB HBM3, compute capability 9.0, 64-core Grace CPU) with PyTorch 2.13 for CUDA 13.0, Triton 3.7.1 and FlashInfer 0.6.18; SGLang~\cite{sglangpaper} at commit \sourcepin{} with the FlashInfer attention backend. The target is Qwen3.5-4B at revision \modelpin~\cite{qwencard}: 32 layers, 24 of them Gated DeltaNet linear attention~\cite{gdn} and 8 full attention, one native MTP layer, and the tied head of Section~\ref{sec:stock}. The drafters are the MTP layer (four draft steps, top-$k$ 1) and the public DFlash block drafter for this target, \code{z-lab/Qwen3.5-4B-DFlash} at revision \code{9a1996cc}~\cite{dflashpaper,dflash4bcard}, which drafts blocks of 16 tokens and projects them through the target's head.

\subsection{Capture and Replay}\label{sec:transport-capture}
A capture-only engine patch stores the head input as BF16 bit patterns and joins it, within the same step, with the tokens the engine chose; under speculation it also stores the draft head inputs, the proposals and the accepted lengths, and pairs each drafted token with the target head input that verifies it (labelled accepted, rejected or unreached). The hooks run in Python between forwards, so the capture servers run without CUDA graphs or the overlap scheduler, with at most 16 running requests (plain decoding), 8 (MTP) or 5 (DFlash). The prompts are 320 public prompts in four domains (MT-Bench, No Robots, HumanEval, MBPP, GSM8K, MATH-500, m-ArenaHard and MGSM, 40 each), a third in thinking mode, decoded greedily for 384 tokens and split by prompt into analysis and held-out halves before any fitting; every result is on held-out prompts.

\paragraph{The capture is what the head read} In plain decoding the FP64 argmax of the captured head input equals the engine's token at 99.51\% of 105,269 positions, and all 519 disagreements lie within one BF16 spacing of the FP64 winner (largest gap 0.118 logits, spacing 0.125). The engine's top two logits tie in BF16 at 1.0\% of positions, and the real-arithmetic winner and the engine's token differ at about 0.5\% \ev{alignment}.

\paragraph{What is compared with what} Candidate counts are for the \Rreal{} decision on 6,005 held-out plain-decode rows from 160 prompts and 750 decode steps (8.0 held-out rows per step), with real logits in FP64; fallback shares apply the \Rstock{} rules to the same rows, and agreement compares each certified token with the engine's token at the capture's batch shape. Drift uses 40,000 DFlash-4B and 16,016 MTP-4B held-out pairs, and transport certification 4,020 DFlash-4B pairs from 110 prompts and the same MTP-4B pairs. No served throughput or latency is reported.
