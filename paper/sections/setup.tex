\section{Measurements on the Engine's States}\label{sec:measure}\label{sec:setup}
Numbers labelled baseline are the stock engine's; all others are the certified head's, replayed offline unless marked as a microbenchmark.

\subsection{Setup}\label{sec:transport-capture}
\paragraph{System} One NVIDIA GH200 with SGLang~\cite{sglangpaper} at commit \sourcepin{}, PyTorch 2.13 for CUDA 13.0 and the FlashInfer attention backend (Appendix~\ref{app:pins} lists every pin). The target is Qwen3.5-4B at revision \modelpin~\cite{qwencard}; 24 of its 32 layers are Gated DeltaNet (GDN) linear attention~\cite{gdn}, and it has one native MTP layer. The drafters are the MTP layer (four draft steps, top-$k$ 1) and the public DFlash block drafter for this target, \code{z-lab/Qwen3.5-4B-DFlash} at revision \code{9a1996cc}~\cite{dflashpaper,dflash4bcard}, which drafts blocks of 16 tokens and projects them through the target's head.

\paragraph{Capture and replay} A capture-only engine patch records each position's head input as BF16 bit patterns, together with the token the engine chose in the same step. Under speculation it also records the drafter's head inputs and pairs each drafted token with the target head input that verifies it. The results below \emph{replay} these records offline: real logits are recomputed in FP64 and the certified head's rules applied to them. The hooks run in Python between forwards, so the capture servers run without CUDA graphs or the overlap scheduler, with at most 16 running requests in plain decoding (Appendix~\ref{app:transport-capture}). The 320 public prompts come from eight datasets in four domains and are decoded greedily for 384 tokens. They are split by prompt into analysis and held-out halves before anything was fitted, and every result is on \emph{held-out} prompts. The capture records what the head read: in plain decoding the FP64 argmax of the captured head input equals the engine's token at 99.51\% of positions, and every disagreement lies within one BF16 spacing of the FP64 winner \ev{alignment}.

\paragraph{What is compared} Candidate counts are for the \Rreal{} decision on 6,005 held-out plain-decoding positions from 750 decode steps (8.0 held-out positions per step). Fallback shares apply the \Rstock{} rules to the same positions, and agreement compares each certified token with the engine's token at the capture's batch shape, so both come from batches of at most 16 positions.

\subsection{How Many Entries Are Recomputed}\label{sec:geometry-selfevidence}\label{sec:candidates}
No error bound was violated on these positions, and the exact winner was always a candidate \ev{selfevidence,selfevidence-csv}. An int8 copy with one scale per row of $W$ needs 1.55 candidates per decision on average and decides 75.4\% of positions without recomputing any entry. With one scale per 128 columns the mean falls to 1.34, at 0.52 of the BF16 head's bytes (Figure~\ref{fig:candidates}a; Table~\ref{tab:candidates} gives the percentiles). FP8 E4M3 needs 4.9 candidates on average, and int4 decides no position at all. Int8 suffices because of the margins, not because its bound is tight (Section~\ref{sec:envelope}): the bound is about one logit wide, while the median gap between the two largest logits is 7.3 logits \ev{stats}. Tightening the bound therefore helps little (Appendix~\ref{app:candidates}), and seeded races behave like greedy selection. On the verification positions a greedy verifier needs, the per-row copy needs 1.57 candidates for DFlash-4B and 1.51 for MTP-4B \ev{selfevidence-csv,selfevidence-dflash,selfevidence-mtp}.

\subsection{Fallback and Agreement}\label{sec:rstock-share}
Table~\ref{tab:rstock} in Appendix~\ref{app:candidates} gives how often the contract leaves a position to the stock kernel. The gap condition has a floor near 2\%; the interval form has none. Under the \modelhopper{} the interval form leaves 0.35\% of the held-out positions to the stock kernel, and under the \modelcons{} 1.40\%. For every model in that table, no position the interval form certified had a token different from the one the engine returned at the capture's batch shape \ev{rstock}. That is consistent with the error models on these positions, not a proof that cuBLAS stays within the smallest of them. The GPU kernel itself, replayed at each engine step's own shape on the first 60,000 capture positions (all splits), leaves 0.28\% and 1.46\% of them to the stock kernel under the two models, and with the fallback every token equals the engine's \ev{kernel-replay}.

Fallback grows with the batch, because one undecided position is enough to send a whole batch to the stock head. If positions fell back independently with probability $p$, a call of $M$ positions would contain an undecided one with probability $1-(1-p)^M$. At the capture's 8.0 positions per step this matches the measured share of steps with a fallback, 2.8\% under the \modelhopper{} and 10.9\% under the \modelcons. At $M=128$ it predicts 36\% and 84\%, and the kernel measures 27\% and 77\% on calls of 128 consecutive capture positions (Figure~\ref{fig:candidates}b). A whole-batch fallback would therefore run the stock head for most large batches, so at serving batch sizes the certified head depends on the per-position fallback.

\subsection{Head-Only Runtime}\label{sec:kernel-runtime}
The certified path pays off only if
\begin{equation}
T_{\rm pass}+T_{\rm select}+T_{\rm rescore}+P_{\rm fb}(M)\,T_{\rm stock}(M)<T_{\rm chain}(M),\label{eq:headgate}
\end{equation}
where $T_{\rm chain}$ is the stock GEMM with its FP32 copy and argmax and $P_{\rm fb}(M)$ the share of calls that fall back. As a baseline, the stock GEMM at $M=1$ runs at 95\% of the measured read bandwidth \ev{profile-head,profile-bw}, so a pass that reads half the bytes cannot take less than about 170~\textmu s (derived).

In a microbenchmark of the head alone (CUDA graphs, warm L2 cache, real decode positions, runtime checks on), the certified path takes 239.6~\textmu s on a decided batch at $M=1$, against 370.5~\textmu s for the stock head and argmax. With the per-position fallback's expected cost under the \modelcons{} it takes 0.67--0.70 of the stock time up to 16 positions and 0.93 at 64 \ev{kernel-path,kernel-rowwise}. Every mode is slower from 128 positions (Figure~\ref{fig:candidates}c), for two reasons. Most calls need a fallback there (77\% under the \modelcons), and the int8 pass alone is slower than the stock chain. The pass is not bound by memory bandwidth: at one position its directed-rounding epilogue keeps the streaming multiprocessors 79\% busy while it draws 62--63\% of DRAM bandwidth, so its time grows with $M$ faster than the stock GEMM's \ev{kernel-micro}. These are head-only times; the served effect is \pend{bench-cert}.
