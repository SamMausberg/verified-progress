\section{Measurements on the Engine's States}\label{sec:measure}\label{sec:setup}
This section measures how many entries the certified head recomputes, how often it falls back, whether its tokens agree with the engine's, and how long it takes. Numbers labelled baseline are the stock engine's; all others are the certified head's, replayed offline unless marked as a microbenchmark.

\subsection{Setup}\label{sec:transport-capture}
\paragraph{System} One NVIDIA GH200 (96~GB HBM3, compute capability 9.0, 64-core Grace CPU) with PyTorch 2.13 for CUDA 13.0, Triton 3.7.1 and FlashInfer 0.6.18; SGLang~\cite{sglangpaper} at commit \sourcepin{} with the FlashInfer attention backend. The target is Qwen3.5-4B at revision \modelpin~\cite{qwencard}: 32 layers, 24 of them Gated DeltaNet (GDN) linear attention~\cite{gdn} and 8 full attention, one native MTP layer, and the tied head of Section~\ref{sec:stock}. The drafters are the MTP layer (four draft steps, top-$k$ 1) and the public DFlash block drafter for this target, \code{z-lab/Qwen3.5-4B-DFlash} at revision \code{9a1996cc}~\cite{dflashpaper,dflash4bcard}, which drafts blocks of 16 tokens and projects them through the target's head.

\paragraph{Capture and replay} A capture-only engine patch records each position's head input as BF16 bit patterns, together with the token the engine chose in the same step. Under speculation it also records the drafter's head inputs, its proposals and the accepted lengths, and pairs each drafted token with the target head input that verifies it. The results below \emph{replay} these records offline: real logits are recomputed in FP64 and the certified head's rules applied to them. The hooks run in Python between forwards, so the capture servers run without CUDA graphs or the overlap scheduler, with at most 16 running requests (plain decoding), 8 (MTP) or 5 (DFlash). The prompts are 320 public prompts from eight datasets in four domains, a third in thinking mode (Appendix~\ref{app:transport-capture}), decoded greedily for 384 tokens and split by prompt into analysis and held-out halves before anything was fitted; every result is on \emph{held-out} prompts.

\paragraph{The capture is what the head read} In plain decoding the FP64 argmax of the captured head input equals the engine's token at 99.51\% of 105,269 positions, and all 519 disagreements lie within one BF16 spacing of the FP64 winner \ev{alignment}.

\paragraph{What is compared} Candidate counts are for the \Rreal{} decision on 6,005 held-out plain-decoding positions from 160 prompts and 750 decode steps (8.0 held-out positions per step). Fallback shares apply the \Rstock{} rules to the same positions, and agreement compares each certified token with the engine's token at the capture's batch shape, so both come from batches of at most 16 positions.

\subsection{How Many Entries Are Recomputed}\label{sec:geometry-selfevidence}\label{sec:candidates}
No error bound was violated on these positions, and the exact winner was always a candidate \ev{selfevidence,selfevidence-csv}. An int8 copy with one scale per row of $W$ needs 1.55 candidates per decision on average (median 1, p99 8) and decides 75.4\% of positions without recomputing any entry; with one scale per 128 columns the mean is 1.34, at 0.52 of the BF16 head's bytes (Figure~\ref{fig:candidates}a; Table~\ref{tab:candidates} in Appendix~\ref{app:candidates}). FP8 E4M3 needs 4.9 candidates on average and 60 at p99, and int4 with 128-column groups leaves a mean of 72,847 candidates and decides no position. Int8 suffices because of the margins, not because its bound is tight (Section~\ref{sec:envelope}): the bound is about one logit wide, while the median gap between the two largest logits is 7.3 logits (p10 0.68) \ev{stats}, so tightening the bound helps little (Appendix~\ref{app:candidates}). Seeded races at $T=1$ and $0.7$ behave like greedy selection (mean 1.33 and p99 5 for the 128-column copy). Taking for each entry the smallest of the rigorous bounds tested, the per-row copy needs 1.54 candidates on plain decoding, and 1.57 (DFlash-4B, 1,162 of 4,000 positions) and 1.51 (MTP-4B, 2,901 of 4,010) on the verification positions a greedy verifier needs \ev{selfevidence-csv,selfevidence-dflash,selfevidence-mtp}.

\subsection{Fallback and Agreement}\label{sec:rstock-share}
Table~\ref{tab:rstock} in Appendix~\ref{app:candidates} gives how often the contract leaves a position to the stock kernel. The gap condition has a floor near 2\%; the interval form has none. Under the \modelhopper{} the interval form leaves 0.35\% of the held-out positions to the stock kernel, and 2.8\% of the capture's decode steps contain such a position; under the \modelcons, 1.40\% and 10.9\%. For every model in that table, no position the interval form certified had a token different from the one the engine returned at the capture's batch shape \ev{rstock}. That is consistent with the error models on these positions, not a proof that cuBLAS stays within the smallest of them. The GPU kernel, replayed at each engine step's own shape on the first 60,000 capture positions (all splits), leaves 0.28\% and 1.46\% of them to the stock kernel under the two models, and with the fallback every token equals the engine's \ev{kernel-replay}.

Fallback grows with the batch, because one undecided position is enough to send a whole batch to the stock head. If positions fell back independently with probability $p$, a call of $M$ positions would contain an undecided one with probability $1-(1-p)^M$. At the capture's mean of 8.0 positions per step this gives 2.8\% and 10.7\%, close to the measured 2.8\% and 10.9\%; at $M=128$ it gives 36\% and 84\% (Figure~\ref{fig:candidates}b). On calls of 128 consecutive capture positions the kernel's measured shares are 27\% and 77\%, below the 30\% and 85\% that independent positions at its own rates would give. Either way a whole-batch fallback would run the stock head for most large batches, so at serving batch sizes the certified head depends on the per-position fallback.

\subsection{Head-Only Runtime}\label{sec:kernel-runtime}
The certified path pays off only if
\begin{equation}
T_{\rm pass}+T_{\rm select}+T_{\rm rescore}+P_{\rm fb}(M)\,T_{\rm stock}(M)<T_{\rm chain}(M),\label{eq:headgate}
\end{equation}
where $T_{\rm chain}$ is the stock GEMM with its FP32 copy and argmax and $P_{\rm fb}(M)$ the share of calls that fall back. The baseline at $M=1$: the stock GEMM takes 354.5~\textmu s, 95\% of the 3.79~TB/s read bandwidth measured over the head's 1.27~GB, and the chain 369.0~\textmu s \ev{profile-head,profile-bw}, so a pass that reads half the bytes has a floor near 170~\textmu s (derived).

In a microbenchmark of the head alone (CUDA graphs, warm L2 cache, real decode positions, runtime checks on), the certified path takes 239.6~\textmu s on a decided batch at $M=1$, against 370.5~\textmu s for the stock head and argmax. With the per-position fallback's expected cost under the \modelcons{} it takes 0.67--0.70 of the stock time up to 16 positions, 0.81 at 32 and 0.93 at 64; every mode is slower from 128 (Figure~\ref{fig:candidates}c) \ev{kernel-path,kernel-rowwise,kernel-micro}. There the int8 pass alone is slower than the stock chain, and 77\% of calls need a fallback under the \modelcons. The pass is not bound by memory bandwidth alone: at one position its directed-rounding epilogue keeps the streaming multiprocessors 79\% busy while it draws 62--63\% of DRAM bandwidth, so its time grows with $M$ faster than the stock GEMM's \ev{kernel-micro}. These are head-only times; the served effect is \pend{bench-cert}.
