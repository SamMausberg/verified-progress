\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{The error model is an assumption} Agreement with \Rstock{} rests on a model of a closed-source kernel's accumulation, which no vendor documents and which does not cover BF16 split-$K$ partials. It was tested on replayed rows (Section~\ref{sec:rstock-share}), not proved; the stock FP32-output kernel's largest observed error is 58 times inside the \modelhopper, but the BF16-output kernel the engine runs hides its accumulator \ev{kernel-error}; the whole-batch fallback needs no model.

\paragraph{Replay, not serving} Candidate sets and fallback shares come from offline replay of states captured at batches of up to 16. On speculative verify rows, including those after a rejection, only the gap condition was evaluated: it leaves 5.7\% (\modelhopper) to 8.6\% (\modelcons) of DFlash-4B rows and 2.8\% to 4.0\% of MTP-4B rows to the stock kernel, against 2.2\% and 3.0\% on plain decoding \ev{selfevidence-dflash,selfevidence-mtp}, so verification may fall back more often. The main open question is whether the complete certified head beats the stock chain once it pays for screening, compaction, re-scoring and fallback (Equation~\eqref{eq:headgate}); low candidate counts do not settle it. Measured alone it does up to 64 rows with the row-wise fallback and loses from 128, where most batches need a fallback (Section~\ref{sec:kernel-runtime}, Figure~\ref{fig:candidates}); the served frontiers decide \pend{bench-cert}.

\paragraph{Where speculation pays} On the serving workload's confirmation split, with every arm tuned on a disjoint split, speculation leads plain decoding through client concurrency 32 and trails it from 48 in every drafter family (Figure~\ref{fig:frontier}) \ev{bench-frontier}. At concurrency 1, DFlash with 16-token blocks gives 3.06 times the throughput and 3.45 times the per-request rate of its matched plain baseline, both with Triton attention; the best speculative arm, MTP, gives 0.98 times plain at 48 and 0.86 times at 128. Over the 52 points with three or four sessions, the coefficient of variation of throughput has a median of 0.35\% and a maximum of 1.9\%. These runs fix the baseline a certified verifier must improve \pend{bench-cert}.

\paragraph{Scope} Transport was refuted for two drafters and the bound families and tilings of Table~\ref{tab:transport}, in a replay that favours it; a drafter trained to reproduce the target's head input is untested. The measurements concern one model, engine commit and GPU, with text-only prompts. The contract and proofs carry over to any head that rounds an FP32 accumulation to BF16 and breaks ties by index, but the error model and fallback shares must be re-established per kernel and GPU. Secondary investigations (a certified decoder tail, the rest of the serving stack, drafting and repair) are in the research notes in the repository.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
A verifier that must agree with a production engine needs less than a full projection of the output head but more than the real-arithmetic winner: the token the engine's own kernel, with its rounding and tie rule, returns at the same batch shape. On this engine's states an int8 copy of the head supplies that with one or two rows re-scored per decision and the stock kernel as a model-free fallback; transported draft summaries do not, because draft head inputs are far from the target's.
