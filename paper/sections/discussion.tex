\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}\label{sec:limits}
For a greedy or seeded decision the verifier needs an enclosure of every row fine enough to fix the stock kernel's argmax, rounding and tie rule included, and nothing more. On the states this engine produced an int8 copy of the head supplies that almost everywhere; transport, which would have reused the drafter's work, does not, because draft and target head inputs are far apart. These results fix what a verifier needs, not yet that providing it is faster.

\paragraph{The error model is an assumption} Agreement with \Rstock{} rests on a model of a closed-source kernel's accumulation, Khattak and Mikaitis's model of Hopper tensor cores or a conservative bound. Neither is a vendor guarantee or covers BF16 split-$K$ partials, and the BF16-output kernel does not expose its accumulator. What was checked is weaker and consistent: on 6,005 replayed rows no certified token differed from the engine's under any model, including two far tighter sensitivity models. The kernel's measured error at the served shapes is \pend{kernel-error}; the whole-batch fallback needs no model.

\paragraph{Replay, not serving} Candidate sets and fallback shares come from offline replay of states captured at batches of up to 16 without CUDA graphs. The fallback shares are for plain decoding. On DFlash-4B verify rows only the gap condition was evaluated, over all verify rows including those after a rejection, and it leaves 5.7\% (\modelhopper) to 8.6\% (\modelcons) of rows to the stock kernel against 2.2\% and 3.0\% on plain decoding \ev{selfevidence-dflash}, so verification may fall back more often; MTP states are \pend{geometry-drift}. At serving batch sizes the runtime case depends on the row-wise fallback \pend{kernel-invariance,kernel-rowwise}, and the runtime, the seeded sampler's token agreement and the served frontiers are \pend{kernel-path,kernel-seeded,bench-cert}. Even a free head would bound the gain at $1.11\times$ for a plain step at batch 1, and less at batch 128, where the recurrent-state kernel of this hybrid model takes 41.6\% of a plain step \ev{profile-attr}.

\paragraph{Scope} Transport was refuted for two drafters, the bound families and tilings of Table~\ref{tab:transport}, and a replay that favours transport by using exact draft logits; a drafter trained to reproduce the target's head input is untested. All measurements concern one model, engine commit and GPU, with text-only prompts. The contract and proofs carry over to any engine whose head rounds an FP32 accumulation to BF16 and breaks ties by index, but the error model and fallback shares must be re-established per kernel and GPU. The exact CPU references test the certificates on small synthetic heads, and the Lean files cover decision logic and scaled-integer bounds, not IEEE rounding or probability (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}). Other investigations, including a certified decoder tail that failed its kill test (Appendix~\ref{sec:tail}), the serving stack (Appendix~\ref{app:stack}) and drafting (Appendix~\ref{app:drafting}), are in the appendices.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
A verifier that must agree with a production engine needs less than a full projection of the output head but more than the real-arithmetic winner: it needs the token the engine's own kernel, with its rounding and tie rule, returns at the same batch shape. An int8 copy of the head meets that requirement on this engine's states with one or two rows re-scored per decision and the stock kernel as a fallback whose frequency was measured and whose correctness needs no model. Reusing the drafter's work through transported summaries fails on both drafters tested, because their head inputs are not close to the target's; the usable evidence comes from a smaller copy of the verifier's own head. Whether it saves time in a served engine is what the pending runtime measurements will decide.
