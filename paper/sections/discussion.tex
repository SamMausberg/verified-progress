\section{Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{The error model is an assumption} Agreement with \Rstock{} rests on an assumed model of a closed-source kernel's accumulation; neither of the two models covers BF16 split-$K$ partial sums (Section~\ref{sec:headcontracts}). Crafted-input probes on this GPU find that the stock head kernels accumulate as the \modelhopper{} assumes, with more fractional bits than the \modelcons{} needs (Section~\ref{sec:headcontracts}), but the probes used products with one exact operand and reached neither subnormals nor other GPUs, so neither model is established for all inputs. The replayed positions cannot tell the two models apart (Section~\ref{sec:rstock-share}). The engine's BF16-output kernel hides its accumulator, so the error measured on its FP32-output variant (Section~\ref{sec:headcontracts}) is indirect evidence \ev{kernel-error}. The int8 kernel, too, is checked per compiled variant, not proved to compute the modelled arithmetic (Section~\ref{sec:kernel-design}).

\paragraph{Replay, not serving} The recomputation and fallback figures come from offline replay of plain decoding, at the capture's batches of up to 16 positions (Figure~\ref{fig:candidates}b also groups consecutive positions into larger calls), and the engine check from 64 prompts under the \modelcons{} only. Each engine change of Table~\ref{tab:levers} was timed in a single session, the composed DFlash changes in three. Two results are not yet measured: the certified head's own effect on served decoding \pend{bench-cert} and the acceptance of fixed-noise verification against the stock rejection-sampling verifier \pend{kernel-seeded-engine}.

\paragraph{Scope} Transport was refuted for two drafters and the bound families and tilings of Table~\ref{tab:transport}, in a replay that favours it by treating draft logits as exact. A drafter trained to reproduce the target's head input is untested. The measurements concern one model, engine commit and GPU, with text-only prompts. The contract and proofs carry over to any head that rounds an FP32 accumulation to BF16 and breaks ties by index; the error model and the fallback shares must be re-established for each kernel and GPU.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
A verifier does not need every logit, but the token it needs is the engine's own, rounding and tie rule included, not the exact-arithmetic winner. On this engine an int8 copy of the head finds that token with one or two exact recomputations per decision, assuming a named bound on the engine kernel's error and falling back to the engine's head when the bounds cannot decide. Bounds carried over from the public drafters' heads cannot do the same in any variant tested, because their head inputs are far from the target's. Whether the certified head shortens served decoding is not yet measured.
