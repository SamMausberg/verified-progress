\section{Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{The error model is an assumption} Agreement with \Rstock{} rests on a model of a closed-source kernel's accumulation that does not cover BF16 split-$K$ partial sums (Section~\ref{sec:headcontracts}). Agreement on replayed positions cannot discriminate between the models (Section~\ref{sec:rstock-share}). The stock FP32-output kernel's largest observed error is 58 times inside the \modelhopper, but the BF16-output kernel the engine runs hides its accumulator \ev{kernel-error}. The int8 kernel, too, is checked per compiled variant, not proved to compute the modelled arithmetic (Section~\ref{sec:kernel-design}).

\paragraph{Replay, not serving} The recomputation and fallback figures come from offline replay of plain decoding at batches of up to 16 positions, and the engine check from 64 prompts under the \modelcons{} only. Two results are not yet measured: the effect of the certified head on served decoding \pend{bench-cert}, and the acceptance of fixed-noise verification against the stock rejection-sampling verifier \pend{kernel-seeded-engine}.

\paragraph{Scope} Transport was refuted for two drafters and the bound families and tilings of Table~\ref{tab:transport}, in a replay that favours it by treating draft logits as exact; a drafter trained to reproduce the target's head input is untested. Seeded agreement covers sampling without top-$k$ or top-$p$ truncation only. The measurements concern one model, engine commit and GPU, with text-only prompts. The contract and proofs carry over to any head that rounds an FP32 accumulation to BF16 and breaks ties by index; the error model and the fallback shares must be re-established for each kernel and GPU.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
A verifier that must agree with a production engine needs less than a full projection of the output head but more than the real-arithmetic winner: the token the engine's own head returns at the same batch shape. An int8 copy of the head supplies that token with one or two entries recomputed per decision, under a named error model and with the stock head as the fallback; bounds carried over from the public drafters' heads do not, because their head inputs are far from the target's. Whether the certified head shortens served decoding is not yet measured.
