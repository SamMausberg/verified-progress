\section{Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{The error model is an assumption} Agreement with \Rstock{} rests on a model of a closed-source kernel's accumulation that does not cover BF16 split-$K$ partial sums (Section~\ref{sec:headcontracts}). Agreement on replayed positions cannot discriminate between the models (Section~\ref{sec:rstock-share}). The stock FP32-output kernel's largest observed error is 1/58 of the \modelhopper's bound, but the BF16-output kernel the engine runs hides its accumulator \ev{kernel-error}. The int8 kernel, too, is checked per compiled variant, not proved to compute the modelled arithmetic (Section~\ref{sec:kernel-design}).

\paragraph{Replay, not serving} The recomputation and fallback figures come from offline replay of plain decoding, at the capture's batches of up to 16 positions (Figure~\ref{fig:candidates}b also groups consecutive positions into larger calls), and the engine check from 64 prompts under the \modelcons{} only. Each exact engine change of Table~\ref{tab:levers} was timed in a single session. Three results are not yet measured: the effect of the certified head on served decoding \pend{bench-cert}, the engine changes composed \pend{stack-composition}, and the acceptance of fixed-noise verification against the stock rejection-sampling verifier \pend{kernel-seeded-engine}.

\paragraph{Scope} Transport was refuted for two drafters and the bound families and tilings of Table~\ref{tab:transport}, in a replay that favours it by treating draft logits as exact; a drafter trained to reproduce the target's head input is untested. The measurements concern one model, engine commit and GPU, with text-only prompts. The contract and proofs carry over to any head that rounds an FP32 accumulation to BF16 and breaks ties by index; the error model and the fallback shares must be re-established for each kernel and GPU.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
A verifier does not need every logit, but the token it needs is the engine's own, rounding and tie rule included, not the exact-arithmetic winner. On this engine an int8 copy of the head finds that token with one or two exact recomputations per decision, assuming a named bound on the engine kernel's error and falling back to the engine's head when the bounds cannot decide. Bounds carried over from the public drafters' heads cannot do the same, because their head inputs are far from the target's. Whether the certified head shortens served decoding is not yet measured.
