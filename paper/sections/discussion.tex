\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}\label{sec:limits}
A greedy or seeded verifier needs an enclosure of every row fine enough to fix the stock kernel's argmax, rounding and tie rule included, and nothing more. An int8 copy of the head supplies that on this engine's states; transport does not, because draft and target head inputs are far apart. What remains open is whether supplying it is faster.

\paragraph{The error model is an assumption} Agreement with \Rstock{} rests on a model of a closed-source kernel's accumulation, which no vendor documents and which does not cover BF16 split-$K$ partials; the BF16-output kernel does not expose its accumulator. What was checked is weaker: on 6,005 replayed rows no certified token differed from the engine's under any model, including two far tighter sensitivity models. The kernel's measured error is \pend{kernel-error}; the whole-batch fallback needs no model.

\paragraph{Replay, not serving} Candidate sets and fallback shares come from offline replay of states captured at batches of up to 16, and the fallback shares are for plain decoding. On DFlash-4B verify rows, including those after a rejection, only the gap condition was evaluated: it leaves 5.7\% (\modelhopper) to 8.6\% (\modelcons) of rows to the stock kernel, against 2.2\% and 3.0\% on plain decoding \ev{selfevidence-dflash}, so verification may fall back more often. At serving batch sizes the case rests on the row-wise fallback \pend{kernel-invariance,kernel-rowwise}; the runtime and the served frontiers are \pend{kernel-path,bench-cert}. Even a free head bounds the gain at $1.11\times$ for a plain step at batch 1, and less at batch 128, where the recurrent-state kernel takes 41.6\% of the step \ev{profile-attr}.

\paragraph{Scope} Transport was refuted for two drafters and the bound families and tilings of Table~\ref{tab:transport}, in a replay that favours it; a drafter trained to reproduce the target's head input is untested. The measurements concern one model, engine commit and GPU, with text-only prompts. The contract and proofs carry over to any head that rounds an FP32 accumulation to BF16 and breaks ties by index, but the error model and fallback shares must be re-established per kernel and GPU; Lean covers the decision logic and scaled-integer bounds, not IEEE rounding (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}). A certified decoder tail that failed its kill test (Appendix~\ref{sec:tail}), the serving stack (Appendix~\ref{app:stack}) and drafting (Appendix~\ref{app:drafting}) are in the appendices.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
A verifier that must agree with a production engine needs less than a full projection of the output head but more than the real-arithmetic winner: the token the engine's own kernel, with its rounding and tie rule, returns at the same batch shape. On this engine's states an int8 copy of the head supplies that with one or two rows re-scored per decision and the stock kernel as a model-free fallback; transported draft summaries do not, because draft head inputs are far from the target's. Whether the certified head saves time in a served engine awaits the runtime measurements.
