\section{Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{The error model is an assumption} Agreement with \Rstock{} rests on a model of a closed-source kernel's accumulation that does not cover BF16 split-$K$ partial sums (Section~\ref{sec:headcontracts}). It was tested on replayed positions (Section~\ref{sec:rstock-share}), not proved. The stock FP32-output kernel's largest observed error is 58 times inside the \modelhopper, but the BF16-output kernel the engine runs hides its accumulator \ev{kernel-error}. The whole-batch fallback needs no model. The int8 kernel, too, is checked per compiled variant rather than proved to compute the modelled arithmetic (Section~\ref{sec:kernel-design}).

\paragraph{Replay, not serving} The candidate counts and fallback shares of Section~\ref{sec:measure} come from offline replay of plain decoding at batches of up to 16 positions. The engine check adds verification and drafting, but on 64 prompts, under the \modelcons{} only and without timing; for DFlash-4B verification it shows the whole-batch fallback in most head calls (Section~\ref{sec:rstock-share}). The runtime is the head's alone (Section~\ref{sec:kernel-runtime}); the effect on decoding as a whole is not yet measured \pend{bench-cert}.

\paragraph{The serving baseline} The stock engine's tuned serving configurations set the bar a certified verifier must clear. Speculation serves more tokens per second than plain decoding up to client concurrency 32 and fewer from 48, for every drafter; at concurrency 1, DFlash with 16-token blocks gives 3.06 times the throughput of its matched plain baseline, both with Triton attention (Figure~\ref{fig:frontier}) \ev{bench-frontier}. None of these configurations uses the certified head.

\paragraph{Scope} Transport was refuted for two drafters and the bound families and tilings of Table~\ref{tab:transport}, in a replay that favours it by treating draft logits as exact; a drafter trained to reproduce the target's head input is untested. Seeded agreement covers sampling without top-$k$ or top-$p$ truncation only. The measurements concern one model, engine commit and GPU, with text-only prompts. The contract and proofs carry over to any head that rounds an FP32 accumulation to BF16 and breaks ties by index; the error model and the fallback shares must be re-established for each kernel and GPU. Secondary investigations are in the research notes in the repository.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
A verifier that must agree with a production engine needs less than a full projection of the output head, but more than the real-arithmetic winner: it needs the token the engine's own kernel returns at the same batch shape, rounding and tie rule included. On this engine's states an int8 copy of the head supplies that token with one or two entries recomputed per decision, under a named error model and with the stock kernel as the fallback, in about 0.7 of the stock head's time up to 16 positions when the head is timed alone. For the two public drafters, bounds transported from the drafter's head computation do not, because their head inputs are far from the target's. Whether the certified head shortens decoding in a served engine is not yet measured.
