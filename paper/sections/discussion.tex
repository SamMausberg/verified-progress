\section{Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{The error model is an assumption} Agreement with \Rstock{} rests on an assumed model of a closed-source kernel's accumulation; neither of the two models covers BF16 split-$K$ partial sums (Section~\ref{sec:headcontracts}). The crafted-input probes behind the \modelhopper{} (Section~\ref{sec:headcontracts}) used products with one exact operand and reached neither subnormals nor other GPUs, so neither model is established for all inputs. The replayed positions cannot tell the two models apart (Section~\ref{sec:rstock-share}). The engine's BF16-output kernel hides its accumulator, so the error measured on its FP32-output variant (Section~\ref{sec:headcontracts}) is indirect evidence \ev{kernel-error}. The int8 kernel, too, is checked per compiled variant, not proved to compute the modelled arithmetic (Section~\ref{sec:kernel-design}).

\paragraph{Replay and serving} The recomputation and fallback figures come from offline replay of plain decoding, at the capture's batches of up to 16 positions (Figure~\ref{fig:candidates}b also groups consecutive positions into larger calls), and the engine check from 64 prompts under the \modelcons{} only. The served runs used one greedy workload, and the fallbacks' time inside the served graphs was not measured, so why the served gains fall short of the head-only prediction is a hypothesis. Each engine change of Table~\ref{tab:levers} that ran alone was timed in a single session, the certified head and the composed DFlash changes in three. The acceptance rate of fixed-noise verification against the stock rejection-sampling verifier was not measured.

\paragraph{Scope} Transport was refuted for two drafters and the bound families and tilings of Table~\ref{tab:transport}, in a replay that favours it by treating draft logits as exact. A drafter trained to reproduce the target's head input is untested. The measurements concern one model, engine commit and GPU, with text-only prompts. The contract and proofs carry over to any head that rounds an FP32 accumulation to BF16 and breaks ties by index; the error model and the fallback shares must be re-established for each kernel and GPU.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
A verifier does not need every logit, but the token it needs is the engine's own, rounding and tie rule included, not the exact-arithmetic winner. On this engine an int8 copy of the head finds it with one or two exact recomputations per decision, under a named bound on the kernel's error and with the engine's head as the fallback; served, this certified head gains 1.2--4.6\% throughput at one request with identical tokens and loses at larger batches, where fallbacks become common. Bounds carried over from the public drafters' heads cannot do the same in any variant tested, because their head inputs are far from the target's. Holding a fast path to the engine's own output, with the engine's kernel as fallback, makes it testable by bit-equality and makes a failed certificate cost time, not correctness, and nothing in that design is specific to the head. On this hybrid model, though, the head is a small target: it takes 10\% of a batch-1 plain decode step, against 42\% for the recurrent-state kernel at batch 128 (Appendix~\ref{app:profile}).
